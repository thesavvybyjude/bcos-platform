# Leadership_Principles.md

## Purpose
To provide leadership frameworks for scaling high-growth companies.

## Core Philosophy
**Leadership is about enabling others, not controlling them.**

- Hire A players
- Trust them
- Give them direction
- Get out of their way

## Principles

**1. Lead by Example**
- Work hard
- Be transparent
- Own mistakes
- Stay passionate

**2. Trust Your Team**
- Hire well
- Trust completely
- Provide resources
- Get out of way

**3. Be Accountable**
- Set clear goals
- Track progress
- Own failures
- Share wins

**4. Work Ethic Is a Non-Negotiable Advantage**
- "I would challenge anyone to outwork me"
- Psychotically competitive
- But not transactional
- Relationships and work ethic combined

**5. Be Even-Keeled**
- Don't get too high or too low
- Neutral energy is sustainable
- Paranoia is healthy
- Protection mode is normal

**6. Play the Long Game Always**
- Everything compounds
- Relationships compound
- Experience compounds
- Reputation compounds

**7. Anger Management Is Essential**
- Emotion loses deals
- Caps lock emails are career-limiting
- Stay even-keeled
- Be a thermostat, not a thermometer

**8. Your Job Is to Enable, Not Control**
- Hire people better than you
- Give them the environment to thrive
- Get out of their way
- Lead by example

**9. If You're Not All In, You'll Lose**
- If you're not working Thanksgiving, someone else is
- All in = Competitive advantage
- Half in = You'll lose
- Commitment is everything

**10. Faith Is a Superpower**
- Believing something bigger is helping you
- Faith sustains through hard times
- Trust the process
- Stay positive even when nothing works

**11. Resilience Is a Choice**
- You can let it sink you or rise above
- It's always a choice
- The worst times create the strongest people
- Adversity is preparation

**12. Timing Matters**
```
Cancer diagnosis (August 2016)
  |
  |- Wedding (October 2016)
  |- Surgery (October 16, 2016)
  |- Insurance payout (November 2016)
  |- First store opened (December 6, 2016)

The worst timing became the best timing
```

## ADDITION: Decision Delegation and Team Diversity

**Core Philosophy Addendum 4:**
**The CEO's job is to decide who decides.**

### Additional Principles:

**16. The CEO's Job: Who Decides?**
- Early days: You decide everything
- As you grow: You decide less
- Your job is to nominate the decision maker
- Delegate, don't abdicate

**17. Nominate, Don't Dominate**
- When a decision comes up: "This is your call"
- Empower the right person
- Use your mandate to back them
- "Christine said this, we're doing it"

**18. Diversity Is Hard But Valuable**
- Different people don't always get along
- Diversity of thought is valuable
- You may need to help them break bread
- The tension can be productive

**19. The IQ 110 Advantage**
- 150 IQ people overthink
- 110 IQ people delegate to 150 IQ people
- Get out of your own way
- Hire people smarter than you

### Updated Rules

**Always:**
- Nominate decision makers
- Empower your team
- Hire people smarter than you
- Foster productive tension

**Never:**
- Make all decisions yourself
- Underutilize your team's expertise
- Hire less intelligent people
- Let diversity become dysfunction

## ADDITION: Founder Flow and Diva Mindset

**Core Philosophy Addendum:**
**Your job is to create an environment where your genius can shine.**

### Additional Principles:

**20. Founder Flow**
- 4 hours daily of deep work
- Only you can do this work
- Intersection of great at, love, and impact
- 5:30 AM wake up
- Gym, smoothie, lock in 7-11 AM

**21. Be a Diva**
- You're the founder, not an employee
- Want what you want the way you want it
- Have printed briefs, perfect lighting

- Your job: show up and perform

**23. The 6-Week Soul Trip**
- Every 6 weeks, unplug
- Go somewhere inspiring
- Read, plan, hike
- Review decade, year, 3-month goals
- Come back with fresh perspective

### ADDITION: Pete Carroll Leadership Lessons

**Core Philosophy Addendum:**
**How you show up matters. Be predictable.**

### Additional Principles:

**24. Show Up the Same Way Every Time**
- Predictability allows people to perform
- When people know what to expect, they can focus
- Pete Carroll was never a yeller
- Consistent energy creates consistency

**25. Repeat the Core Messages**
- Pete said the same things every game
- "Can't win in the first, second, third quarter—can you win in the fourth?"
- Repetition becomes belief
- Belief becomes reality

**26. Do It Better Than It's Ever Been Done Before**
- This came from Pete Carroll
- It's meaningful to the team
- They can recite the core values
- Repetition matters

**27. Choose Purpose Over Winning at All Costs**
- Pete did ride-alongs in South Central
- He believed he could change lives
- You can choose to do good or do nothing
- Choose impact

**28. It's Not About Bill vs. Pete (Winning vs. Impact)**
- Bill won more games
- Pete made more impact
- Do you want to be known as the guy who won or the guy who impacted?
- Choose what matters

**29. Listening Is a Superpower**
- Most negotiations break down over lack of listening
- Hearing ≠ Listening ≠ Comprehension
- Demonstrate your comprehension
- Great leaders are listeners, not talkers

**30. Genuineness Wins**
- Deception by omission is a time bomb
- Be genuine about your limitations
- Honesty builds trust
- Trust enables collaboration

## ADDITION: Humility and Servant Leadership

**Core Philosophy Addendum:**
**Humility is not weakness; it is strength.**

### Additional Principles:

**31. The Strength of Humility**
- People want to follow leaders who do not think they are better than everyone else
- Humility makes you approachable
- Approachable leaders get better information
- Better information leads to better decisions

**32. The Danger of Arrogance**
- Arrogance is the enemy of learning
- Arrogant leaders stop listening
- They stop learning
- They make bad decisions

**33. Humility in Practice**
- Admit when you are wrong
- Give credit to others
- Do not take yourself too seriously
- You are not the smartest person in the room

**34. The Compound Effect of Humility**
- Humility compounds over time
- People trust the humble
- Trust is the currency of leadership
- The more you lead with humility, the more powerful you become
