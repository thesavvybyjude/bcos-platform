# Humility

## Purpose
To provide a framework for humility in leadership.

## Core Philosophy
**Humility is not weakness; it is strength.**

- The most powerful leaders are the humblest
- Humility attracts talent
- Humility enables learning
- Humility is the enemy of arrogance

## Principles

### 1. The Strength of Humility
- People want to follow leaders who do not think they are better than everyone else
- Humility makes you approachable
- Approachable leaders get better information
- Better information leads to better decisions

### 2. The Danger of Arrogance
- Arrogance is the enemy of learning
- Arrogant leaders stop listening
- They stop learning
- They make bad decisions

### 3. Humility in Practice
- Admit when you are wrong
- Give credit to others
- Do not take yourself too seriously
- You are not the smartest person in the room

### 4. The Compound Effect of Humility
- Humility compounds over time
- People trust the humble
- Trust is the currency of leadership
- The more you lead with humility, the more powerful you become

## Decision Framework

```
Am I Leading with Humility?
  |
  +-- Do I admit when I am wrong?
  |   +-- YES -> Good
  |   +-- NO -> Need to work on it
  |
  +-- Do I give credit to others?
  |   +-- YES -> Good
  |   +-- NO -> Need to share
  |
  +-- Do I take myself too seriously?
  |   +-- YES -> Need to lighten up
  |   +-- NO -> Good
  |
  +-- Am I learning from others?
      +-- YES -> Good
      +-- NO -> Need to listen
```

## Rules

### Always:
- Admit mistakes
- Give credit to others
- Do not take yourself too seriously
- Listen

### Never:
- Think you are the smartest person in the room
- Take credit for others' work
- Refuse to admit mistakes
- Let arrogance creep in
