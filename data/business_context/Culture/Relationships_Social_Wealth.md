# Relationships & Social Wealth

## Purpose
To provide frameworks for building and maintaining deep, meaningful relationships and understanding the role of social connections in a wealthy life.

## Core Philosophy

> "Relationships are the single greatest predictor of living a good life."

> "Investing in your relationships is the one tried and true way to build the happy, healthy life that we really want."

## Principles

### 1. Relationships are the Foundation of Civilization
- Margaret Mead: First sign of civilized society is a healed broken femur
- Someone was cared for when they couldn't contribute physically

### 2. Relationships Predict Health
- Harvard Study of Adult Development (100 years)
- Single greatest predictor of physical health at age 80 = relationship satisfaction at age 50

### 3. Shared Struggle Builds Deep Bonds
- Summer friends: There when it's sunny and warm
- Winter friends: There when it's cold and hard
- Deepest relationships are winter friends

### 4. Ambivalent Relationships are Most Damaging
- Sometimes supportive, sometimes demeaning
- Worse than purely toxic relationships
- We put up guards against toxic people, but let ambivalent people in

### 5. Earned Status vs. Bought Status
- Bought status: Things you can purchase (Rolex, car, club membership)
- Earned status: Things you can only earn (business, health, relationships)
- A six-pack is a better status flex than a Rolex

### 6. Falling in Love vs. Growing in Love
- Falling in love: Easy, what you see on social media
- Growing in love: Hard, what you don't see
- Deep relationships are built through shared struggle

### Depth, Breadth, Earned Status Framework

| Pillar | Definition | Action |
|--------|------------|--------|
| **Depth** | Close, intimate relationships | Invest time, show up, have hard conversations |
| **Breadth** | Community, groups, networks | Find third places, value-aligned communities |
| **Earned Status** | Respect earned through meaningful contribution | Build something, earn it |

## Rules

- **Always check in on people** — A text saying "How are you?" can have an enormous ripple effect
- **Always ask "Helped, Heard, or Hugged?"** — Show up the way they need
- **Never avoid hard conversations** — You're taking on debt that must be repaid with interest
- **Never let ambivalent relationships drain you** — They're more damaging than purely toxic ones
- **Never chase bought status** — It's an infinite game you can't win
- **Never fall for the "right now" trap on social invitations** — Would you do it tonight?

## Heuristics

- Winter friends > Summer friends
- Growing in love > Falling in love
- Earned status > Bought status
- A healed femur is civilization; a healed relationship is humanity

## Triggers

### When a Friend is Struggling:

```
Friend comes to you with a problem

→ Ask: "Do you want to be helped, heard, or hugged?"

HELPED: Advice, solutions, "Let me fix it"
HEARD: Just listen, allow venting, no fixing
HUGGED: Comforting touch, presence, support

Default to helping (fixer mentality) is a trap.
Show up the way they need, not the way you want to.
```

### When Invited to Something:

```
Invitation for something 2 months from now

→ Ask: "Would I do this tonight?"

If YES → Say yes
If NO → Say no (you won't want to do it in 2 months either)

Reason: We're terrible at forecasting future time availability.
```

### When Considering a Purchase for Status:

```
About to buy something

→ Ask: "Would I buy this if I couldn't tell a single person I had it?"

If NO → It's bought status. Don't buy it.
If YES → It's intrinsically valuable. Consider it.
```

## Templates

### Helped, Heard, or Hugged Protocol

```
When someone comes to you with a problem:

1. Before responding, ask: "Do you want to be helped, heard, or hugged?"

2. Then respond accordingly:
   - HELPED: "Okay, let me help. Here's what I think..."
   - HEARD: "Okay, I'm going to listen. Tell me everything."
   - HUGGED: "I'm here for you."

3. Use this a few times with each person to create a pattern.
   Eventually, you won't need to ask—you'll know.
```

### Right Now Test

```
When deciding on any future commitment:

1. Ask: "Would I want to do this tonight?"

2. If YES → Accept (you'll want to do it later too)
3. If NO → Decline (you won't want to do it later either)
```
