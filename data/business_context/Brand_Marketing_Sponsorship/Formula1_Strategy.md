# Formula1_Strategy.md

## Purpose
To provide frameworks for using F1 sponsorship for brand positioning.

## Core Philosophy
**F1 is a statement without having to say anything.**

- Being in F1 signals premium quality
- F1 is global (24 races, 150+ countries)
- Cost-effective for global reach
- Brand association is immediate

## Principles

**1. F1 Is a Global Platform**
- 24 races in 18+ countries
- 150+ countries broadcast
- Massive reach
- Premium association

**2. Cost Is Surprisingly Low**
```
Annual F1 sponsorship: $2-3M
  |
  |- Divide by 50 markets = $40-60K/market
  |- Divide by 24 races = $1.7-2.5K/race/market
  |- Extremely cost-effective for global brand
```

**3. The Content Flywheel**
- F1 races generate content
- Netflix Drive to Survive features sponsors
- F1 video game includes sponsors
- Year-round exposure

## Decision Framework

```
Should I Sponsor F1?
  |
  |- Do I operate globally?
  |   |- YES → F1 makes sense
  |   |- NO → Regional might be better
  |
  |- Do I want premium positioning?
  |   |- YES → F1 is perfect
  |   |- NO → Other options
  |
  |- Do I have $2-3M budget?
  |   |- YES → Consider
  |   |- NO → Too expensive
  |
  |- Is my brand a fit?
      |- YES → Proceed
      |- NO → Find other platform
```

## Rules

**Always:**
- Leverage global reach
- Use F1 for premium positioning
- Create content from sponsorship
- Leverage the halo effect

**Never:**
- Sponsor F1 if not global
- Underestimate the statement it makes
- Ignore content opportunities
- Think it's "too expensive" without doing the math
