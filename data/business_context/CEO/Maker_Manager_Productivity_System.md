# Maker Manager Productivity System

## Purpose
To provide a comprehensive productivity system that distinguishes between Maker (deep work) and Manager (coordination) time, allowing entrepreneurs to maximise returns on their time investment.

## Core Philosophy

> "Productivity is how much money you get out for the time you put in."

## Principles

### 1. Time Is the Investment
- Time is the investment; money is the return
- The best investors of time make the most money
- Measure productivity by output, not calendar fullness

### 2. Two Types of Workers

```
MANAGER SCHEDULE
├── Time Chunks: Small (15-60 minutes), 20+ per day
├── Maximally Productive: Fully booked calendar
├── Work: Meetings, coordination, data, reporting, persuasion, training
├── Day Structure: Clear start/end based on meeting schedule
└── Cost of Interruption: Low (one of many chunks)

MAKER SCHEDULE
├── Time Chunks: Large (4-6 hours), 2-3 per day
├── Maximally Productive: Empty calendar
├── Work: Building, creating, writing, coding, deep work, strategy
├── Day Structure: Open-ended, variable end times
└── Cost of Interruption: High (entire block destroyed)
```

### 3. The Conflict Problem
- Managers see empty calendar → assume not working → schedule meetings
- One meeting costs a Maker 10% of their weekly output
- Makers accept to avoid offense → destroy productivity → work longer hours
- **Result:** Vicious cycle; business stays small

### 4. The $400 Chili's Analogy
- 1-hour meeting with 10 people at $50/hour = $500 cost
- That's like taking the team to Chili's for $400
- Multiple times daily = major expense
- **Rule:** Treat meetings like the expensive investments they are

## Decision Frameworks

### The Three-Pronged Solution

```
FOR MANAGERS:
├── 1. Understand the cost you put on Makers (coordination + meeting = 2 disruptions)
├── 2. A "Maker's No" is not personal — it's them keeping a larger commitment
├── 3. Ask your team: "What's your ideal day/week?" and follow it
└── 4. Don't take offense at a "Maker's No"

FOR MAKERS:
├── 1. Communicate how you work — send this content to your team
├── 2. When meetings unavoidable → switch to Manager mode and stack them
├── 3. Set standard meeting times (e.g., Monday/Thursday afternoons)
├── 4. Be responsive when you say you'll be responsive
└── 5. Actually work during Maker blocks — prove the empty calendar is productive

FOR ORGANIZATIONS:
├── 1. Mandate quiet time/days (e.g., Wednesdays)
├── 2. Spread Maker/Manager language across the team
├── 3. Audit meetings quarterly — delete, delegate, systemize
└── 4. Measure by output, not calendar fullness
```

### Phased Implementation

```
V1 (SMALL BUSINESS):
├── Maker time outside business hours (nights/weekends/early mornings)
├── This is where you build the systems that will run your business
└── You work the job you have AND work the job you want

V2 (INTERMEDIATE):
├── 50/50 split — first half Maker, second half Manager
├── Plan calendar back-to-front to protect mornings
├── Back-to-front: meetings from end of day backwards
└── Maker blocks protected in the morning

V3 (ADVANCED):
├── Entire Maker days (2-4 per week)
├── Entire Manager days (stacked meetings)
├── Clear separation between building and coordinating
└── Maximum deep work time
```

### Back-to-Front Calendar Planning

```
HOW TO PLAN:
├── Start with the end of your day
├── Stack meetings from 4pm backwards
├── Protect morning blocks for Maker time
├── Leave buffers between meetings
└── Never schedule meetings in your best Maker hours

RULE:
└── Your calendar should reflect your priorities, not other people's.
```

### Meeting Audit Process

```
QUARTERLY AUDIT:
├── List all recurring meetings
├── For each meeting, ask:
│   ├── Is this still necessary?
│   ├── Can this be an email/message?
│   ├── Can this be delegated?
│   └── Can this be systemized?
├── Delete unnecessary meetings
├── Delegate what can be delegated
├── Systemize what can be systemized
└── Keep only what requires your Maker/Manager presence

RULE:
└── Delete meetings first, then delegate, then systemize.
```

### Maker/Manager Communication Protocol

```
COMMUNICATE YOUR HAT:
├── "I'm putting my Maker hat on today"
├── "I'm putting my Manager hat on today"
└── Sets expectations with one phrase

IDEAL DAY CONVERSATIONS:
├── Ask your team: "What does a maximally productive day/week look like for you?"
├── Follow their answer to the greatest degree possible
└── Example: Editing team requested all meetings bunched on certain days → implemented → productivity increased

THE "MAKER'S NO":
├── When a Maker declines a meeting, do not take offense
├── See it as them keeping a larger commitment to you, the company, and the work
└── This is not a "real world no" — it's a "Maker's no"
```

## Rules

- **Always plan back-to-front** — protect morning Maker blocks
- **Always communicate your hat** — "Maker hat on" / "Manager hat on"
- **Always audit meetings quarterly** — delete, delegate, systemize
- **Always ask about ideal days** — follow your team's answer
- **Never take a "Maker's No" personally** — it's them keeping a larger commitment
- **Never schedule meetings in Maker blocks** — unless truly unavoidable
- **Never measure by calendar fullness** — measure by output
- **Never let Managers interrupt Makers** — understand the cost first

## Heuristics

- If your calendar is full, you're in Manager mode — is that intentional?
- If your calendar is empty, you're in Maker mode — protect it
- If one meeting costs 10% of weekly output, it better be worth it
- If you're working nights/weekends, you're in V1 — time to move to V2
- If you can't walk away for a day without emails piling up, you don't have a communication firewall
- If your team doesn't know if you're in Maker or Manager mode, you haven't communicated
- If meetings are multiplying, you haven't audited in too long
