# CEO Operating Systems

The operational framework for how a CEO structures their time, decisions, and communications to maximise leverage and minimize bottlenecks.

## Purpose

This module defines the CEO's personal operating system — the daily, weekly, and quarterly rhythms that keep the business on track. It covers decision architecture, communication protocols, delegation frameworks, and the dashboards that provide real-time visibility into business health.

## Principles

1. **The CEO's Job is to Remove Bottlenecks** — If you are the bottleneck, you are failing at the job. The CEO's primary function is to identify and eliminate constraints to growth — including themselves.

2. **Decision Architecture Over Decision Making** — Design systems that make 80% of decisions automatically. The CEO should only handle decisions that are irreversible, high-stakes, or cross-functional.

3. **Communication Firewall** — Not every message deserves a CEO response. Implement a structured communication hierarchy: Slack for async, email for external, weekly 1:1s for strategic, and office hours for ad hoc.

4. **The CEO Dashboard** — Maintain a single-page dashboard with no more than 12 key metrics. If a metric doesn't drive action, remove it. The dashboard should be reviewed daily and takes < 5 minutes.

5. **Weekly Rhythm is Sacred** — Monday: review dashboard + set priorities. Tuesday-Thursday: deep work + meetings. Friday: retrospective + next-week planning. This rhythm is the heartbeat of execution.

6. **Delegation is Not Abdication** — Delegating a task means transferring execution, not accountability. The CEO must maintain visibility through check-in cadences, not micromanagement.

## Rules

**Always:** Start the week by reviewing your CEO Dashboard before any meetings or emails.

**Always:** Maintain a "not doing" list alongside your "doing" list. What you choose not to do is as important as what you do.

**Never:** Let operational tasks consume more than 20% of your week. If they do, you're doing someone else's job.

**Never:** Skip the weekly leadership meeting. It's the single most important alignment mechanism.

**Avoid:** Reacting to every Slack notification. Batch communication into 3 daily windows.

**Prefer:** Asynchronous communication (Loom, written updates) over synchronous meetings for status updates.

## Decision Frameworks

### CEO Time Allocation

If the task requires your unique expertise or authority:
- Then: Do it yourself, but timebox to 90-minute blocks.
- Otherwise: Delegate immediately with clear success criteria and a review date.

### Meeting Evaluation

If a meeting could be replaced by a written memo or async video:
- Then: Cancel the meeting and replace with an async update.
- Otherwise: If the meeting requires real-time debate or decision → keep it but cap at 30 minutes.
