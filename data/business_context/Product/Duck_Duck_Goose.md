# Duck_Duck_Goose.md

## Purpose
To provide a framework for portfolio review and product pruning.

## Core Philosophy
**Everything should be "duck, duck, duck." If it becomes a "goose," pull it.**

- Review your portfolio quarterly
- Each product should meet your standards
- If a product doesn't fit, cut it
- Hard decisions define your brand

## Principles

### 1. The Duck/Goose Framework
```
Duck, Duck, Duck:
  └─ Every product meets standards

Duck, Duck, Goose:
  └─ One product doesn't fit

Goose → Out
```

### 2. Quarterly Reviews
- Review every product quarterly
- Does it still fit?
- Is the science still strong?
- Is it still "duck, duck, duck"?

### 3. The Fidosia Example
- Launched with early clinical data (mice)
- Science didn't evolve over 2 years
- Became a "goose"
- Cut it, even though it was $6M/year
- Reinforced the brand

### 4. Why Cut a $6M Product?
- Because brand > short-term profit
- Because trust > revenue
- Because standards matter
- Because if you don't, you become like everyone else

## Process

### Quarterly Portfolio Review:
```
1. Review every product
2. Does it meet science standards?
3. Does it meet sourcing standards?
4. Does it meet certification standards?
5. Is it "duck, duck, duck"?
6. If yes → Keep it
7. If no → Cut it
8. Communicate the decision
```

## Decision Framework

```
Should I Keep or Cut This Product?
  │
  ├─ Does it still fit our standards?
  │   ├─ YES → Keep
  │   └─ NO → Cut
  │
  ├─ Has the science evolved (or not)?
  │   ├─ Evolved positively → Keep
  │   └─ Stagnant or negative → Cut
  │
  ├─ Is it a "duck" or a "goose"?
  │   ├─ Duck → Keep
  │   └─ Goose → Cut
  │
  └─ Would a customer trust this product?
      ├─ YES → Keep
      └─ NO → Cut
```

## Rules

**Always:**
- Review portfolio quarterly
- Cut what doesn't fit
- Communicate decisions
- Reinforce standards

**Never:**
- Keep a "goose" in your portfolio
- Compromise standards for revenue
- Ignore science that doesn't evolve
- Make decisions without communication
