# First Mover Disadvantage

## Purpose
To provide a framework for evaluating first-mover strategy.

## Core Philosophy
**Being first is often overrated. Fast followers often win.**

- First movers pay the cost of education
- Fast followers profit from the education
- The market rewards the winner, not the pioneer
- Often the best strategy is to be the fast follower

## Principles

### 1. The Cost of Being First
- First movers pay to educate the market
- They make the mistakes that others learn from
- They burn capital on unproven ideas
- The fast follower enters a validated market

### 2. Fast Followers Win
- Fast followers see what works and copy it better
- They improve on the first mover's mistakes
- They have lower risk and better economics
- Examples: Android following Apple, Facebook following MySpace

### 3. First Mover Advantage Is Overrated
- The market rewards the winner, not the pioneer
- Being first is not a strategy; winning is
- The first mover advantage is often the first mover disadvantage
- The fast follower often wins

## Decision Framework

```
Should I Be First or Follow Fast?
  |
  +-- Do I have the capital to educate the market?
  |   +-- YES -> Consider first mover
  |   +-- NO -> Better to follow
  |
  +-- Is the market validated?
  |   +-- YES -> Follow fast
  |   +-- NO -> Need to educate
  |
  +-- Can I execute better than the first mover?
  |   +-- YES -> Follow fast
  |   +-- NO -> Consider being first
  |
  +-- Is the first mover ahead or vulnerable?
      +-- Vulnerable -> Attack
      +-- Strong -> Avoid
```

## Rules

### Always:
- Consider the cost of being first
- Look for the fast follower opportunity
- Do not be first just to be first
- It is better to win than to be first

### Never:
- Overvalue first mover advantage
- Underestimate the cost of education
- Think being first is the same as winning
- Ignore fast follower opportunities
