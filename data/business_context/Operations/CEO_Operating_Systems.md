# CEO Operating Systems & Leadership

## Purpose
To provide frameworks for transitioning from entrepreneur/founder to professional CEO, building operating rhythms, and leading organizations at scale.

## Core Philosophy

> "There's a fundamental difference between CEOs and entrepreneurs."

> "A great leader needs to understand how much resource—time, money, and people together—it takes to go make a meaningful impact."

## Principles

### 1. The Player-to-Coach Transition
- CEO is a coach, not a doer
- Value is in the business, not building the brand
- Hardest part: staying even-keeled (not too excited, not too down)

### 2. People, Process, Product
- Table stakes: Finance (cash is king)
- People: Right people who believe in the vision
- Process: Operating rhythm, consistency, reps
- Product: Invest in building great products with right go-to-market

### 3. Clarity = Trust
- Defined brand identity = people sign up for it
- Share everything (including failures) in town halls
- Under-promise, over-deliver (especially with PE/boards)

### 4. Constant and Consistent
- Winning championships happens in practice (same thing over and over)
- Same applies to business operating rhythm
- Discipline fuels creativity, doesn't kill it

### 5. The Two People Who See the Whole Business
- CEO and CFO see the entire business all the time
- CFO = money and resources
- CEO = operating efficiency and strategic direction

### 6. Calm Under Pressure
- Any given day: 10 things going well, 10 going poorly
- Must think 3 steps ahead (short-term issues + long-term strategy)
- Stay calm; don't get too excited or too down

## Decision Frameworks

```
ENTREPRENEUR                    CEO
─────────────────────────────────────
Creative                    → Operational
Sales cures all wounds      → Efficiency-focused
Builds from scratch         → Scales what exists
Risk-taker                  → Risk-manager

Transition: Player → Coach
Value shifts from "doing the thing" to "building the system"
```

### Under-Promise, Over-Deliver Framework

```
If I KNOW I can deliver $100 in sales, $10 in profit:

→ Tell them: "We can deliver $100 and $10"
→ Deliver: $105 and $11
→ Result: "Fantastic! Keep going!"

WRONG APPROACH:
→ Tell them: "We can deliver $200"
→ Deliver: $100
→ Result: Daily meetings, micromanagement, trust lost

Rule: Under-promise, over-deliver. Always.
```

## Rules

- **Always set the vision first** — Everything follows from clarity
- **Always know your cash position** — Cash is king; it's table stakes
- **Always have an operating rhythm** — Consistency > sporadic bursts
- **Always under-promise and over-deliver** — Especially with PE/boards
- **Always share both wins and failures** — Transparency builds trust
- **Never chase butterflies** — Shiny objects kill resource allocation
- **Never let emotion drive decisions** — Stay even-keeled; objective, not paranoid

## Heuristics

- If you have the right people and processes, product builds itself
- The team can only execute what they can see clearly
- Discipline fuels creativity; it doesn't kill it
- A leader's job is to remove obstacles, not create them

## Triggers

### When You Feel Overwhelmed as a CEO:

```
10 things going well, 10 things going poorly

→ Check if this is a short-term or long-term issue
→ Short-term: Delegate, trust the team
→ Long-term: Address it yourself

The hardest part is staying even-keeled.
Don't get too high on wins; don't get too low on losses.
```

### When You're Tempted to Chase a New Opportunity:

```
Shiny new idea appears

→ Ask: "What resource (time, money, people) does this require?"
→ Ask: "What am I taking away from to do this?"
→ Ask: "Can I stay committed for the full lifecycle?"

If you're 9 months into a 12-month process and want to switch:
→ You're throwing away all that resource.
→ Don't chase butterflies. Stay committed.
```

### When Hiring for Leadership:

```
New leader candidate

→ Evaluate three things:
1. COMPETENCE: Hall of Fame-caliber for their function
2. CULTURE: Fits your values
3. INSPIRATIONAL: People want to follow them into battle

The third is non-negotiable for leadership.
```

## Templates

### Monthly Business Review Template

```
MBR AGENDA

1. Financial Review (CFO)
   - Sales vs. budget / Margin vs. budget
   - Cash position / Inventory position

2. Functional Updates (All ELT Members)
   - Green box / Red box visuals
   - What worked / What didn't
   - Trends (2+ months of red = fix it)

3. OKR Review
   - Each ELT member reviews OKRs/KPIs
   - Progress against strategic initiatives
   - Course corrections

4. Forward-Looking
   - Next month's priorities
   - Resource allocation changes
```

### One-on-One Meeting Template

```
1-ON-1 WITH DIRECT REPORT

1. How are you doing? (Personal check-in)
2. Deep dive on red boxes from MBR
   - What's happening? People issue? Process issue? External?
   - What do you need from me?
3. Strategic direction
   - What's on your horizon?
   - How can I remove obstacles?
4. Career development
   - How are you growing? What are your goals?
```

## The "Who" Hiring Method (Added by Ken Yoshimura)

### The Scorecard Method

```
THE SCORECARD METHOD:
├── Step 1: Define the role (job description + expectations)
├── Step 2: Create a scorecard (what success looks like)
├── Step 3: Recruit (recruiter finds candidates)
├── Step 4: Homework assignment (test the candidate)
└── Step 5: Multi-gate interview

THE HOMEWORK ASSIGNMENT:
├── Give candidates a real-world project
├── Example: "We're entering 2400 stores. How would you activate it?"
├── See who does it vs. who doesn't
└── "If you don't do the homework, we're not hiring you"

MULTI-GATE INTERVIEW:
├── Gate 1: Recruiter (culture fit, qualifications)
├── Gate 2: Adjacent team members (will they work well together?)
├── Gate 3: Department head (functional expertise)
├── Gate 4: CEO (core values only)
└── Everyone needs to clear their gate

RESULT:
└── No one has left the company in 3 years
└── 98% retention target
└── "Hire slow, fire fast"
```

### Organizational Fluidity Framework

```
THE PROBLEM WITH RIGID ROLES:
└── If you're a digital marketer and don't understand retail,
    you only understand 80% of the business

ORGANIZATIONAL FLUIDITY:
├── How are we additive to each other?
├── Cross-departmental understanding
├── No rigid 4-pillar system
└── Every employee generates >$1M in revenue

IMPLEMENTATION:
├── Weekly standups (15 minutes)
├── Key priorities (4 per quarter)
├── Every department funnels up to the same priorities
└── "It doesn't matter which department; we're all aligned"
```

## The Buyout Framework (Added by Nate Checketts)

### The Buyout Framework

```
THE PROBLEM:
└── Venture/PE clock (5-7 years) forces short-term decisions

THE OPPORTUNITY:
└── Buy out the investor and take control

THE PROCESS:
├── Step 1: Fall in love with the mission
├── Step 2: Recognize short-term decisions vs. long-term decisions
├── Step 3: Build conviction, "I don't want to give this up"
├── Step 4: Create an SPV (special purpose vehicle)
│   └── Raise money from high-net-worth individuals
│   └── Family offices, tactical investors
├── Step 5: Negotiate with the investor
│   └── "I can get you a benchmark return"
└── Step 6: Refresh the cap table

THE RESULT:
├── Long-term thinking returns
├── Better decisions (women's, NBA deals, etc.)
├── Company more than doubled in 2 years
└── Burn the boats, no safety net, better outcomes
```

## Brian Tracy — The Three Thinking Tools

### Zero-Based Thinking (ZWINK)

```
THE QUESTION:
    "Knowing what I now know, is there anything that I am doing that I would not start up again today?"

THE PROCESS:
    │
    ├── Look at every part of your life and business
    ├── Ask: "Would I get into this again today?"
    ├── If NO → Ask: "How do I get out, and how fast?"
    └── Have the courage to cut your losses

WHEN TO USE IT:
    │
    ├── When you experience CHRONIC STRESS
    ├── When something keeps you awake at night
    ├── When something irritates, angers, or frustrates you
    └── When you're in a situation that "pisses you off"

APPLICATION AREAS:
    │
    ├── Relationships (personal and business)
    ├── Products and services
    ├── People (employees, partners)
    ├── Investments (time, emotion, money)
    └── Business model

THE RULE:
    └── "The best time to fire a person is the first time it crosses your mind."
    └── "Cut your losses quickly. Let your winnings ride."

THE COURAGE WORD:
    └── Zero-based thinking requires the "C word" — COURAGE
    └── You need the courage to admit you made a mistake
    └── "If you don't like it, change it. If you don't want to change it, shut up."
```

### Worst Possible Outcome (WPO)

```
THE QUESTION:
    "What is the worst possible outcome of this course of action?"

THE PROCESS:
    │
    ├── Ask: "What's the worst that could happen?"
    ├── Ask: "Can I survive it if it were to occur?"
    ├── If YES → Your worry goes away → Mind goes calm
    └── If NO → "What could I do to make sure it doesn't happen?"

WHY IT WORKS:
    │
    └── Fear is the #1 reason we worry about anything
    └── When you ask the question and can survive the answer, worry disappears
    └── Now you become PROACTIVE and take charge of your life

THE RESULT:
    └── Calm mind → Clear thinking → Better decisions
```

### The Principle of Constraints

```
THE PRINCIPLE:
    │
    └── Between you and anything you want to accomplish, there is always a constraint,
         a limiting factor, or a choke point that sets the speed at which you accomplish your goal.

THE 80/20 RULE OF CONSTRAINTS:
    │
    └── 80% of the constraints holding you back are within yourself and within your business
    └── They are NOT on the outside (competition, market, taxes, regulation)

THE QUESTION:
    │
    └── "What is it in ME that's causing this problem?"

THE SKILL QUESTION:
    │
    └── "What ONE skill, if I were absolutely excellent at it, would help me the most to double my income?"

THE TRUTH:
    │
    └── Most of us are only ONE SKILL AWAY from doubling our income
    └── All business skills are learnable
    └── The challenge: You don't like the skill (it involves rejection, embarrassment, ego, potential failure)

THE APPROACH:
    └── "Like climbing the ladder of success... sniper, one shot, one kill."
    └── Learn ONE skill at a time. Focus. The most important skill.
```

### The Fast vs. Slow Thinking Framework

```
FAST THINKING (System 1):
    │
    ├── Intuitive, instinctive, automatic, reactionary
    ├── Example: Driving through busy traffic
    └── Appropriate for: Low or no consequence decisions

SLOW THINKING (System 2):
    │
    ├── Deliberate, thoughtful, analytical
    ├── Example: Major business decisions, hiring key people
    └── Appropriate for: High potential consequence decisions

THE PROBLEM:
    │
    └── Where people should use slow thinking, they use fast thinking
    └── Entrepreneurs are extremely guilty of this

THE RULE:
    │
    └── "The longer that you take to make an important decision, the better the decision will be."
    └── "When it is not necessary to decide, it is necessary not to decide."

THE APPLICATION:
    │
    ├── Hire slowly (fast hiring = invariably wrong)
    ├── Buy as much time as you can for important decisions
    ├── Put it off for a day, a weekend, a week, a month
    └── "Sleep on it" — let it ruminate

PETER DRUCKER'S RULE:
    └── "Fast people decisions are invariably wrong people decisions."
```

## The Seven Greats of Business

### The Complete Business Success Framework

```
1. GREAT LEADERSHIP
    │
    ├── The ability to get results
    ├── The ability to allocate resources (make decisions)
    ├── The ability to make HARD decisions
    ├── "The first job of the leader is to ask: 'What results are expected of me?'"
    └── "Of all the results expected of you, what is the most important result?"

2. GREAT PRODUCT OR SERVICE
    │
    ├── 90% of your success is having a great product in the first place
    ├── "Put it on the plate" — superb food at good prices
    ├── Be OBSESSED with quality as YOUR CUSTOMERS define it
    ├── Be CLOSE to the customer
    └── "Invest in improving the quality of your product"

3. GREAT MARKETING PLAN
    │
    ├── Marketing = ATTRACTING interested prospects
    ├── "How can you tell if you have a great marketing plan? You have a steady stream of interested prospects."
    ├── The fastest way to kill a bad product is to advertise it
    └── Marketing is "Who's hungry?" — attracting people interested in your benefit

4. GREAT SALES PLAN
    │
    ├── Sales = CONVERTING interested people into customers
    ├── "If you go from random selling to a proven sales process, you increase your sales 500% with the same number of prospects."
    ├── All successful companies have a PROFESSIONAL SALES PROCESS
    └── Sales process must be SYSTEMATIZED and DEBUGGED

5. GREAT BUSINESS PLAN
    │
    ├── Forces you to think LONG TERM and THINK SLOWLY
    ├── "What kind of technology will you require? What will it cost? Who's going to run it?"
    ├── "What kind of advertising, publicity, promotion? How much will it cost? What returns?"
    └── The Napoleon principle: "We don't have the horses" — what's your constraint?

6. GREAT NUMBERS
    │
    ├── All business activities and results can be expressed in numerical form
    ├── Identify your ECONOMIC DENOMINATOR — the most important number
    ├── "What is your number? Do you know the most important number?"
    └── Common mistake: Focusing on sales when you're losing money on every sale

7. GREAT CUSTOMER SERVICE EXPERIENCE
    │
    ├── Make customers so happy they buy again and bring their friends
    ├── "We're not in the shoe business; we're in the customer happiness business."
    ├── Customer happiness = repeat business = rapid growth
    └── If you have a customer complaint, the head of the company doesn't sleep at night

THE RULE:
    └── "If you're missing one of them, it's like dialing a seven-digit number. Miss one, and your company goes broke."
```

## Fundraising Strategy (Added by D Murphy)

### The "Raise From Strength" Framework

```
NEVER RAISE WHEN YOU NEED IT:
+-- Fundraising should be done from a position of strength
+-- If you're red hot, go fundraise

WHY:
+-- Leverage → Better terms
+-- Momentum → Investors want in
+-- Valuation → You control the price

THE TRAP:
+-- Raising when you're desperate
+-- Bad terms, lower valuation
+-- Investors smell weakness

THE ALPHA:
+-- If you're going to take money, take dilution
+-- Force them to up the ante on valuation and terms
+-- Timing is the most important part of fundraising
```

### The "Raise to Scare Off Competition" Framework

```
RAISING CAPITAL AS A TACTIC:
+-- Raising enough money says "stay the [__] out of our way"
+-- The quantum of capital signals seriousness
+-- First mover advantage + deep pockets = hard to catch

THE MATH:
+-- If you have the best investors in the space,
    they're not going to invest in anyone competing with you

RESULT:
+-- Keep the competition out
+-- Build awareness that you're doing something serious
```

### The "Jockey Over Horse" Framework

```
WHY D GOT MONEY:
+-- "I got money because of my experience, not because of my idea"
+-- He didn't even have an idea at the breakfast meeting

THE LESSON:
+-- Great investors bet on the jockey, not the horse
+-- Subject matter expertise = trust
+-- If you spent 20 years in an industry, go do something in that space

THE AVERAGE AGE OF VENTURE FOUNDERS:
+-- 43 years old
+-- D was 41 (young for the stat, old in his own mind)

THE RED FLAG IN INVESTORS:
+-- If they don't understand what you're doing, it's tough
+-- They need to fundamentally understand it
+-- "You want people to have subject matter expertise where they can come in and actually give you help"

THE QUOTE:
+-- "If I looked under the hood of every single investment I ever made, I would never have gotten there."
+-- Fred Wilson, Union Square Ventures
```

### The "Raise in a Dead Market" Framework

```
SPRING 2022: VC WAS DEAD
+-- Couldn't raise money
+-- Bottom of the market

BUT GHOST WAS GROWING:
+-- $1M/month in 4 months
+-- Momentum

THE RESULT:
+-- 30 firms took meetings
+-- "Nobody's growing like this right now"

WHY:
+-- VCs need growth
+-- If you can't 10x, 50x, 100x, there's no point
+-- They're okay being wrong 99 out of 100 if the one is SpaceX
```

### Simon Squibb — The 7 and 8 Rule

- 9-10: Keep them, give them equity
- 1-2: They're at their job—fire them or they quit
- 7-8: Almost good enough—the hardest to fire
- If you keep 7-8s, your 9-10s will leave
- "If you have to talk about someone more than once or twice in a week, you probably have a problem"
- **Fire 7s and 8s**—they will often thank you later

### Simon Squibb — Hire for Purpose

- If people believe in the purpose, you never have to manage people—just manage purpose
- Check their social media to see if they really care
- Equity aligns your team with your success
- "Most people don't have the guts to do it"
- High turnover, high stress is a bigger risk than any equity problem

### Simon Squibb — Systems Over Generalists

- Move from generalist mindset to specialist mindset
- Build systems to allow people to specialize
- "I have often gotten myself replaced in a company I built—that's actually pretty smart"

### Simon Squibb — Know Your Destination

- Why are you growing?
- What's the real reason?
- If you don't know your destination, growth is aimless

### Simon Squibb — Equity ≠ Control

- You don't lose control if you drop below 50%
- Control comes from the shareholder agreement, not equity percentage
- "Equity ownership does not equal control"
- 52/48 creates resentment; 50/50 creates partnership
- But have a shareholder agreement to avoid decision lock

### Deeke — The CEO-Centric Trap vs. Systems-Driven CEO

### Chris (VC) — The Sales & Marketing Flip

```
FOR MATURE SaaS COMPANIES:
    │
    ├── 40% of revenue → Sales & Marketing
    ├── 20% of revenue → R&D (Product Development)
    └── 20% of revenue → SG&A (General & Administrative)

THE FLIP:
    │
    ├── Early days: 80% R&D, 20% Sales & Marketing
    ├── As you scale: Flip to 40-20-20
    └── Be prepared for this transition

WHY IT MATTERS:
    │
    ├── You can't build a company on product alone
    ├── Sales & marketing costs dominate mature companies
    └── Plan your hiring and spending accordingly
```

## Strategic Positioning (The 3 D's) (Added by iLab)

When setting strategy, define your 3 D's:

1. **Disruptive** — How does your business change the industry? (e.g., Airbnb: homes as hotels)
2. **Discontinuous** — What new capabilities does your product enable? (e.g., AWS: cloud computing)
3. **Defensible** — What's your moat? (IP, network effects, data, switching costs)

**Rule:** Avoid "faster, better, cheaper" — it's not defensible. Find your 3 D's.

### Service-Based Business — The "Closest to Cash" Mindset

```
AS A CEO, YOUR JOB IS TO:
    │
    ├── Build systems starting closest to the cash
    ├── Systematize delivery before sales
    ├── Systematize sales before marketing
    └── Systematize marketing before traffic

WHY:
    │
    └── If you can't deliver well, you can't keep clients
    └── If you can't sell well, you can't get clients
    └── If you can't market well, you can't attract clients
    └── If you can't generate traffic, you can't find clients
```

### Service-Based Business — The Founder Bottleneck Test

```
ASK YOURSELF:
    │
    ├── Can I take a week off without things falling apart?
    ├── Can a new client be onboarded without me?
    ├── Can a sale happen without me?
    └── Can a client get help without me?

If any answer is "No," you have a system gap.
Start building the closest-to-cash system first.
```

### WSJ — The Acquisition vs. Organic Growth Framework

```
ORGANIC GROWTH:
    │
    ├── Find sites one by one
    ├── Build from scratch
    ├── Time: 18-24 months per location
    └── Cost: High (construction, real estate)

ACQUISITION:
    │
    ├── Buy existing chain

    ├── Time: 6-12 months per conversion
    └── Cost: Half of building new

    └── 3x faster than organic expansion

LESSON: Acquisition can accelerate scale dramatically
```
