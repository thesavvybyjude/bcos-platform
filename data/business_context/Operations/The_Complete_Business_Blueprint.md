# The Complete Business Blueprint

## Purpose
To provide a comprehensive, step-by-step framework for starting, building, scaling, and exiting a business—from mindset to exit—based on 35 years of entrepreneurial experience across 19 companies.

## Core Philosophy

> "A business starts with a feeling, an instinct that perhaps you need to make a change, and then applying yourself to learning what is the best way to build something that you love."

> "Build a brand, not a business."

## Principles

### Part 1: How to Start

#### 1. Start with Passion, Not a Niche
- A business starts with a feeling, not an idea
- Follow your passions—don't listen to people who say that's not the way to start
- Your passions initially don't generate revenue because you haven't applied a business mindset to them
- "I love marketing, so I started a creative agency. There were 500 other businesses doing almost exactly the same. How did I win? I didn't have an original idea."

#### 2. The Love/Hate List
- Write down what you like doing and what you don't like doing
- Get really good at what you love
- Outsource what you don't like doing
- "The school system has taught you the wrong thing: if you're not good at something, you need to spend more time getting better at it. That is a lie."

#### 3. Combining Forces
- You don't need an original idea—you need to combine forces with other people
- Find someone who can execute on the thing you love but can't do yourself
- Example: Simon loved marketing, partnered with someone who could turn ideas into visuals—created a 50/50 business

#### 4. The Idea Phase
1. Link to your passion
2. Find a partner if there's a gap in your ability to execute
3. Don't try to do it all on your own

#### 5. Execution First
- What is the first thing you can do to make your business idea come to life?
- For Simon: a podcast
- For you: a blog, a LinkedIn post, a social media handle
- Keep the first step simple
- Execution doesn't have to be brilliant at the start—just start

#### 6. Revenue Models
- Experiment with different ways to make money
- Don't limit yourself to one revenue model from the start
- Example: Instead of charging by the hour, charge by the outcome

#### 7. Purpose
- Make sure your purpose is strong
- If it's just about profit, everyone will want more money
- If you have a purpose bigger than yourself, you won't have to manage people—you only have to manage purpose

#### 8. The Airbnb Example
- Initially sold cereal boxes at conventions to get to know customers and make money
- That was the money they used to get the business going

### Part 2: How to Win

#### 9. Delayed Gratification
- Don't rush to charge customers
- First customer: do the work for free
- Build relationships, over-deliver, create advocates
- Facebook and Instagram built massive user bases before monetizing
- "Do not think about making money straight up. Build a brand not a business."

#### 10. Culture Eats Strategy
- Culture will eat strategy for breakfast every single time
- Make sure your culture is client-centric
- Most people are too busy thinking about how to get money out of their customer—instead, think about how to bring value

#### 11. Hack Luck
1. **Be persistent**—outlast other people (purpose and passion help with this)
2. **Know your destination**—what is success for you?
3. **Learn to take risk**—lean into fear, learn to love fear

#### 12. The Secret to It All
- Learn to accept that you could lose everything and it doesn't matter
- You've got to be willing to risk it all
- "Do not rush. Do not see this as anything but a marathon. It's not a sprint."

---

### Part 3: How to Lose (Embrace Failure)

#### 13. Let Things Not Own You
- If you let things own you, you are controlled by the very thing that's meant to bring you pleasure
- Learn to lose those things and not care

#### 14. Let Your Short-Term Ego Go
- Don't worry about driving the right car to give an image to people
- Learn to enjoy looking like a loser
- Let people underestimate you—it's powerful
- "A students end up working for D students because A students are scared to lose"

#### 15. Learn to Be a D Student
- Love to fail
- Take your time
- Don't let someone else decide whether you're successful or not
- Power up your internal ego that tells you you're not a loser

---

### Part 4: The Mind Map (vs. Business Plan)

#### 16. Why Mind Maps Win
- Business plans kill companies—they lock you in
- Mind maps keep you nimble and free
- No template to buy, no product to sell you

#### 17. How to Build a Mind Map
1. **Center**: Your hobby/passion
2. **Branch 1**: The Business
3. **Branch 2**: Network (from podcast, etc.)
4. **Branch 3**: Brands (potential partners)
5. **Branch 4**: Team (who you need)
6. **Branch 5**: Partners (not just employees)
7. **Branch 6**: Merchandising/Products
8. Continue branching infinitely

#### 18. Key Mind Map Insight
- Once you know something is on your mind map, you can look out for it as you build other things
- A mind map is infinite—a 20-page document is linear and restrictive

### Part 5: How to Find Purpose

#### 19. The Wrong Question
- Schools ask: "What are you going to do when you grow up?"
- The right question: "What problem are you going to solve?"

#### 20. How to Find Your Purpose
1. **Think about problems that have affected you**—big or small
2. **Start breaking down how to make your life and purpose match**
3. **You don't have to do this alone**—team up with others
4. **1+1 = 11** (once you know your purpose, seek out people and communities)

#### 21. The 3% Rule
- Your dream is often just a 3% difference from your existing life
- 3% difference between chimpanzees and humans
- You might be just 3% away from living your purpose

#### 22. Purpose Insight
- Once you find your purpose, don't think it's so big that you don't do it
- Team up with other people to do it
- "It's harder to work for someone else than work for yourself, despite what you have been told."

---

### Part 6: How to Find a Co-founder

#### 23. Why You Need a Co-founder
- Accountability (like having a gym buddy)
- "I'd rather have 50% of a business I enjoy that's successful than 100% of a nightmare and a failure"

#### 24. The Co-founder Framework
1. **Identify what you love and hate doing**—be honest and clear
2. **Find someone with the opposite skill but the same moral code**
3. **Write down exactly what this person looks like**—be detailed
4. **Post it**—tell people you're looking, post on LinkedIn, ask around

#### 25. The Moral Code Test
- "What if from this day until you're 70, you have the most amazing life—three houses, private jets, everything—but at 70 everyone finds out you're a financial fraud? Do you want to take the deal?"
- 50/50 of people say yes. Be careful.

#### 26. The Red Car Theory
- If I mention "red car," you'll start seeing red cars
- If you don't mention it, you won't see them
- Write down what you want so you can see it

#### 27. Equity Structure
- 50/50 if you have a co-founder
- Don't belittle the other partner with 52/48
- But have a shareholder agreement to avoid decision lock

### Part 7: How to Sell

#### 28. Sell the Sizzle, Not the Steak
- Steve Jobs didn't say "here's a phone with an Intel processor"
- He talked about how the product was for game changers, creative types
- Your accountant can be your best salesperson if you give them the tools

#### 29. The Three-Step Sales Process
1. **Do they need you?** — Research, understand their needs
2. **Do they like you?** — Genuine connection matters
3. **The deal** — If steps 1 and 2 are right, the deal will happen

#### 30. The Top 1% Sales Secret
- Top 50 salespeople contact a lead 5 times
- Top 1% contact a lead every single month until they get the deal
- Simon approached some companies for 9 years before they became clients

#### 31. Be Authentic
- Sales is about selling someone a car they actually need
- The best salespeople tell you "you don't want this car, you want that car"
- Build a relationship, not a transaction

---

### Part 8: How to Market

#### 32. The 50% Rule
- 50% of what you spend on marketing will be wasted
- If you have a million views but the wrong views, it's wasted

#### 33. Marketing is Complex
- It's combining PR, brand messaging, product market fit
- "Understanding who your customer is" is #1

#### 34. The Staircase Philosophy
- Do something wild and unexpected (like buying a staircase)
- News jack it—use it for PR and marketing
- Evolve it (add a doorbell for dreams)
- Get a partner to sponsor it
- "Marketing is meant to be fun"

#### 35. Marketing is Connected to Sales
- How your sales team speaks to someone is also marketing
- That's brand marketing

#### 36. The Systems Approach
- Build a system for whatever marketing you do
- Better to do one platform well than many badly
- Example: One core video edited in-app for each channel

#### 37. Apply Your Marketing Love
- If you hate being on camera but love writing, do email marketing and LinkedIn
- Don't force yourself to do something that's not sustainable

---

### Part 9: How to PR Your Business

#### 38. Be Targeted
- PR should be strategic, not just for ego
- Getting on Tech Crunch might feel good but not impact your business
- Better to reach the local paper that reaches your actual customers

#### 39. Journalists are Lazy
- Write the press release like the actual story
- Provide photos, quotes, everything they need
- Make it easy for them—they want to go to lunch

#### 40. Connect with Journalists
- Follow them on Twitter
- Comment on their posts
- Build a relationship before you need anything
- Be disciplined in what you post—you are always the #1 PR engine of your brand

---

### Part 10: How to Get an Investor

#### 41. Ask Yourself First
- Do you actually need an investor?
- "Sometimes if you get the wrong investor, you will have a new boss and your life will become a nightmare"

#### 42. Ways to Raise Money
1. **Family and friends**—be honest, tell them they could lose everything
2. **Your team**—people already working with you can invest (lower salary + equity)
3. **Angel investors**—ask for help, not money (make them feel lucky to be involved)
4. **VCs (Venture Capitalists)**—only if you have traction and they have money
5. **Clients/brands**—they can fund your expansion
6. **Crowdfunding**—pre-sell product, equity crowdfunding, gofundme

#### 43. The Best Way to Get an Angel Investor
- Don't ask for money
- Ask for help
- Make them feel valued
- Create FOMO (fear of missing out)

#### 44. VC Advice
- Check if they actually have money
- See who they've invested in before
- Get introduced by a portfolio company
- "Some of these VCs are awful"—do your due diligence

---

### Part 11: How to Get a Sponsor

#### 45. Two Reasons Sponsorship Happens
1. **Value return**—trackable ROI (views, sales)
2. **Emotional sale**—personal connection

#### 46. Understanding the Brand
- Understand what their brand values are
- Research how they traditionally advertise
- Visa = Olympics—don't ask them to sponsor your event

#### 47. The People Inside the Brand
- Build champions inside the company
- They won't get fired for sponsoring something relevant
- The brand is made up of people

#### 48. Media Buyers and Agencies
- Media buyers hold the money—sell to them, not the brand
- Agencies can insert your idea into campaigns

#### 49. Use the Brand Naturally
- Wear whoop on the street with millions of views
- They'll contact you
- "Just leaning in to a brand relationship and not initially thinking about the money"

---

### Part 12: How to Build a Brand (Company)

#### 50. Start with Personal Brand
- Write down your values
- What do you care about?
- What are your non-negotiables?
- What would you say no to? What would you say yes to?

#### 51. Two Brand Models
1. **Reference Model**—sponsor someone who represents your brand values (Nike, Canon)
2. **Leadership Model**—you personally represent the brand (Apple, Steve Jobs)

#### 52. Risks of Each
- Reference: celebrity does something bad—brand damage
- Leadership: leader leaves—brand loses magic

#### 53. Learn to Say No
- If your spider senses tell you something isn't right, don't take the money
- Your brand is your most valuable asset
- "They told me they bought the brand, not the business"

---

### Part 13: How to Hire, Grow, and Build

#### 54. Hire for Purpose
- If people believe in the purpose, you never have to manage people—just manage purpose
- Check their social media to see if they really care

#### 55. Give Equity
- Equity aligns your team with your success
- "Most people don't have the guts to do it"
- High turnover, high stress is a bigger risk than any equity problem

#### 56. Systems Over Generalists
- Move from generalist mindset to specialist mindset
- Build systems to allow people to specialize
- "I have often gotten myself replaced in a company I built—that's actually pretty smart"

#### 57. Know Your Destination
- Why are you growing?
- What's the real reason?
- If you don't know your destination, growth is aimless

#### 58. The 7 and 8 Rule
- 9-10: Keep them, give them equity
- 1-2: They're at their job—fire them or they quit
- 7-8: Almost good enough—the hardest to fire
- If you keep 7-8s, your 9-10s will leave
- "If you have to talk about someone more than once or twice in a week, you probably have a problem"
- **Fire 7s and 8s**—they will often thank you later

---

### Part 14: How to Go Global

#### 59. Reduce Risk
- If you're in more than one market, you spread your risk
- Hong Kong had ups and downs—global expansion provided a counterbalance

#### 60. Research
- Find out where your product/service could work
- Even if you don't open the second office, this research is valuable

#### 61. Franchising
- Set up a franchising arrangement
- Someone else opens it, you make money
- Closest thing to passive income

#### 62. Big Companies Are Easier
- "It's easier to run a big company than a small company"
- Don't trap yourself in any one market
- A big business gives you freedom from your own business

---

### Part 15: How to Get a Mentor

#### 63. You Don't Need a Mentor
- You need someone who can answer your questions
- You need a co-founder for accountability
- You need a salesperson for sales
- You need specific advice, not generic mentorship

#### 64. How to Get Mentorship Anyway
1. **Research what matters to that person**—don't ask a proponent of property development to mentor you if you hate property
2. **Define what you mean**—"I would like 10 minutes of your time once a week"
3. **Change the word to "advisor"**—more glamorous, more structured
4. **Get a referral**—someone who knows you to refer you
5. **Give value first**—redesign their website, help them before you ask for help

#### 65. The Give Without Take Philosophy
- Don't just extract value
- Give without taking

---

### Part 16: How Equity Works

#### 66. Equity ≠ Control
- You don't lose control if you drop below 50%
- Control comes from the shareholder agreement, not equity percentage
- "Equity ownership does not equal control"

#### 67. Know Your Destination
- If you don't know where you're going, you don't know how much equity you need
- Too many people sell too much equity early and can't get to the finish line

#### 68. Don't Sell Equity If You Don't Have To
- Bootstrap is healthier
- 100% control and 100% ownership is always better

#### 69. 50/50 Co-founder Rule
- 52/48 creates resentment
- 50/50 creates partnership
- But have a shareholder agreement to avoid decision lock

#### 70. Share Options vs. Actual Equity
- Share options ≠ actual equity (you might not have decision-making power)
- Actual equity = real ownership

#### 71. Reverse Engineer
- Know your end goal and reverse the share equity structure from day one

#### 72. Use SAFE Notes
- Y Combinator's SAFE (Simple Agreement for Future Equity)
- No need to determine company value now
- It's a discount on future value

---

### Part 17: How to Sell Your Business

#### 73. Don't Want to Sell
- The best way to sell your company is not want to sell it
- That's the strongest negotiating position
- Mark Zuckerberg turned down $1B from Yahoo; now worth $1T

#### 74. Partnership Work
- Work with a company that might want to buy you
- Build the relationship
- They'll realize they should just buy you

#### 75. Merger
- Merge with a competitor
- Often the easiest way
- But sometimes selling outside the industry gets more money

#### 76. Management Buyout
- Let the leadership team buy the business over time
- Really powerful way to exit while giving your team what they deserve

#### 77. Never Build to Sell
- Don't pitch that you're building to sell
- Build a business you love
- "People want to buy a business like that"

## Master Principles Summary

### The Foundations
1. **Start with passion**—not a market gap or niche
2. **Love what you do**—if you don't, you won't outlast competitors
3. **Know your purpose**—why are you doing this?

### The Mindset
1. **Delayed gratification**—build value before monetizing
2. **Embrace failure**—learn to lose, learn to be a D student
3. **Take risk**—lean into fear, don't fear losing everything
4. **Know your destination**—what is success for you?

### The Execution
1. **Mind map, not business plan**—stay nimble, flexible
2. **Build systems**—move from generalist to specialist
3. **Culture over strategy**—client-centric culture wins

### The People
1. **Find the right co-founder**—opposite skills, same moral code
2. **Hire for purpose**—give equity, manage purpose not people
3. **Fire 7s and 8s**—don't let them drive out your 9s and 10s

### The Strategy
1. **Go global**—reduce risk, big companies are easier
2. **Sell the sizzle, not the steak**—sell outcomes, not features
3. **Build a brand, not a business**—brand is the most valuable asset

### The Money
1. **Experiment with revenue models**—don't limit yourself
2. **Raise money from strength**—don't need it when you raise
3. **Equity does not equal control**—structure matters

## Alex Hormozi — The Grand Slam Offer Framework

### The Value Equation

```
VALUE = (Dream Outcome × Likelihood) / (Time × Effort)

INCREASE THE TOP:
├── Bigger dream outcome
├── Higher likelihood of achievement
└── More proof and testimonials

DECREASE THE BOTTOM:
├── Faster time to result
├── Less effort required
└── Fewer obstacles

EXAMPLE:
├── $500 service: "I'll help you lose 5 lbs"
├── $5,000 service: "I'll help you lose 20 lbs in 8 weeks with a done-for-you plan"
└── $50,000 service: "I'll help you lose 50 lbs in 12 weeks with a team of experts, meal plans, and weekly coaching"
```

### The First Business Steps (Service Business)

- Pick one skill you're good at
- Go B2B (sell to businesses, not consumers)
- Sell a result, not a service
- Get clients cold (DMs, calls, door-to-door)
- Charge more than you're comfortable with
- Get a testimonial (even if you have to do it for free)
- Post on social media daily
- Hire someone to do the fulfillment
- Reinvest everything back into the business

### The Skilled vs. Unskilled Path

- If you have a skill: sell the skill directly (consulting, freelancing)
- If you don't have a skill: sell appointments (book appointments for others)
- Both paths work; the skilled path is faster

### Volume Negates Luck

- "I was willing to do the thing that nobody else wanted to do, and that's how I won."
- Post more, DM more, call more, meet more
- Reputation is built by doing, not by waiting
- If you're not embarrassed by your first offer, you launched too late

### The Three Paths to Scale

- More clients (acquire more)
- Higher price (charge more)
- Better fulfillment (deliver more value)
- Do all three simultaneously

### The Lead Magnet → Appointment → Client Pipeline

- Create a lead magnet (free value)
- Drive traffic to it (organic + paid)
- Capture name, email, phone
- Book a call (sales conversation)
- Close the client
- Deliver the result
- Get referrals and testimonials
- Repeat

### WSJ — Front-Loaded Offer Strategy

```
THE DISCOUNT TRAP:
├── Customers sign up for promotional rates
├── Price normalization causes shock
├── 90% cancel within a year
└── "American consumers don't like dramatic change in prices"

THE SOLUTION:
├── Higher initial price + greater value = lower churn
├── Premium positioning attracts right customers
├── Methodology: $17-$30/meal, no discounts, profitable from Year 2
└── "Our sweet spot is men who live on takeout 3-4x/week spending $35/meal"

LESSON: Don't over-discount to acquire customers
```

### WSJ — Brand Differentiation in Commodity Markets

```
LIQUID DEATH EXAMPLE:
├── Bottled water is a commodity
├── "There's nothing different about our water... it's all just brand differences"
├── Sell rebellion, not water
├── Organic viral growth
└── $1.4B valuation in a commodity market

LESSON: In a commodity market, brand is everything
```

## Brian Tracy — Product Quality as the Foundation

### The "Put It on the Plate" Principle

```
THE TEST:
    │
    └── How many people, after using your product or service, turn to another person and say:
         "Geez, that's a great product. Geez, that's a great service. Geez, that's a great company."

THE NUMBERS:
    │
    ├── Average in America: 30-35%
    ├── Top companies (Apple, etc.): 70-80%+
    └── Customers sleep on sidewalks to be first purchasers

THE INC 500 STUDY:
    │
    ├── Fastest growing company: 42,500% growth
    ├── All were OBSESSED with quality
    └── All were CLOSE to the customer

THE KEY:
    └── "Invest in improving the quality of your product. Make the product better and better and better."
```

### The Customer Happiness Flywheel

```
CUSTOMER HAPPINESS FLYWHEEL:
    │
    ├── Get customers to buy from you FIRST (rather than competitor)
    ├── Get them to buy AGAIN (because they're so happy)
    └── Get them to BRING THEIR FRIENDS (word of mouth)

THE ZAPPOS MODEL:
    │
    └── "We're not in the shoe business; we're in the customer happiness business."
    └── Sold for $1.2 billion → Founder took home $350 million

WHY IT WORKS:
    └── "Nothing gives us greater joy than to make our customers happy."
```
