# Time & Attention Management

## Purpose
To provide frameworks and systems for managing time and attention as your most precious assets, enabling you to work smarter, not harder.

## Core Philosophy

> "Your outcomes in life follow your attention."

> "The three pillars of time wealth: Awareness, Attention, and Control."

## Principles

### 1. Time is Your Most Precious Asset
- It's the one thing you can never get back
- Worth more than all the money in the world

### 2. Attention Determines Outcomes
- All outcomes follow your attention
- Where you put your attention matters more than how much time you have

### 3. Dislocate Input from Output
- Normal: 1 unit input = 1 unit output
- Unlocked: 1 unit input = 10 units output
- This is how you gain control over your time

### 4. Parkinson's Law
- Work expands to fill the time allotted
- Give yourself 8 hours for email = 8 hours of email
- Give yourself 30 minutes = 30 minutes of email
- Use constraints to force productivity

### 5. Attention Residue
- Popping open email/text pulls attention away
- Tiny bit of attention gets stuck on the distraction
- Constant state of cognitive hangover
- You're never operating at full capacity

### 6. The Four Types of Professional Time
- **Management:** Emails, meetings, phone calls, processing (80% of most people's time)
- **Creation:** Writing, coding, creating things
- **Consumption:** Reading, listening, learning (intentional)
- **Ideation:** Thinking, connecting ideas, big picture questions

## Decision Frameworks

### The Four Types of Professional Time

```
| Type         | Description                    | Examples                    | Typical % |
|--------------|--------------------------------|-----------------------------|-----------|
| Management   | Processing, coordinating       | Email, meetings, admin      | 80%       |
| Creation     | Producing, making              | Writing, coding, designing  | 15%       |
| Consumption  | Learning, input                | Reading, listening, research| 3%        |
| Ideation     | Thinking, connecting           | Big picture, strategy       | 2%        |

GOAL: Flip this distribution
```

### Parkinson's Law Application

```
Identify low-leverage management tasks
    │
    ▼
Set uncomfortably short time blocks
    │
    │   Example: Email → 30 minutes, not all day
    │
    ▼
During blocks, do only that task
    │
    ▼
Free up time for creation, consumption, ideation
```

### Attention Residue Prevention

```
When working on a main task:

1. Close email entirely
2. Close messaging apps
3. Put phone away/on grayscale
4. No checking anything during focus block

Why? Because even a "quick check" leaves attention residue
→ You operate at less than full cognitive capacity
→ Switching costs are real

Batching is the antidote.
```

## Rules

- **Always time block email and low-leverage tasks** — Don't let them bleed across your day
- **Always create space for consumption and ideation** — Most people have zero
- **Never check email during creative work** — Attention residue is real
- **Never use Parkinson's Law on high-leverage tasks** — Constraints are for management, not creation
- **Always plan Sunday nights** — 15 minutes on Sunday = 2.5 hours on Monday
- **Never let management time exceed 50%** — You need space for what matters

## Heuristics

- If it's management, compress it
- If it's creation, protect it
- If it's consumption, be intentional
- If it's ideation, create space for it
- 15 minutes on Sunday is worth 2.5 hours on Monday

### Parkinson's Law Schedule

```
LOW-LEVERAGE TASKS (Compressed):
- Email: 9:00-9:30 AM, 2:00-2:30 PM (30 min blocks)
- Admin/Invoices: 3:00-3:30 PM
- Meetings: Batch on Tuesdays/Thursdays

HIGH-LEVERAGE TASKS (Protected):
- Creation: 7:00-10:00 AM (Deep Work, no interruptions)
- Consumption: 10:00-10:30 AM (Reading)
- Ideation: 4:00-5:00 PM (Thinking, walking, no phone)
```
