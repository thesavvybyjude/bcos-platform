# Market Stacking & TAM Strategy

## Purpose
To provide frameworks for identifying adjacent markets, stacking them for total addressable market (TAM), and positioning a product to capture from multiple categories simultaneously.

## Core Philosophy

> "If you can stack multiple markets and take little pieces from each, and eventually a large piece from each, that's where your opportunity is."

> "Don't just take numbers at face value—understand why the numbers are the way they are."

## Principles

### 1. Market Stacking Creates Massive TAM
- Identify adjacent markets you can capture from
- Slate: Chocolate milk ($1.5B) + Protein drinks ($3-4.5B) + RTD coffee ($?B)
- Each market adds to your total addressable market
- "Take little pieces from each, then eventually a large piece"

### 2. Understand the "Why" Behind the Numbers
- Skim milk declining 42% YoY
- Whole milk and 2% growing 11%
- The dairy market isn't dying—it's shifting
- Look at the trends beneath the surface

### 3. The "Better for You" Thesis
- Brands that will be here for decades are better for you
- "If you're just taking something that's [__] for you and giving it a new face, you can buy the sales but the world is moving away from that"
- Less sugar, more protein, cleaner ingredients

### 4. Taste Always Wins
- Peter Rahal (RXBar): "Taste always wins"
- People won't sacrifice on taste for better ingredients
- You need both: great taste AND better ingredients

### 5. Vision-Driven Product Development
- "What do you think the world is going to be in 5-10 years?"
- Build a brand and product line that fits that vision
- Don't position based on what already exists

### 6. Kickstarter as a Launch Vehicle
- Build awareness, raise funds, create buzz
- Whole Foods reached out because of Kickstarter
- Direct correlation: Kickstarter → Whole Foods → Retail growth

### 7. The Whiteboard Method
- Start with Google and LinkedIn
- Meet one person, then another, then another
- "We found this dude on LinkedIn and shot him a call"

## Decision Frameworks

### Market Stacking Framework

```
MARKET 1: RTD Chocolate Milk ($1.5B)
    |
    +-- 
MARKET 2: RTD Protein Drinks ($3-4.5B)
    |
    +-- +
MARKET 3: RTD Coffee ($?B)
    |
    +-- =
TOTAL ADDRESSABLE MARKET = Stack of all three

HOW TO CAPTURE:
+-- Product appeals to all three audiences
+-- Brand positioned in the middle
+-- Marketing targets each segment differently

WHY IT WORKS:
+-- You can capture from multiple markets
+-- Create a much larger TAM than any single market
+-- "Take little pieces from each, then eventually a large piece"
```

### "Better for You" Thesis Framework

```
THE OPPORTUNITY:
+-- Everything that exists in the market can be made better

THE THESIS:
+-- Less sugar
+-- More protein
+-- Cleaner ingredients
+-- Better taste

THE TREND:
+-- Skim milk declining 42% YoY
+-- Whole milk and 2% growing 11%
+-- GLP-1 and Ozempic craze

THE VISION:
+-- We see less plastic, more protein, less sugar
+-- People consuming things they love the taste of
+-- People aware of the ingredients they're putting in their bodies
```

### Vision-Driven Product Development Framework

```
START WITH VISION:
+-- What do you think the world will be in 5-10 years?
+-- How does your product fit into that world?
+-- Build the product and brand for that world

THEN POSITION:
+-- Where is the white space in the market?
+-- What do you think is the best brand?

THE MISTAKE:
+-- Trying to position because of what already exists
+-- "That brand's not there in three years, who gives a [__]?"

THE RESULT:
+-- Build the brand you think the world is going to need
+-- Don't build for what exists today
```

### Kickstarter Strategy Framework

```
WHY KICKSTARTER:
+-- Raise capital for first production run
+-- Build awareness and buzz
+-- Create social proof

THE PROCESS:
+-- Final formula (not commercially produced yet)
+-- Set a funding goal
+-- If funded, you get the money
+-- Launch to your network + community

THE RIPPLE EFFECT:
+-- Whole Foods North Atlantic President reached out
+-- "I love the video. I'm vegan so I can't drink it, but let me know if there's anything I can do to help."

RESULT:
+-- Direct correlation: Kickstarter → Whole Foods → Retail growth
```

## Rules

- **Always stack adjacent markets** — Find markets you can capture from
- **Always understand the "why" behind the numbers** — Not just surface-level data
- **Always prioritize taste** — People won't sacrifice on taste
- **Always build for the future** — 5-10 year vision, not today's market
- **Always validate with real customers** — Focus groups, wear testing
- **Never compromise on product quality** — For a quick COGS improvement

## Heuristics

- If you can stack three markets, you've created a much larger TAM
- Taste wins over ingredients (even for founders who care about ingredients)
- The can is a brand identity, not just packaging
- UF milk tastes like cardboard, but it's pure protein

## Triggers

### When Developing a New Product:

```
New product idea

-> Ask: "What is the vision for the world in 5-10 years?"
-> Ask: "How does this product fit into that vision?"
-> Ask: "What markets can I stack?"

Then: Google it, LinkedIn it, find an expert.
"Meet one person, meet another person, meet another person."
```

### When Evaluating a Product Change:

```
Once you're established (5M+ in sales)

-> If you want to change the product:
    +-- Do proper research
    +-- Test with real customers
    +-- Don't make changes to save 2% on COGS

Example: Companies that changed their product to save money
and hurt their brand for years to come.
```
