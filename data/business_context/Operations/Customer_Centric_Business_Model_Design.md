# Customer-Centric Business Model Design

## Purpose
To provide a comprehensive framework for designing business models that are customer-centric, scalable, sustainable, and defensible—moving beyond product-market fit to build a lasting company.

## Core Philosophy

> "The most important thing we're going to do tonight is to try to give you a chance to figure out what your business model is."

> "Even if your idea was terrible, if the business model you come up with is itself a breakthrough, you can win."

## Principles

### 1. Business Models Can Be as Disruptive as Technology
- Norton had a worse product than competitors
- Changed the business model to subscription → Took out two competitors in a year
- "Even if your idea was terrible, the business model you come up with can be a breakthrough"

### 2. The Four Components of a Business Model
- **Create**: Value proposition (what do you do uniquely well for who?)
- **Deliver**: Go-to-market model
- **Make Money**: Cash flow formula / revenue model
- **Sustain**: Ability to operate for the long haul

### 3. The Core (Capability of Really Exceptional Value)
- The one thing that makes people say "because X"
- Ideally one word: Taste, Recommendations, Community, Prestige
- You can't monetize everything—pick one core to monetize

### 4. Minimum Viable Segment (MVS)
- Where everybody has the same need
- Focus your energy on one segment with consistent needs
- If you're pulled in different directions, your energy gets dissipated

### 5. The SLIP Framework
- **S**imple to install and use
- **L**ow to no initial cost
- **I**nstant and ongoing value
- **P**lays well in the ecosystem

### 6. Time to Value is Critical
- For enterprise products: sub-3 months is ideal
- The faster customers see value, the faster they adopt
- Pain is the single biggest barrier to adoption

### 7. The Three UPs
- **Update**: Security fixes, data refreshes (retention)
- **Upgrade**: New capabilities, new technologies (retention + value increase)
- **Upsell**: Additional products/services to same customer (value increase)

### 8. The RSVP Model
- **R**epeatable: Customers keep buying
- **S**calable: You can keep operating at a profitable basis
- **V**aluable: Effective creation (community, partnerships, easy to sell)
- **P**rofitable: Disruptive and defensible

### 9. Co-Creation & Win-Win
- Tap into communities to help build your product
- Must be a win-win—they want to solve the problem too
- Open source is the ultimate example

### 10. The Whole Product Concept
- You are a piece of the puzzle
- You need partners for cloud, models, applications, etc.
- Strategic partnerships are not optional

## Decision Frameworks

### The RSVP Business Model Framework

```
R = REPEATABLE
    │
    └── Customers keep buying
    └── You have a repeatable sales process
    └── Not a one-time sale

S = SCALABLE
    │
    └── You can keep operating at a profitable basis
    └── As you grow, unit economics get better
    └── You can hire, train, and scale

V = VALUABLE
    │
    └── Effective creation (community, partnerships, easy to sell)
    └── You're creating value for customers
    └── Your creation process is efficient

P = PROFITABLE / DEFENSIBLE
    │
    └── Disruptive (doing something nobody else can do)
    └── Defensible (moat around your business)
    └── You make money doing it
```

### The Core Framework

```
FIND YOUR CORE:
    │
    ├── What is the one thing that makes customers say "because X"?
    │
    ├── Examples:
    │   ├── Netflix: Recommendations
    │   ├── Spotify: Discovery
    │   ├── Reddit: Community
    │   ├── Patagonia: Culture/Social Mission
    │   └── Apple: Integrated stack
    │
    └── You can't monetize everything—pick one core to monetize

THE TRAP:
    │
    └── Customers may give you different answers
    └── That's brand attributes—different from your monetizable core
    └── Example: Apple monetizes the integrated stack, not privacy
```

### The SLIP Framework (Product-Led Growth)

```
S = SIMPLE TO INSTALL AND USE
    │
    ├── Out-of-the-box experience is frictionless
    ├── Onboarding takes minutes, not hours
    ├── "The best value props are highly disruptive but non-disruptive to use"
    └── Example: Twilio API—developers love it because it's simple

L = LOW TO NO INITIAL COST
    │
    ├── Free to try (no credit card)
    ├── Freemium model
    ├── Free samples for physical products
    └── CAUTION: Free can devalue your product (use free trials)

I = INSTANT AND ONGOING VALUE
    │
    ├── Time to value is critical
    ├── Enterprise: sub-3 months
    ├── Self-proving value (analytics that show it works)
    └── Example: Pagos—onboard in a phone call, see data instantly

P = PLAYS WELL IN THE ECOSYSTEM
    │
    ├── Don't make customers change everything
    ├── Integrate with what they already use
    └── Example: Klaviyo became preferred SMS partner of Shopify
```

### The Three UPs Framework

```
UPDATE (Retention)
    │
    ├── Security fixes
    ├── Data refreshes
    ├── Bug fixes
    └── Customer keeps paying because product stays current

UPGRADE (Retention + Value Increase)
    │
    ├── New capabilities
    ├── New technologies (AI, mobile, etc.)
    ├── New features
    └── Customer pays more for more value

UPSELL (Value Increase)
    │
    ├── Additional products/services
    ├── Cross-sell to same customer
    ├── Enterprise tier
    └── Customer pays more for more products

THE MATH:
    └── Update + Upgrade + Upsell = Higher LTV (Lifetime Value)
    └── Higher LTV = Better business model
```

### The Whole Product Framework

```
YOUR PRODUCT IS A PIECE OF THE PUZZLE
    │
    └── You can't be successful alone
    └── Customer needs an end-to-end solution
    │
    ▼
IDENTIFY WHAT YOU'RE MISSING:
    │
    ├── Cloud provider
    ├── Storage
    ├── Models/Analytics
    ├── Application integration
    └── Distribution partners
    │
    ▼
FIND PARTNERS:
    │
    ├── They need you (you solve a problem for them)
    ├── You need them (they complete your solution)
    └── Win-win partnership
    │
    ▼
RESULT:
    └── You get to market faster
    └── You reduce costs (not building everything)
    └── You find customers through partners
```

### Co-Creation / Win-Win Framework

```
FIND A COMMUNITY:
    │
    ├── People who have the same problem you're solving
    ├── They want to solve it too
    └── They'll contribute if it's a win-win
    │
    ▼
OPEN SOURCE EXAMPLE (Drupal):
    │
    ├── Founder solved his own problem
    ├── Others contributed because they had the same problem
    ├── Tens of thousands of modules built by community
    └── Monetized via cloud services (convenience, security, scale)
    │
    ▼
THE RULE:
    └── It can't be just your need
    └── If you keep asking for help without giving value, it won't work
    └── Find the win-win
```

### Customer Journey Framework

```
CUSTOMER JOURNEY:
    │
    ├── 1. PROBLEM: Customer spends time/money figuring out solutions
    ├── 2. BUY: They purchase your product
    ├── 3. IMPLEMENT: They deploy it
    ├── 4. TRAIN: They learn to use it
    ├── 5. TEST: They verify it works
    ├── 6. ROLL OUT: They deploy at scale
    ├── 7. MEASURE: They track results
    ├── 8. OPTIMIZE: They improve usage
    └── 9. RENEW/UPGRADE: They keep buying

YOUR GOAL:
    └── Reduce friction at every step
    └── Increase LTV (Lifetime Value)
    └── Reduce CAC (Customer Acquisition Cost)
```

### The Business Model Impact on Valuation

```
A GREAT BUSINESS MODEL:
    │
    ├── Lower cash burn
    ├── Better valuations
    ├── Lower dilution
    ├── Higher ownership
    └── Bigger outcome for founders

WHY:
    │
    └── Investors pay for scalable, repeatable, profitable models
    └── A great business model makes you cash efficient
    └── Lower burn = less need to raise = less dilution
```

## Rules

### DO:
- Always start with the customer—customer-centric business models win
- Always find your core—one thing to monetize, not everything
- Always find your MVS—focus on one segment with consistent needs
- Always design for SLIP—Simple, Low cost, Instant value, Plays well
- Always reduce time to value—the faster they see value, the faster they buy
- Always think about the three UPs—Update, Upgrade, Upsell
- Always look for strategic partnerships—you are a piece of the puzzle
- Always find the win-win—co-creation requires mutual benefit

### DON'T:
- Never monetize everything—pick one core and build around it
- Never make customers change everything—integrate with what they use
- Never ignore the RSVP test—if it fails any component, go back
- Never skip the win-win—asking for help without giving value won't work

---

## Heuristics

- If you have to change too much to adopt it, it won't work
- The best value props are highly disruptive but non-disruptive to use
- Time to value under 3 months = easier enterprise sales
- Free can devalue your product—use free trials, not free forever
- You can't build the whole product alone—partner with others
- The customer's success is your success
- A great business model = better valuation, less dilution

## Triggers

### When You're Designing Your Business Model
→ Ask: "What is my core? (The one thing customers say 'because X')"
→ Ask: "Who is my MVS? (Minimum Viable Segment)"
→ Ask: "Is my product SLIP? (Simple, Low cost, Instant value, Plays well)"
→ If you can't answer these, your business model isn't customer-centric

### When You're Pricing Your Product
→ Ask: "Is there a free trial option?"
→ Ask: "Can I walk customers up the value chain?"
→ Ask: "What are my three UPs? (Update, Upgrade, Upsell)"
→ If you only have one price point, you're leaving money on the table

### When You're Considering a Partnership
→ Ask: "Is this a win-win?"
→ Ask: "Do they need me as much as I need them?"
→ Ask: "Does this complete my 'whole product'?"
→ If it's not a win-win, it won't work

### When You're Evaluating Your Business Model
→ Ask: "Is it Repeatable?"
→ Ask: "Is it Scalable?"
→ Ask: "Is it Valuable?"
→ Ask: "Is it Profitable/Defensible?"
→ If it fails any of these, go back to the drawing board

## Failure Modes

### Common Mistakes

1. **Not finding your core**
   - Trying to monetize everything
   - No clear "because X" reason for customers

2. **Not finding your MVS**
   - Trying to serve too many segments at once
   - Energy gets dissipated

3. **Not designing for SLIP**
   - Product is complex to install/use
   - High initial cost
   - No instant value
   - Doesn't play well with others

4. **Time to value too long**
   - Customers don't see value quickly enough
   - They churn before they get the benefit

5. **Not thinking about the three UPs**
   - No update strategy (product becomes outdated)
   - No upgrade path (can't increase value)
   - No upsell opportunities (leaving money on the table)

6. **Trying to build the whole product alone**
   - No partnerships
   - Building everything yourself
   - Missing the ecosystem

7. **Not finding the win-win**
   - Asking for help without giving value
   - Communities won't engage

### WSJ — The Subscription Retention Framework

```
THE DISCOUNT TRAP:
├── Customers sign up for promotional rates
├── Price normalization causes shock
├── 90% cancel within a year
└── "American consumers don't like dramatic change in prices"

HELLOFRESH vs. BLUE APRON:
├── HelloFresh (78% market share): Consistently profitable
├── Blue Apron (6% market share): Steadily declined
├── "Once you establish yourself as the leader, it becomes very, very hard to be disrupted"
│
THE SOLUTION:
├── Gradual price increases (no dramatic changes)
├── Higher initial price + greater value = lower churn
├── Premium positioning = right customers
│
HELLOFRESH ECONOMICS:
├── Takes 6 months to earn back $1 spent on acquisition
├── Customer acquisition cost payback takes time
└── Leader advantage is real

LESSON: Don't over-discount to acquire customers
```

### WSJ — The Premium Business Model Framework

```
METHODOLOGY (MEAL KITS):
├── $17-$30/meal
├── No heavy discounts
├── Profitable from year two
├── Target: Time-starved, high-income (several hundred thousand/year)
├── "Our sweet spot is men who live on takeout 3-4x/week spending $35/meal"

SWEETGREEN:
├── Premium positioning: Tech company, not just restaurant

### WSJ — The Efficiency Creates Pricing Power Framework

```
ALDI:
├── Smaller store footprint (12,000 sq ft vs. 31,000 sq ft average)
├── Fewer products (1,600 vs. 31,000 at supermarkets)
├── Limited staff (3-5 on floor vs. 10+)
├── Products displayed in shipping boxes
│
├── THE SIGNALS:
│   ├── Cart deposit: "This store saves money on labor"
│   ├── Products in boxes: "They don't waste money on fancy displays"
│   ├── Limited selection: "They buy in bulk for lower prices"
│   └── Result: Customer infers low price = efficiency, not low quality
│
├── THE RESULT:
│   ├── 96% value for dollar rating (highest)
│   ├── Store count growing ~100/year
│   └── Thrives during recessions
│
LESSON: Operational efficiency creates pricing power
```

```
CAVA:

LESSON: Acquisition is a growth model, not just an exit strategy
