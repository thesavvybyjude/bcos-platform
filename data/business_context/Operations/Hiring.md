# Hiring.md

## Purpose
To provide frameworks for hiring high-quality remote talent.

## Core Philosophy
**A players hire A players.**

- Culture is determined by who you hire
- Hire for passion, not just experience
- Users make great employees
- Maintain high bar always

## Principles

**1. Hire Users**
- They love the product
- They know what works
- They want to improve it
- Built-in alignment

**2. Passion Over Experience**
- Scrappiness > Credentials
- Drive > Pedigree
- Fit > Resume
- Potential > Past

**3. Maintain High Bar**
- Every hire matters
- Bad hires destroy culture
- A players want A players
- Good hiring compounds

**4. Ask the Right Interview Question**
- "Name three brands you love following on digital and why"
- If they can't answer, they're not right
- You need people who get culture
- This applies to every role

**5. Don't Hire From Corporations (Early)**
- They're brainwashed by slow pace
- They don't understand startup speed
- They don't know what's happening now
- Experience can be a liability

**6. Hire Your Consumer**
- Your team should look like your avatar
- They are your consumer
- They understand the brand
- They are your incubation lab

## Core Philosophy Addendum
**Hire entrepreneurs, not employees.**

## ADDITION: Selfishly Aligned

**Core Philosophy Addendum 2:**
**People are selfish. Align that selfishness with your company.**

### Additional Principles:

**7. Be Honest About Selfishness**
- People want careers, money, growth
- They don't wake up thinking about you
- That's okay and honest
- Find the overlap

**8. Selfish Alignment**
```
Individual's Selfish Goals:
  │
  ├─ Career growth
  ├─ Money
  ├─ Learning
  └─ Lifestyle
  │
  ↓
  │
Company's Goals:
  │
  ├─ Growth
  ├─ Revenue
  ├─ Market share
  └─ Innovation
  │
  ↓
  │
Overlap = Great hire
```

**9. Missionaries vs Mercenaries**
- Missionaries: Aligned with mission
- Mercenaries: Just collecting a paycheck
- You want missionaries
- But be honest about their selfishness

**10. The Venn Diagram Test**
- When hiring: Where's the overlap?
- If minimal: Wrong hire
- If significant: Great hire
- Continuous conversation

### Updated Hiring Framework

**Interview Question:**
- "What do you want with your life and career?"
- Not "Why do you want this job?"
- Honest conversation about selfishness
- Find the overlap

### Updated Rules

**Always:**
- Have honest conversations about goals
- Find the overlap between selfishness and company
- Hire missionaries, not mercenaries
- Keep the conversation ongoing

**Never:**
- Pretend people aren't selfish
- Ignore individual goals
- Hire mercenaries
- Let alignment drift

## ADDITION: Extraordinary Question

**Core Philosophy Addendum:**
**A players aren't looking for balance—they're looking to build.**

### Additional Principles:

**11. The Extraordinary Question**
- "Tell me about something you've done that's extraordinary"
- Their answer tells you everything
- If it's not extraordinary, they're not extraordinary
- This is the best filter

**12. Values-Based Hiring**
- All-in mentality (not work-life balance obsessed)
- Extreme ownership
- High craftsmanship
- "Get the [  ] out of here" to B players

**13. The Challenge Test**
- Give candidates a challenge
- Mimics what they'd do in role
- If they use ChatGPT poorly, they're out
- If they impress, they're in

### Updated Hiring Framework

**The Extraordinary Question in Practice:**
```
Interview Question:
"Tell me about something you've done that's extraordinary."

Good Answer:
"Built a product in college that got 1,000 customers."

Bad Answer:
"I sent an email to someone famous."

Great Answer:
"Started a business that scaled to X revenue in Y months."

=> The answer reveals their ambition and capability.
```

### Updated Rules

**Always:**
- Ask the extraordinary question
- Test with challenges
- Hire A players only
- Be strict on values

**Never:**
- Hire B players
- Skip the challenge
- Ignore values
- Let mediocrity in

## ADDITION: Good vs Bad Role Documents

**Core Philosophy Addendum:**
**Tell people what good looks like and what bad looks like.**

### Additional Principles:

**14. Good vs Bad Role Documents**
- Tell people what it looks like to be good
- Tell people what it looks like to be bad
- Aspirational identity + boundaries
- A16Z/Ben Horowitz approach

**15. The Framework**
```
Good:
  → Meets expectations consistently
  → Takes ownership
  → Delivers on time
  → Communicates proactively

Bad:
  → Misses deadlines repeatedly
  → Avoids ownership
  → Needs constant direction
  → Communicates only when forced
```

### Updated Rules

**Always:**
- Document good vs bad for every role
- Be specific about expectations
- Use aspirational identity
- Set clear boundaries

**Never:**
- Hire without clear role docs
- Be vague about expectations
- Skip the documentation
- Assume people know what good looks like

## ADDITION: The Who Method

**Core Philosophy Addendum:**
**Hire for who they are, not what they've done.**

### Additional Principles:

**16. The Who Interview**
- "Who are you when no one is watching?"
- "What do you do when you're struggling?"
- "What do you do when you're winning?"
- The answer reveals character

**17. Fit Interviews**
- Don't just test skills
- Test for cultural fit
- Test for values alignment
- Test for how they handle adversity

**18. The Reference Check**
- Talk to former managers
- Ask: "Would you hire them again?"
- Ask: "What's their biggest weakness?"
- Look for honesty in the answer

### Updated Decision Framework

```
Who Should We Hire?
  │
  ├─ Who are they when no one is watching?
  │   ├─ Good → Continue
  │   └─ Bad → Pass
  │
  ├─ Do they fit our culture?
  │   ├─ YES → Continue
  │   └─ NO → Pass
  │
  ├─ Do they have the right values?
  │   ├─ YES → Continue
  │   └─ NO → Pass
  │
  └─ Would their former manager hire them again?
      ├─ YES → Hire
      └─ NO → Pass
```

### Updated Rules

**Always:**
- Ask the extraordinary question
- Test with challenges
- Hire A players only
- Be strict on values
- Use The Who method
- Test for fit, not just skills
- Check references deeply
- Hire a content operator when at scale
- Clone yourself into them
- Give them a curriculum
- Yes/no them 100+ times

**Never:**
- Hire B players
- Skip the challenge
- Ignore values
- Let mediocrity in
- Hire based on resume alone
- Skip the Who interview
- Let ideation be a bottomless pit
- Try to clone someone without a curriculum
- Underestimate the 3-6 month investment
- Think AI can fully replace this role

## ADDITION: The Content Operator Role

**Core Philosophy Addendum:**
**The Content Operator is the quarterback of your content process.**

### Additional Principles:

**19. The Content Operator Role**
- Finds ideas
- Researches
- Pitches ideas like a showrunner
- Frees you from the bottomless pit of ideation
- Takes 3-6 months to clone yourself into them

**20. How to Clone Yourself**
```
1. Install your brain (3-6 months)
2. Define what a good idea is
3. Define how to filter ideas
4. Define what makes a good idea in your niche
5. Create a curriculum (180-page Google doc)
6. Have them watch everything you've made
7. Live calls and Looms
8. Yes/no feedback cycle
9. They become 90% as good as you
```

**21. The Idea Pipeline**
- Other creators in the niche
- Twitter
- Reddit
- YouTube
- Newsletters
- Each niche has different pipes

**22. The Content Operator Workflow**
1. Scans idea pipes
2. Finds good ideas
3. Creates shortlist
4. Develops ideas (finds visuals, angle)
5. Pitches to you
6. You approve
7. You write script
8. Record
9. Hand to editors

### Updated Hiring Framework

**Where to Find a Content Operator:**
- Offshore (harder to train)
- Domestic (easier, more expensive)
- Someone who's been doing this type of thing
- Doesn't want to do it alone

**Never:**

- Think you can do it all

**Always:**
- Hire for passion
- Look for scrappiness
- Maintain high bar
- Trust team feedback
- Hire entrepreneurs
- Test culture understanding
- Hire your avatar
- Look for hunger

**Never:**
- Settle for average

- Rush the process

## ADDITION: Curiosity as the #1 Trait

**Core Philosophy Addendum:**
**Curiosity is the most important trait in a hire.**

### Additional Principles:

**23. Curiosity Is Non-Negotiable**
- Skills fade, curiosity compounds
- The most valuable employees are the ones who ask questions
- Hire for curiosity, train for skills
- A curious person will figure it out

**24. How to Assess Curiosity**
- Do they ask questions in the interview?
- Do they show genuine interest in the business?
- Do they want to understand the why behind things?
- Did they research the company before they came?

**25. The Cost of Hiring Without Curiosity**
- They will do only what they are told
- They will not improve processes
- They will not find better ways
- They will hold the company back

### Updated Rules

**Always:**
- Hire for curiosity
- Look for good questions
- Value learning over credentials
- Curiosity compounds

**Never:**
- Hire someone who does not ask questions
- Value skills over curiosity
- Think curiosity is not important
- Hire average curiosity
