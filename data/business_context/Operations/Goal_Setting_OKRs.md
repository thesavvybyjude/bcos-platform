# Goal Setting, OKRs & Operating Rhythm

## Purpose
To provide frameworks for setting goals, measuring progress, and creating operational consistency through OKRs, KPIs, and recurring meetings.

## Core Philosophy

> "If you have more than three true priorities, you have no priorities."

> "Measure what matters."

## Principles

### 1. OKRs + KPIs = Goal Framework
- OKRs (Objectives & Key Results): What you want to achieve
- KPIs (Key Performance Indicators): Measurement of success
- Annual strategic initiatives → Leadership OKRs → Team KPIs

### 2. Stretch Goals vs. Baseline Goals
- Stretch goals: Top-down, big new launches, new categories
- Baseline goals: Bottom-up, existing distribution, year-over-year growth
- Need both: "Magic + Data" = Product market fit

### 3. Green Box / Red Box Visualization
- Monthly reviews of all OKRs/KPIs
- Green = Working; Red = Not working
- 2+ months of red = Need to fix it
- 2+ months of green = Increase the goals

### 4. One Person Owns Each OKR
- Every ELT member owns specific OKRs
- Accountability is clear
- Cross-functional coordination happens through CEO/CFO

### 5. The Innovation Index
- Measure: % of trailing 12-month sales from new products
- Too high = Not enough cash cows
- Too low = Brand isn't exciting enough
- Tells you if you're investing enough in product development

### 6. Product Lifecycle Math
- 3-5 year product life cycle (consumer electronics)
- Chop that into quarters
- Need 20-30% of products as new introductions
- If you only have 1 new product coming, you're late

## Decision Frameworks

### OKR Cascading Framework

```
ANNUAL STRATEGIC INITIATIVES (Top-down)
├── Directly tied to financial objectives
├── Top-down stretch goals
└── Leadership team creates these together
    │
    ▼
ELT MEMBER OKRs
├── Each ELT member has specific OKRs
├── Tied to strategic initiatives
└── Example: Head of Ecom → Grow DTC sales, launch loyalty program
    │
    ▼
TEAM KPIs
├── Measurement of success behind OKRs
├── Example: Launch loyalty program by X date
└── Goal: % of retention, % of sales from repeat purchasers
```

### Product Lifecycle Framework

```
PRODUCT CATEGORIES

1. NEW PRODUCT INTRODUCTIONS (Highest investment)
   └── Development + Marketing investment

2. GROWTH PRODUCTS
   └── Marketing investment, low development

3. CASH COWS (Lowest investment)
   └── Minimal marketing, minimal development
   └── Example: Skullcandy Crusher Evo (black t-shirt product)
   └── Goal: Make these evergreen; fund new product development

4. END OF LIFE (EOL)
   └── Discount and remove from cycle

PRODUCT LIFE CYCLE MATH:
- 3-5 years for consumer electronics
- Chop into 4 quarters
- Need 20-30% as new products
- If you have 10 products lasting 2 years → need 2 new products every 2 years
- If you only have 1 → you're late; rush to market
```

### Innovation Index Decision Framework

```
IF Innovation Index is:

TOO LOW (< threshold)
└── Brand isn't exciting enough
    └── Invest more in new product development

TOO HIGH (> threshold)
└── Not enough cash cows
    └── Need more evergreen products for margin/consistency

OPTIMAL (20-30%)
└── Brand is exciting; cash cows are funding innovation
```

### Green Box / Red Box Review Process

```
For each OKR/KPI, evaluate:

GREEN BOX (Working)
├── What's driving success?
├── Can we replicate this elsewhere?
└── If green for 2+ months → Increase the goal

RED BOX (Not Working)
├── What's causing the problem?
├── People issue? Process issue? External issue?
├── If red for 2+ months → Need to fix it
└── What resources are needed?
```

## Rules

- **Always cascade OKRs from annual initiatives** — Every team KPI should ladder up
- **Always have one owner per OKR** — Accountability is non-negotiable
- **Always do green box/red box reviews monthly** — If it's red 2+ months, fix it
- **Always measure the innovation index** — Tells you if you're investing enough
- **Always under-promise, over-deliver** — Trust is the currency
- **Never have more than 3 true priorities** — Everything else is a distraction

## Heuristics

- If 2 months of red → trend = problem. Fix it.
- If 2 months of green → increase the goal. Push harder.
- 20-30% new products in the mix is optimal (consumer goods)
- Math > intuition for product lifecycle

## Triggers

### When Setting Annual Goals:

```
Annual planning session

→ Stretch goals: Top-down (big launches, new categories)
→ Baseline goals: Bottom-up (existing distribution, year-over-year growth)
→ Combine for "Magic + Data" forecast

If you can't find data to support it, use magic (product market fit intuition).
If demand is higher than expected, spend on supply, not marketing.
```

### When Evaluating Product Pipeline:

```
Looking at product roadmap

→ Ask: "How many products are in each stage?"
→ New: ___ Growth: ___ Cash: ___ EOL: ___

If you have <20% in New stage:
→ You're not investing enough; brand will get stale

If you have >30% in New stage:
→ You're not producing enough cash cows; margins will suffer
```
