# Amazon_Supply_Chain.md

## Purpose
To provide frameworks for complex supply chains (using Amazon acai as example).

## Core Philosophy
**The hardest supply chains create the strongest moats.**

- If it's hard, few will do it
- Native sourcing is unique
- Logistics complexity is a barrier
- Vertical integration is essential

## Principles

**1. Sourcing Must Be Native**
- Acai only grows in Amazon region
- 99% from Brazil
- Cannot be planted elsewhere with same quality
- Native = Unique advantage

**2. Time Is Critical**
- Acai goes bad in 4-8 hours after harvest
- Transport by river is only option
- Factory must be in the forest
- Processing must be immediate

**3. Community Partnership Is Essential**
- Local cooperatives harvest
- Certification ensures quality
- Social impact is real
- Sustainable supply is guaranteed

## Process

**Amazon Supply Chain:**
```
1. Native acai harvested by local cooperatives
2. Transported by river (4-8 hour window)
3. Arrives at factory in Amazon forest
4. Processed into 50kg frozen fruit bars
5. Final product: 7kg frozen buckets
6. Shipped globally to stores
7. Store machine gives final texture
```

## Decision Framework

```
Should I Own My Supply Chain?
  |
  |- Is my product unique/hard to source?
  |   |- YES → Need control
  |   |- NO → Can outsource
  |
  |- Do I have the capital?
  |   |- YES → Verticalize
  |   |- NO → Partner
  |
  |- Is quality at risk?
  |   |- YES → Control it
  |   |- NO → Third-party is fine
  |
  |- Can I scale with third-party?
      |- YES → Keep outsourcing
      |- NO → Need to own
```

## Rules

**Always:**
- Own your IP
- Control quality
- Build partnerships with suppliers
- Maintain supply chain security

**Never:**
- Let third parties control your quality
- Underestimate complexity
- Lose IP control
- Ignore supply chain risk
