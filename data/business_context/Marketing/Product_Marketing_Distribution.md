# Product, Marketing & Distribution Strategy

## Purpose
To provide frameworks for building products, positioning brands, managing marketing mix, and scaling distribution channels.

## Core Philosophy

> "Completely forget everything you just heard. There is always opportunity and uncertainty. Take a step back, look at the holistic market, understand where there could be opportunities, and then get creative."

## Principles

### 1. Product is the Marketing
- Great product does the marketing for you
- Early adopters → Reviews → Word of mouth
- CAC drops; reach accelerates

### 2. Consumer Understanding is Everything
- Demographics: Age, location, income, what they buy, price points
- Psychographics: What makes them them (fashion, music, colors, features)
- Balance data (rearview mirror) + culture (future-facing)

### 3. Brand Positioning: Challenger Brand
- The biggest brands are "vanilla ice cream" (appeal to everyone)
- Challenger brands need to be specific and committed
- Win a small, loyal group; they inspire the rest

### 4. Core → Participant → Fan Model
- Core: The bullseye (most important) - active youth, board sports
- Participant: Inspired by core, participates in the lifestyle
- Fan: Inspired by core and participants, consumes the culture
- Spend brand dollars on core, conversion dollars on fans

### 5. The Innovation Index
- Measure new product sales as % of trailing 12-month sales
- Critical metric for brand health

### 6. 1+1 = 3 Partnerships
- Organic fit: Shared values
- Audience: Complimentary, not identical (you don't want to talk to yourself)
- Product: Creates something exciting

### 7. Go Where the Puck is Going
- Understand TAM (Total Addressable Market) and CAGR (Compound Annual Growth Rate)
- Growing category > Shrinking category
- Category of business matters: Is it growing or shrinking?

### 8. Brand Perception vs. Positioning
- Position how you want to be perceived
- Perception is what consumers actually think
- It takes years to fix perception

```
       CORE (Bullseye)
    ┌─────────────────────────┐
    │   Active Youth          │
    │   Board Sports          │
    │   12-25 years old       │
    │   Urban/Suburban        │
    │   Fashion-forward       │
    │   Music-focused         │
    └─────────────────────────┘
           │
    ┌──────▼──────┐
    │  PARTICIPANT │
    │  (Inspired)  │
    │  Activates around core│
    └──────┬──────┘
           │
    ┌──────▼──────┐
    │     FAN     │
    │  (Consumes) │
    │  Inspired by culture   │
    └─────────────┘

SPEND:
- Core: Brand dollars (support culture, athletes, events)
- Participant: Brand + conversion mix

THE MATH:
- Core market: ~12M people (board sports)
- Total TAM: ~1.5B people globally
- Win the core → The rest follow → Win the TAM
```

### Total Addressable Market (TAM) Framework

```
TAM (Total Addressable Market)
└── 1.5B people who should buy Skullcandy

Core Market (Board Sports)
└── 12M people

If we win the 12M core:
└── Others are inspired → Win the 1.488B others
└── Less marketing spend needed

Goal: Win the core → Inspire the participants → Convert the fans

Key insight: Do a really good job with specific consumers and you win
a dedicated, loyal group. They inspire the rest.
```

### Product Lifecycle + Innovation Index Framework

```
PRODUCT MIX OPTIMAL:
├── 20-30% New Products (driving excitement)
├── 20-30% Growth Products (scaling)
├── 20-30% Cash Cows (funding new products)
└── Remainder EOL (phasing out)

INNOVATION INDEX:
└── % of TTM sales from products launched in last 12 months

IF TOO LOW (<20%):
└── Brand isn't exciting → Invest more in new product development

IF TOO HIGH (>30%):
└── Not enough cash cows → Need more evergreen products for margin

CORRECT RANGE: 20-30%

The innovation index is a leading indicator of brand health.
```

### Challenger Brand Positioning Framework

```
BIGGEST BRANDS (Apple, Samsung)
├── Unlimited resource
├── Mass appeal ("vanilla ice cream")
└── Can't be everything to everyone

CHALLENGER BRAND (Skullcandy)
├── Limited resource
├── Specific and committed
├── Win a small, dedicated group
└── They inspire the rest

GOAL: Differentiate clearly
├── Product: Features, benefits, style
├── Price: Accessible vs. premium
└── Promotion: Athletes, influencers, events

POSITIONING vs. PERCEPTION:
├── Position: How you want to be perceived
├── Perception: What consumers actually think
└── If there's a gap → You have work to do
```

### Partnership Fit Framework

```
POTENTIAL PARTNER
├── ORGANIC FIT
│   └── Shared values?
│   ├── YES → Proceed
│   └── NO → Reject
│
├── AUDIENCE
│   └── Complimentary audience?
│   ├── YES (different audience) → Proceed
│   └── NO (same audience) → Reject (why talk to yourself?)
│
└── PRODUCT
    └── 1+1 = 3?
    ├── YES (creates something exciting) → Proceed
    └── NO → Reject

KEY: "It's got to be two things. Organic fit and audience."
```

### Market Growth Evaluation Framework

```
CATEGORY ANALYSIS
├── Is the category growing or shrinking?
│   ├── GROWING (e.g., AI)
│   │   └── Show up and you can grow with tailwinds
│   └── SHRINKING (e.g., corded telephones)
│       └── Good luck; headwinds are strong
│
└── CAGR (Compound Annual Growth Rate)

Example:
└── Category size: $100 → $105 (5% growth)
└── Your goal: 20% growth
└── You must steal share from competitors
└── Know what everyone else is doing
```

## Rules

- **Always understand your consumer** — Demographics + Psychographics
- **Always balance data + culture** — Rearview mirror data + future-facing culture
- **Always define your core first** — Win the core; the rest follows
- **Always differentiate** — If you're not different, you're not memorable
- **Always have a product pipeline** — If you only have 1 new product, you're late
- **Always know your category's growth rate** — Growing category > shrinking category
- **Never chase butterflies** — Stay committed to the process

## Heuristics

- If 70% of market is earbuds and 30% headphones, but headphones are growing → Invest in headphones
- Brand awareness costs millions; reviving a brand is easier than starting one
- Great product + undeniable offer = winner
- Sell-in is only half the battle; sell-through is the other half
- Under-promise, over-deliver applies to products too

## Triggers

### When Deciding What Products to Develop:

```
Considering new product development

→ Ask: "What does the data say is growing?"
→ Ask: "What does our culture say our community wants?"
→ Data tells you the past; culture tells you the future

Example: If 70% of market is earbuds, 30% headphones, but headphones are growing:
→ Invest in headphones (the market is swinging that direction)
```

### When You're a Small Brand Getting a Big PO:

```
Small brand gets $5M PO from a giant customer

→ This will throw you into a tailspin
→ You need cash to build inventory (you'll need access to capital)
→ You're creating more inventory than you ever have
→ Must sell through or you'll be in a world of hurt 6-12 months from now

What to do:
1. Understand your cash position
2. Can you finance the inventory?
3. Do you have the sell-through plan?
4. If not, don't take the deal

Cash + Inventory = Two most important things.
```

### When Choosing Distribution Channels:

```
Prioritizing sales channels

→ Start with where your core consumer shops
→ Then expand outward

Skullcandy Example:
1. Direct-to-consumer + Specialty retail (core consumer)
2. Premium consumer electronics (Best Buy, Amazon, Walmart)
3. Travel channel (airports)
4. Mobility channel (Sprint, AT&T)
5. Club stores (Costco, Sam's Club)

Closest to the pin → Further away
```

## Retail Entry Framework (Added by Manny Lubin)

### The Retail Entry Framework

```
START SMALL:
├── Whole Foods North Atlantic (first account)
├── Harris Teeter
├── Central Market (14 stores in Texas)
└── Build a case study

EXPAND:
├── Take the case study to the next retailer
├── Bigger case study → next retailer
└── Bigger case study → next retailer

THE PITCH:
└── "This brand is doing 2 units per SKU per week, I'm doing 7."
└── "You should take mine."

THE REALITY:
└── Buyers are taking someone else out to put you in
└── They need conviction or to be convinced

THE DATA:
└── If you can't show data, you're asking for a leap of faith
└── Once you have data, you can sell in

HOW TO WIN RETAIL:
├── Start with 5 stores, win, then go bigger
├── Show velocity and sell-through data
├── Build relationships with store managers
└── Be patient — retail is a long game
```

## Distribution Strategy (Added by D Murphy)

### The "Be Everywhere" Framework

```
THE OLD MODEL:
+-- Win on price or brand

THE NEW MODEL:
+-- Brand + Distribution = Win

THE DATA:
+-- Temu and Shein changed American retail
+-- They're both profitable
+-- They're marketplaces anyone can sell on

THE MISTAKE NIKE MADE:
+-- Shifted from wholesale to DTC
+-- Closed accounts at Macy's, DSW
+-- Thought people would come to their site
+-- They didn't

THE LESSON:
+-- Brands need to be everywhere
+-- You don't know where every consumer is
+-- DTC alone cannot build a billion-dollar business today

THE WINNERS:
+-- Ralph Lauren, Michael Kors, Tommy Hilfiger
+-- Sell everywhere: DTC, Amazon, wholesale, Costco, Sam's Club
```

### The Ghost Marketplace Framework

```
THE PROBLEM:
+-- Brands have excess inventory
+-- Off-price is opaque (TJ Maxx, Burlington, Ross)
+-- Recovery rates vary wildly ($2-$5 for a $25 shirt)

THE SOLUTION:
+-- Private B2B marketplace
+-- Sellers control where product goes (restrictions)
+-- Transparency on pricing
+-- Aggregated small buyers (bigger than TJ Maxx)

THE DEMOCRATIZATION:
+-- Small businesses never had access to this supply
+-- Now they can buy direct from brands
+-- 18-year-old students can build resale businesses

THE FUTURE VISION:
+-- Flip the switch → Global distribution
+-- "You cannot flip a switch today and have global distribution"
+-- Ghost is building the infrastructure
```
