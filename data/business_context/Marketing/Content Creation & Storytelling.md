# Content Creation & Storytelling

The framework for creating compelling content that captures attention, builds trust, and drives action.

## Purpose

This module covers the principles and processes behind effective content creation — from hook engineering and format selection to narrative structure and audience resonance. It applies to video, written, and visual content across all channels.

## Principles

1. **Hook in 3 Seconds** — Every piece of content must earn attention in the first 3 seconds. Open with a pattern interrupt, provocative question, or bold claim. If the hook fails, nothing else matters.

2. **One Idea Per Piece** — Effective content communicates a single clear idea. Multiple competing messages dilute impact. Ask: "If the audience remembers one thing, what should it be?"

3. **Story > Information** — People remember stories 22x better than facts alone. Wrap every insight in a narrative with characters, conflict, and resolution.

4. **Tension Creates Attention** — The gap between "what is" and "what could be" drives engagement. Create and sustain tension before resolving it. Don't give the answer too early.

5. **Format Follows Platform** — A YouTube video, an Instagram reel, and a LinkedIn post require fundamentally different structures. Never repurpose content without reformatting for the platform's native behaviour.

6. **Consistency Compounds** — A good piece published on schedule beats a perfect piece published whenever. Regularity builds audience expectation and algorithmic favour.

7. **Authenticity Over Polish** — Audiences reward genuine voice over production quality. A rough video with real insight outperforms a polished video with empty platitudes.

## Rules

**Always:** Test at least 3 hooks for every piece of content. The hook determines 80% of performance.

**Always:** Include a clear call to action. Every piece should drive the audience to a next step.

**Never:** Start with context. Open with the most compelling moment, then backfill context as needed.

**Never:** Use jargon without defining it. Assume the audience is smart but uninformed.

**Avoid:** Content calendars longer than 2 weeks. They kill responsiveness to trends and real-time opportunities.

**Prefer:** Batch creation over daily creation. Produce 5 pieces in one session rather than 1 per day.

## Decision Frameworks

### Format Selection

If the topic requires demonstration or visual proof:
- Then: Video format (short-form for awareness, long-form for depth).
- Otherwise: If the topic is analytical or data-heavy → written/carousel format.

### Hook Type Selector

If the audience is unfamiliar with the topic:
- Then: Use a "surprising fact" or "myth-busting" hook to create curiosity.
- Otherwise: If the audience is expert-level → use a "contrarian take" or "advanced technique" hook.
