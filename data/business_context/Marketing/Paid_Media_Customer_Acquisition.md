# Paid Media & Customer Acquisition

## Purpose
To provide frameworks for acquiring customers profitably through paid media, understanding unit economics, and optimizing ad spend across platforms.

## Core Philosophy

> "The narratives and stories being told within your ad account is more important than the audiences in which you're choosing to tell this to."

> "If no one knows you exist, you have to spend money."

## Principles

### 1. Understand Your LTV Before Setting CAC Goals
- You can spend more to acquire if LTV is high
- Subscription products justify higher CAC than one-time purchases
- Example: Breeze's goal CAC is $85-90, but can spend $120-130

### 2. First Purchase Profitability vs. LTV Play
- Some brands need to be first-order profitable to survive
- If you have subscription or high LTV, you can accept initial losses

### 3. The Smoke Test
- Start with the cheapest creative (images) to test value props
- Test angles before investing in video production
- "I need to understand what is the reason why someone's even going to click"

### 4. Testing is the Same at Any Budget
- The difference between $10K and $1K is speed, not outcome
- You'll learn the same things; it just takes longer with less budget

### 5. Creative is the New Targeting
- Meta's algorithm prioritizes story over audience selection
- If you have creative of people in Florida, it performs better in Florida
- The "Power Five" movement is doubling down on creative, not targeting

### 6. The Death of the Traditional Media Buyer
- Media buyers who think they control the ad account are obsolete
- Media buyers must become creative strategists
- Structure: Media buyer = Creative strategist + Designer support

### 7. Organic Activity Directly Helps Paid Performance
- When you don't post organically, ad account performance declines
- Organic content creates trust; paid ads are perceived differently

### 8. The 70/30 Rule
- 70% of budget on proven winners
- 30% on testing new things
- When company is struggling: 90/10 or 100/0

### 9. The Three-Tiered Funnel (Added by Manny Lubin)
- Upper Funnel: Brand awareness, reach, top-of-mind
- Mid Funnel: Value prop, education, consideration
- Bottom Funnel: Retargeting, conversion, closing
- Each tier has different metrics and creative needs
- Don't just measure on ROAS — brand awareness builds long-term equity

### 10. The Field Sales Advantage (Added by Manny Lubin)
- Direct relationships with retailers
- Understanding of local markets
- Ability to execute in-store
- CPG: In-store is king — demos, sampling, in-store promotions
- "There's no better way than someone going in there and being friends with the store manager"
- Data + relationships = winning combination

## Decision Frameworks

### CAC/LTV Framework

```
FOR SUBSCRIPTION/HIGH LTV PRODUCTS:
└── Accept break-even or loss on first purchase
    └── You'll make it back on retention

FOR ONE-TIME PURCHASE PRODUCTS:
└── Must be first-order profitable
    └── No LTV to make up for acquisition loss

CAC GOAL EXAMPLE (Breeze):
├── Target CAC: $85-90
├── Max CAC: $120-130 (if needed)
└── Based on: LTV + retention predictability
```

### Smoke Test Framework

```
START WITH IMAGES (Cheapest)
├── Product on white with text callout
├── Test different value props:
│   ├── "Reduce anxiety"
│   ├── "Increase focus"
│   └── "Stress relief"
└── Same image, different copy → See what gets clicks

THEN MOVE TO VIDEO (Based on data)
├── Make video explaining the winning angle
├── Test men vs. women in the video
├── Test hands/body vs. full faces
│   └── (People see themselves in hands/body more easily)
└── Continue expanding on what works

WHY PEOPLE DON'T BUY:
├── Cost-per-click (CPC) too high? → Creative or audience issue
├── Getting to cart but abandoning? → Price is wrong
└── Solution: "Playfulness of price point or product"
```

### Ad Account Structure Framework (2025)

```
OLD STRUCTURE (Segmented Funnels):
├── Top of Funnel: Prospecting (new customers only)
├── Middle of Funnel: Value prop driven (attribution)
└── Bottom of Funnel: "Don't forget us" (discounts)

NEW STRUCTURE (Trust the Algorithm):
└── Organized by ASSETS, CREATIVE, ANGLES, STORIES
    └── Everything under one consistent story
    └── Folder structure: Product → Value Prop → All Assets

WHY:
└── Meta's algorithm is now good enough to serve the right ad
└── We can't control targeting anyway
└── Spend time on telling convincing stories, not audience segmentation
```

### Budget Allocation Framework

```
HEALTHY COMPANY:
├── 70%: Proven winners (what's working)
└── 30%: Testing (new angles, new creatives)

STRUGGLING COMPANY:
├── 90%: Winners
├── 10%: Testing
└── Or 100/0 (all winners until stable)

CYCLICAL PERFORMANCE:
└── Know your peak days (e.g., Friday-Sunday for Breeze)
└── Don't blow budget on low-performing days
└── Save spend for high-performing days
```

## Rules

- **Always understand your LTV before setting CAC goals** — If you don't, you'll spend wrong
- **Always start with images to smoke test** — Cheapest way to learn
- **Always test multiple value props** — You don't know which one resonates
- **Always test men AND women in creative** — People see themselves in the messenger
- **Always optimize creative for mobile 4x5/9x6** — That's where people are watching
- **Always have a 70/30 split** — Winners to testing
- **Never trust a media buyer who doesn't know the P&L** — They're guessing
- **Never let fear of spending stop you from learning** — You'll learn the same things slower

## Heuristics

- If CPMs are $35-45, you need to increase AOV to survive
- 75%+ of traffic is mobile → Design for mobile first
- Dark mode matters → Design for both light and dark
- Organic posting improves paid performance → Post every day

```
├── Proof ("We've done this for [similar companies]")
├── Guarantee/Value ("Can I send you a personalized video...")
└── Low friction ask ("If interested, just reply 'yes'")

VOLUME REQUIRED:
├── 100 emails is not enough
├── 1,000 emails might not be enough
├── 1% response rate = 100 responses from 10,000 emails
├── 10% convert = 10 conversations
└── 50% close = 5 deals
```

### Hormozi Lead Generation Framework — Content as Trust Builder

```
CONTENT'S ROLE:
├── Lubricant for all other lead gen efforts
├── Builds trust before you even reach out
├── Makes warm and cold outreach more effective
└── People need ~7 hours of content to truly trust you

THREE TYPES OF CONTENT:
├── Type 1: Short Form (60-90 sec, top of funnel, hooks)
├── Type 2: Long Form (30-60+ min, builds deep trust)
└── Type 3: Pre-Selling (addresses objections, answers questions)

REVIEW CYCLE:
├── Get customer → Deliver results → Ask for review
├── Get review → Leverage it everywhere
└── More reviews → More trust → More customers

DISPLAY REVIEWS EVERYWHERE:
├── Website (testimonial pages)
├── Social media (short form clips)
├── YouTube (long form case studies)
├── Sales presentations
├── Physical locations (floor to ceiling)
└── Email signatures
```
