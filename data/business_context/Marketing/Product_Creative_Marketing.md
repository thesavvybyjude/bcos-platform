# Product & Creative Marketing Strategy

## Purpose
To provide frameworks for building product-driven businesses, creating differentiated creative, and balancing brand vs. performance marketing.

## Core Philosophy

> "The greatest form of marketing for us is just giving it away. If you believe in your product, you should be able to give it to somebody and if they love it, they're going to buy it later."

> "The opener is the whole thing. The sharper you get on that, the more you're going to hook people in."

## Principles

### 1. Product is Number 1
- People think it's mostly marketing, but it's not
- The product really stands on its own
- If it was just any other generic product, you're not building a great brand

### 2. Start with What's Working
- Look at Amazon: what's selling, what has volume of reviews
- All the data is there: work backwards from what works
- Use the market to validate before you invest

### 3. The Opener is Everything
- Every ad needs 4-5 openers
- If you can't get them in the first 3 seconds, they'll miss the greatness of the ad
- What you think is funny may not resonate; test multiple options

### 4. Test Everything
- Iteration and finding winners within your existing content
- Test openers, test formats, test approaches
- One piece of creative, five different hooks, put dollars behind each, rocket fuel on the winner

### 5. The Three Creative Buckets
- Comedy/Entertainment (top of funnel)
- Value Prop/Side-by-Sides (middle of funnel)
- Direct Response/UGC (bottom of funnel)

### 6. Brand vs. DR Shift
- Moving away from DR and into brand territory
- Worst case: you move back to what's worked
- Brand is a bet on the greater good long-term

### 7. Creative First Company
- Let creatives do whatever they want
- Take big-time risk on creative
- If something is too over the top, pull it back and reiterate
- Not afraid to take risk: that's the big differentiator

### 8. "Fewer But Better" (Added by Manny Lubin)
- Luxury market operates on scarcity, not volume
- When you try to serve too many segments, you lose your soul
- True luxury is about constraint, precision, and emotional power
- Example: Nike with Jordan Brand — one athlete, one shoe, global domination
- Fewer, greater, better
- Fewer styles, fewer SKUs, but better quality and stronger brand equity

### 9. Focus Groups: The Underrated Weapon (Added by Manny Lubin)
- All great brands have them, but rarely talked about
- How to run one:
  - Go to a market, call local retailers (Whole Foods, Harris Teeter, Central Market)
  - Ask: "You got any customers you love? We'll take them to dinner."
  - Bring 15-20 people, buy dinner, ask questions
  - Show them product, ask for feedback, observe reactions
  - Give them credit (they usually spend more than the credit)
- Why it works: You don't need a thousand-person sample
  - 100 target demo customers saying the same thing = listen

## Decision Frameworks

### Product Development Framework

```
START WITH MARKET DATA:
├── Look at Amazon: what's selling, volume of reviews
├── Work backwards from what works
└── Price point and product type validated by market

BUILD THE BEST PRODUCT:
├── Fit (number one priority)
├── Comfort
├── Durability (washing, longevity)
└── Quality control

PRICE AFTER PRODUCT:
├── Not: "We need to hit this MSRP"
└── Yes: "What price works for our customer specifically?"

WHY:
└── If you build a great product, you can give it away
    and people will come back
```

### Creative Testing Framework

```
CREATE ONE PIECE OF CONTENT:
└── 4-5 different openers/hooks

TEST:
├── Put small budget behind each
├── Measure: Which gets engagement?
└── What resonates with the customer?

ROCKET FUEL:
└── Double down on the winner
└── More iterations of what works

REPEAT:
└── This is how you find winners
```

### Creative Buckets Framework

```
BUCKET 1: COMEDY/ENTERTAINMENT
├── Parodies, humor, feel-good
├── Top of funnel
├── Creates emotional connection
└── Example: Dodgeball parody

BUCKET 2: VALUE PROP/SIDE-BY-SIDES
├── Visual comparison of market vs. you
├── Middle of funnel
├── Shows why you're different
└── Example: Side-by-side t-shirt fit

BUCKET 3: DIRECT RESPONSE/UGC
├── In-your-face selling
├── Bottom of funnel
├── Straight DR
└── Example: UGC testimonials

IDEAL: People see comedy first (brand),
       then value prop (convince),
       then DR (convert)
REALITY: People see whatever, whenever
```

### Brand vs. Performance Shift Framework

```
CURRENT STATE:
└── 90% DR-focused, 10% Brand

TARGET STATE:
└── 50%+ Brand/Comedy, 50% DR

THE BET:
├── If you can get someone to laugh and create emotional connection
├── The website will convert them
├── You don't need the ad to convert them directly

WHY IT WORKS:
├── Emotional connection leads to better feeling about brand
├── When they walk by a store, they remember you
├── Great website CRO closes the deal
└── CRO does the selling; the ad just gets them there

WORST CASE SCENARIO:
└── Go back to what's worked (DR)
```

### Side-by-Side Framework

```
WHY:
└── Visually shows why your product is different

PROVEN: ROAS crushes on side-by-side ads

DOUBLEDOWN:
├── T-shirts to Underwear to Denim to Everything
└── Every collection page and PDP shows side-by-side

IMPACT:
└── Market vs. You
└── "Why we're so different"
```

## Rules

- **Always test 4-5 openers per ad** — The opener is everything
- **Always start with product** — Build the best product first; price comes after
- **Always use market data** — Amazon tells you what's working
- **Always let creatives take risk** — Creative first company
- **Always test side-by-sides** — They're the most compelling visual
- **Never let fear stop you from trying brand** — Worst case: go back to DR
- **Never change too many things at once** — Can't pinpoint what worked

## Heuristics

- If you can't get them in 3 seconds, they'll miss the greatness of the ad
- Side-by-sides are the most compelling visual
- Brand is a bet on the greater good long-term
- Comedy works because nostalgia works

## Triggers

### When Creating an Ad:

```
New ad concept

→ Create 4-5 different openers
→ Test each with small budget
→ See what resonates
→ Double down on winner

If it's a comedy ad: Test 6 different openers
If it's DR: Test 4-5 different openers

What you think is funny may not be what the customer thinks is funny.
```

### When Testing a New Product:

```
Considering new product category (e.g., luggage, cologne, watches)

→ Ask: "Why not?"
→ If you can build a better product than the market
→ The market doesn't care about your category

Examples:
- Ridge did luggage (wallet company)
- True Classic doing backpacks, cologne, watches
- If the product is better, people will buy it
```

## Product Innovation Frameworks (Added by Nate Checketts)

### The "Fewer Better" Product Framework

```
THE PROBLEM:
└── Designers will fill the room if you give them an empty room

THE SOLUTION:
├── Add appropriate constraints to drive innovation
├── Development counts, adoption rates, CC targets
└── "This is the only wall you can paint on"

THE RESULT:
└── Better product innovation

THE ANALOGY: KITE
└── Constraint is what allows it to fly
└── Cutting the string doesn't make it go higher

EXAMPLE:
└── 3 commuter shirts, picked the best one
└── "This is the only dress shirt you ever need to buy"
```

### The Focus Group Framework

```
WHO:
├── People who don't know the brand (fresh perspective)
├── Some existing customers
└── 15-20 people per market, multiple markets

WHAT:
├── Show them product
├── Ask questions
├── Observe reactions
└── Give them credit (they usually spend more than the credit)

WHY IT WORKS:
└── You don't need a thousand-person sample
└── 100 target demo customers saying the same thing, listen

COST:
└── Relatively inexpensive
└── Food + refreshments + credit
└── Net net: you make money
```

### The Product Extension Framework

```
HOW TO DECIDE WHAT PRODUCTS TO MAKE:

1. FOCUS GROUPS
2. WEAR TESTING
3. DATA (Adoption rates, CC targets)
4. INTUITION (What's the white space?)

THE WHITE SPACE CHECK:
├── Is the market overcharging and underdoing?
├── Can we make it better, cheaper, more comfortable?
└── If yes: Go for it

LESSON LEARNED:
└── Just because you can make it doesn't mean you should
└── Be clear about why you're making it
└── Tell the story as clearly as possible
```
