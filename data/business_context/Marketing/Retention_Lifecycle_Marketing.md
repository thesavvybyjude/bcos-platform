# Retention & Lifecycle Marketing

## Purpose
To provide frameworks for retaining customers, reducing churn, and maximizing customer lifetime value through email, SMS, and product strategy.

## Core Philosophy

> "Revenue per recipient tells you how valuable each segment is."

> "Churn reduction is around education around the product."

## Principles

### 1. Revenue Per Recipient is the Key Metric
- Different segments have different values
- Subscribers vs. repeat buyers vs. one-time buyers vs. non-buyers
- Send to the highest value segments first

### 2. Churn Happens in Peaks
- Day 1: Biggest churn peak (discount seekers)
- Day 28: Billing reminder churn
- 31-45 days: Natural attrition if product didn't deliver

### 3. Education is the Antidote to Churn
- If they know how to use the product, they'll stay
- Walk them through step-by-step tutorials
- Set expectations early

### 4. Four Reasons People Churn
- "I can't afford this"
- "I have too much"
- "The effect is not what I want"
- "I found another brand"

### 5. Pre-Churn Strategy
- Every reason has a rebuttal
- Too much? Smaller size or gift suggestions
- Too expensive? Discounts or smaller commitment
- Wrong effect? Different product recommendation

### 6. The Wow Budget
- Surprise and delight budget for customer service
- When product is damaged, send more for free
- "Keep the damaged product and give it to someone else"
- The cost of goods is less than the cost of acquisition

### 7. GWP (Gift With Purchase) Strategy
- High perceived value, low COGS
- If GWP costs $100 and reduces CAC by more than $100, it is worth it
- Arbitrage the CAC with cost of goods

## Decision Frameworks

### Churn Reduction Framework

```
CHURN REASONS AND REBUTTALS:

"I CAN'T AFFORD THIS"
└── Discount, smaller size, smaller commitment

"I HAVE TOO MUCH"
├── Suggest giving to friends
├── Suggest consuming differently (recipes, mocktails)
└── Downsize (12-pack to 6-pack)

"THE EFFECT IS NOT WHAT I WANT"
├── Cross-sell to different product
├── Suggest different consumption method
└── Self-dose options

"I FOUND ANOTHER BRAND"
└── Don't let them get there: More communication, more value
```

### Segmentation Framework

```
BIGGEST BUCKETS:
├── Subscribers (Highest value)
├── Repeat buyers (Non-subscription)
├── One-time buyers
└── Non-buyers (Lowest value)

REVENUE PER RECIPIENT:
├── Subscribers: $8+ per email sent
├── Repeat buyers: Varies
├── One-time buyers: Low
└── Non-buyers: Very low

ACTION:
└── Send most to highest value segments first
└── Elapse (remove) people who only buy on discount
```

### Pre-Churn Cadence Framework

```
DAY 1-7 (The Hype Phase):
├── What to expect
├── The feeling/effect to expect
├── Quality of product
├── Use cases
└── Implementation guides

DAY 28 (The Billing Reminder):
└── "You're about to be charged"
└── "You can still cancel"

31-45 DAYS (The Natural Churn Window):
└── Product has been received
└── They've decided if they like it
└── If not: This is where you lose them

SOLUTION:
└── Don't let them get to "not sure"
└── Over-communicate in the first 7 days
```

### Wow Budget Framework

```
SURPRISE AND DELIGHT BUDGET:
├── Customer service team discretion
├── "Keep the damaged product, here's more"
└── Send a six-pack instead of one can

WHY IT WORKS:
├── Cost of goods vs. cost of acquisition
├── One delighted customer tells others
└── Future LTV > current cost

THE MATH:
└── If giving away $10 of product reduces CAC by $15, it is worth it
```

## Rules

- **Always track revenue per recipient** — It tells you who to send to
- **Always over-communicate in the first 7 days** — This is where churn is prevented
- **Always have rebuttals for churn reasons** — Don't just let them leave
- **Always have a wow budget** — Surprise and delight pays for itself
- **Never let customers leave without giving them another option** — Cross-sell, downsize, or upsell

## Heuristics

- If you can, give a 12-pack instead of a six-pack for subscription
- Founder-only plain text emails convert better than designed emails
- Customer support and product development should email customers monthly
- GWP is better than Discount (better perceived value, same cost)

## Triggers

### When Customer Says They Are Leaving:

```
Customer: "I'm canceling"

→ Identify the reason (from four options)
→ Provide the rebuttal:
   ├── Too expensive: Discount or smaller size
   ├── Too much: Give to friends or downsize
   ├── Wrong effect: Different product
   └── Found another brand: Not much you can do

If they stay: Great, keep them
If they leave: Get feedback, learn
```

## Welcome Flow Framework (Added by Ryan Bartlett)

### The Welcome Flow Framework

```
WHY IT MATTERS:
└── The most important touch point you will ever have
└── First email: Customer decides if they are going to try you

THE OFFER:
├── Test 10% to 20% to 30%
├── 10 to 20: Astronomical improvement
└── 20 to 30: Did nothing

THE SWEET SPOT:
└── 20% is the sweet spot (True Classic)

THE EVOLUTION:
└── Cash back instead of percentage off (Fondue)
└── Another amazing transition

THE FLOW STRUCTURE:
├── Email 1: Welcome + offer
├── Email 2: Value prop
├── Email 3: Free shipping offer
└── Email 4+: More value, more offers

THE HAIL MARY:
└── If someone hasn't bought in 120 days
└── Give them a deeper discount
└── See if they're a valuable customer
```

### Retention as a Lever Framework

```
THE PROBLEM:
└── Everyone lives in acquisition mode
└── Forgetting your best customers are the ones that come back

THE LEVERS:
├── Send more emails
├── Send more SMS
├── Better retention flows
└── Welcome flow optimization

THE IMPACT:
└── Repeat drives the whole business
└── That's where you grow by leaps and bounds

THE TIMING:
└── Invest in a retention leader early
└── Build out welcome flow, abandoned cart flows, recoveries
```

### Service-Based Business — The Three R's Framework

```
RETENTION:
    │
    ├── Keep existing clients longer
    ├── Deliver consistent value
    ├── Set expectations upfront
    └── First 100 days are critical

RESELL:
    │
    ├── Sell them again (ongoing support, retainer)
    ├── Same client, same service, longer term
    └── Repeatable process

UPSELL:
    │
    ├── Sell them a higher ticket item
    ├── Move them to a premium service
    ├── Same client, higher value
    └── Repeatable process

GOAL:
    ── Every system should be documented and repeatable
    ── Not custom for each client
```

### Service-Based Business — Client Retention System

```
ONBOARDING (First 100 Days):
    │
    ├── Set expectations
    ├── Show how to be successful
    ├── Where to get help
    └── Implementation pathway

DELIVERY & SUPPORT:
    │
    ├── Frequency of communication
    ├── Platforms used
    ├── Cadence of check-ins
    └── Emergency protocols

RETENTION CHECKPOINTS:
    │
    ├── 30-day check-in
    ├── 60-day progress review
    ├── 90-day success review
    └── 100-day retention decision

GOAL:
    ── Never lose a customer again
```
