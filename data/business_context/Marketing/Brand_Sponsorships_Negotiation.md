# Brand Sponsorships & Negotiation

## Purpose
To provide a comprehensive framework for creators to land, negotiate, and maximize brand sponsorship deals while maintaining audience trust and building long-term relationships.

## Core Philosophy

> "The barriers to creation are truly going to disappear and the only thing that's going to remain is taste, creativity, understanding of culture and distribution."

- **Inbound over Outbound:** Brands should come to you through high-quality organic content, not cold outreach
- **Positioning = Pricing Power:** How you position yourself determines how much you can charge
- **Quality over Views:** Brands want creative that performs, not just reach
- **Respect the Audience:** For every paid post, deliver 4-5 high-signal organic pieces

## Principles

### 1. Create for Free First
- Make organic content about brands you genuinely love
- All major brand relationships started from organic coverage
- Example: Meta relationships began with covering Instagram updates and Ray-Ban Meta

### 2. Brands Come to You
- 32 brands worked with in one year through inbound inquiries
- Outbound negotiation is "overrated"
- Patience is required—many creators lack it

### 3. Followership = Trust
- Brands still value followers despite "followers don't matter" sentiment
- Trust is the actual asset being sold

### 4. Quality Creates Quantity
- 2024: Working 10-12 hours/day, output high
- 2025: Revenue tripled while output decreased significantly
- Better positioning = higher value per video

### 5. The 80/20 Rule
- For every brand deal: 4 high-signal organic videos
- Ratio maintains audience trust
- Prevents diminishing returns on paid partnerships

### 6. Anchor High
- Never settle for less than you're worth
- High anchor ($25K vs $2.5K) commands more respect
- Brands respect confidence even if they can't pay that rate yet

### 7. Get Management Early
- Management 3xed business overnight
- Deal flow stayed same; deal value 3xed
- Creators are bad negotiators for their own talent
- Management handles "bad cop" role

### 8. Audience Can Tell Authenticity
- Only work with brands you genuinely use and love
- Faked passion is detectable
- Authentic recommendations build long-term trust

### 9. The Athlete Partnership Framework (Added by Manny Lubin)
- UFC partnerships: Authentic alignment with fighters
- "The UFC is going to be massive" — Manny Lubin
- Athlete partnerships: Find athletes who genuinely align with your brand
- Authenticity > reach: An athlete who uses your product is more valuable than one with a huge following who doesn't
- Long-term relationships: Build with athletes as they grow
- Example: MMA fighters wearing your brand to press conferences

### 10. The UFC Sponsorship Opportunity (Added by Manny Lubin)
- UFC is growing rapidly (PPV, ESPN deal, international expansion)
- Less competition for sponsorships than NBA/NFL
- Authentic alignment with the sport's values: grit, discipline, performance
- Fighter partnerships: Affordable, authentic, engaged audience
- In-venue, on-canvas, athlete apparel opportunities

## Decision Frameworks

### Pricing & Negotiation Decision Tree

```
Brand approaches with opportunity
    │
    ▼
Do you genuinely use/love this product?
    │
    ├── NO → Decline or counter with organic-first approach
    │
    └── YES → Move to pricing
        │
        ▼
Anchor at PREMIUM PRICE (minimum 3x what you think)
        │
        ├── Brand accepts → Great! Execute
        │
        ├── Brand counters low → Decide:
        │   │
        │   ├── Is this a logo/credibility play? → Consider lower for 1st deal
        │   │
        │   └── Is it purely financial? → Hold firm or walk
        │
        └── Brand rejects → Maintain relationship
            │
            ▼
            Continue creating organic content about them
            │
            ▼
            Return when they see your value
```

### Deal Evaluation Framework

| Factor | Weight | Considerations |
|--------|--------|----------------|
| Authenticity Fit | High | Do I genuinely use this product? |
| Price | High | Is this anchoring high enough? |
| Logo Value | Medium | Will this help land future deals? |
| Creative Freedom | Medium | Can I make this my style? |
| Audience Fit | High | Will my audience benefit? |
| Time Required | Medium | What's the actual time investment? |

## Rules

- **Always anchor high** — Start negotiations at a premium price point
- **Never accept first offer** — It's likely undervaluing you
- **Never discount immediately** — Reframe to ROI first
- **Always follow a paid post with 4 organic pieces** — Respect the audience
- **Only work with brands you genuinely use** — Authenticity is non-negotiable
- **Get management before scaling deals** — Professionals negotiate better than you
- **Never optimize only for views** — Optimize for the right audience
- **Never post filler content to meet quotas** — Last 10 pieces define your brand
- **Treat every organic post as business development** — Free content is lead generation

## Heuristics

- If you can't genuinely endorse it, don't take the deal
- One $25K deal > ten $2.5K deals (in every dimension)
- Your last 10 pieces are your resume to brands
- Brands hire creators for creativity + distribution, not just reach
- The follower count still matters for high-level deals
- Inbound deals > Outbound deals by 10x value

## Triggers

### "This is too expensive" Response Protocol

```
Customer/Client says: "This is expensive"

→ Do NOT offer discount
→ Instead explain ROI first
→ Reframe value before considering price adjustments
```

### Brand Negotiation Protocol

```
Brand proposes low price

→ Do NOT accept immediately
→ Anchor higher with justification
→ If they can't meet price, offer reduced scope at same rate
→ Never discount the rate itself
```

## Failure Modes

### Common Mistakes

1. **Starting low in negotiations** — Can't go up from there; signals low self-worth
2. **Optimizing only for views** — Attracts wrong audience (16-year-olds vs CMOs)
3. **Posting filler content** — Last 10 pieces are what brands evaluate
4. **Not respecting the audience** — Trust loss at scale; diminishing returns
5. **Resisting management** — Creators are bad at negotiating their own worth
6. **Not maintaining ratio** — 1 paid : 4 organic is minimum
7. **Working with brands you don't actually use** — Authenticity is detectable

## Offer Creation & GWP Strategy (Added by Nick Shackleford)

### Offer Structure Framework

```
OFFERS DO TWO THINGS:
├── 1. Get them to buy MORE (AOV increase)
└── 2. Get MORE people to buy (Acquisition)

OFFER MUST BE:
├── Clearly more valuable than just landing on the website
├── Positioned as the best they can't find anywhere else
└── Designed to bring them back

GIFT WITH PURCHASE (GWP) STRATEGY:
├── High perceived value, low COGS
├── Example: Blender with AG1, Mug with coffee
├── The MSRP feels higher than the actual cost
└── Arbitrage the CAC with cost of goods

THE MATH:
└── If GWP costs $100 and reduces CAC by more than $100, it is worth it

PRODUCT EXPANSION THINKING:
├── Think about secondary/tertiary products
├── What can you bundle?
├── What has high perceived value and low COGS?
└── What drives the next purchase?
```

### Building in Public Framework

```
WHY BUILD IN PUBLIC:
├── Signal to market: "Watch us"
├── Attract employees
├── Attract retail partners
├── Attract tech partners (investments, deals)
└── Lower CAC (organic content helps paid performance)

THE DOUBLE-EDGED SWORD:
├── Welcome more competition
├── Key man risk (if you stop, brand suffers)
└── Must be sustainable long-term

THE COMMITMENT:
├── If you start, you cannot stop
├── Disproportionate gain when doing it
└── Disproportionate loss when stopping

THE SUSTAINABLE APPROACH:
├── Make it feasible long-term
├── Don't let your personal brand become the only moat
└── Build the business beyond you
```

## Live Sports & Brand Sponsorships (Added by Ryan Bartlett)

### Live Event Sponsorship Framework

```
TRADITIONAL SPONSORSHIP:
├── Static logos on ice/court/field
├── Logo on jerseys
└── Overpaying for assets that don't net out

TRUE CLASSIC APPROACH:
├── Trigger events (like Chick-fil-A free throw)
├── Create experiences: if X happens, everyone gets free product
├── Gamified experiences for fans
└── Seat upgrades, giveaways, "True Classic moments"

THE MATH:
├── Seven figures minimum for major partnerships
├── Six figures for on-ice/on-court placement
└── Can't track day 1 ROAS: it's an upper-funnel brand play

THE ADVICE:
└── Gut the bloat, get to what matters
└── Avoid static integrations
└── Spend on fan experiences
```

### Simon Squibb — Two Reasons Sponsorship Happens

1. **Value return**—trackable ROI (views, sales)
2. **Emotional sale**—personal connection

### Simon Squibb — Understanding the Brand

- Understand what their brand values are
- Research how they traditionally advertise
- Visa = Olympics—don't ask them to sponsor your event

### Simon Squibb — The People Inside the Brand

- Build champions inside the company
- They won't get fired for sponsoring something relevant
- The brand is made up of people

### Simon Squibb — Media Buyers and Agencies

- Media buyers hold the money—sell to them, not the brand
- Agencies can insert your idea into campaigns

### Simon Squibb — Use the Brand Naturally

- Wear whoop on the street with millions of views
- They'll contact you
- "Just leaning in to a brand relationship and not initially thinking about the money"

### WSJ — The Brand in Commodity Markets Framework

```
THE PROBLEM:
├── Bottled water is a commodity
├── "There's nothing incredibly different about our water"
├── Traditional branding: "pure, pristine, mountain spring"
│
THE SOLUTION:
├── Sell rebellion, not water
├── Punk rock, skulls, counterculture
├── "Marketing water like booze"
│
THE RESULT:
├── $1.4B valuation
├── $263M retail scan sales (2023)
├── 140% YoY growth
├── 113,000 retail locations
│
KEY INSIGHT:
└── "The success of the bottled water industry is the success of the marketing industry"

LESSON: In a commodity market, brand is everything
```
