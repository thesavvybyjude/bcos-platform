# Organizational Structure & Legal Entities

## Purpose
To provide a framework for choosing the right legal structure, registering the business, and establishing governance that ensures sustainability and liability protection.

## Core Philosophy

> "A business without a legal identity is like a person without a name — it cannot stand, sue, or be sued."

## Principles

### 1. Register a Name
- The first step; make it known to the law
- A business name is an asset, not just a label
- Protect it through trademark registration

### 2. Separate Business from Owner
- Limited liability protects personal assets
- The business is a legal person distinct from its owners
- This separation enables risk-taking without personal ruin

### 3. Choose the Right Structure
- Structure determines liability, taxation, and growth potential
- Wrong structure = unnecessary risk or missed opportunity
- Revisit structure as the business evolves

### 4. Governance Even in Small Businesses
- Even a one-owner Ltd should have a board of directors
- Oversight prevents blind spots
- Good governance attracts investors and partners

### 5. Compliance is Non-Negotiable
- File annual returns, pay taxes, keep records
- Regulatory scanning prevents costly surprises
- Documentation is your defense in disputes

## Decision Frameworks

### Legal Structure Selection

```
SOLE PROPRIETORSHIP
├── Simplest form
├── Full personal liability
├── Best for: Solo freelancers, low-risk ventures
└── Risk: Personal assets at stake

PARTNERSHIP
├── Shared risk and profit
├── Joint and several liability
├── Best for: Two+ founders with complementary skills
└── Risk: Partner disputes, unlimited liability

LIMITED LIABILITY COMPANY (Ltd)
├── Separates personal from business assets
├── More credibility with banks and investors
├── Best for: Growing businesses, seeking investment
└── Risk: More paperwork, compliance costs

PUBLIC LIMITED COMPANY (PLC)
├── Shares traded publicly
├── High regulation and disclosure
├── Best for: Large-scale operations, exit via IPO
└── Risk: Loss of control, regulatory burden

NOT-FOR-PROFIT (NGO)
├── Registered by trustees or guarantee
├── Tax benefits, donation-friendly
├── Best for: Social enterprises, charitable missions
└── Risk: Restricted activities, funding dependence
```

### Structure Selection Decision Tree

```
START
│
├── Solo founder?
│   ├── YES → Low risk? → Sole Proprietorship
│   │         High risk? → Ltd (even as sole director)
│   └── NO → Partnership or Ltd
│
├── Seeking investment?
│   ├── YES → Ltd (investors require limited liability)
│   └── NO → Structure based on risk level
│
├── Going public eventually?
│   ├── YES → PLC structure from early planning
│   └── NO → Ltd is sufficient
│
└── Social mission?
    ├── YES → NGO or social enterprise structure
    └── NO → Commercial structure
```

### Board Composition Framework

```
MINIMUM BOARD: 3 Directors
├── Founder/CEO (operational vision)
├── Independent Director (governance, objectivity)
└── Industry Expert (domain knowledge, network)

RECOMMENDED ADDITIONS AT SCALE:
├── Financial Expert (audit, risk management)
├── Legal Expert (compliance, contracts)
└── Investor Representative (capital allocation)

THE RULE:
└── "If you're the smartest person in the room, you're in the wrong room."
```

## Rules

- **Always register the business** — operating without registration is illegal and risky
- **Always choose limited liability** — protect personal assets from business risks
- **Always have at least 3 directors** — balance prevents blind spots
- **Always file annual returns** — compliance is cheaper than penalties
- **Never operate as sole proprietor if scaling** — personal liability is unacceptable at scale

## International Law Extension

For businesses operating globally (or aspiring to), understanding international law is valuable:
- Contract law differs by country
- International trade law
- Cross-border dispute resolution

**Rule:** Consider studying basic business law or international law as an extension.
- **Never skip governance** — even small businesses need oversight

## Heuristics

- If you're seeking investment, you need Ltd structure
- If you have partners, you need a shareholder agreement
- If you're hiring, you need employment contracts
- If you're selling, you need terms and conditions
- If you're operating, you need insurance

## Templates

### Business Registration Checklist

```
□ Choose business name (unique, available)
□ Select legal structure
□ Register with relevant authority
□ Obtain tax identification number
□ Open business bank account
□ Register for applicable taxes
□ Obtain necessary licenses/permits
□ Set up accounting system
□ Draft shareholder/partnership agreement
□ Appoint directors
□ File initial documents
```

### Shareholder Agreement Essentials

```
1. Ownership percentages
2. Decision-making rights
3. Transfer restrictions (pre-emptive rights)
4. Dividend policy
5. Exit mechanisms (drag-along, tag-along)
6. Non-compete clauses
7. Dispute resolution
8. Deadlock resolution
```
