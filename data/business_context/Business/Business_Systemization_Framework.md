# Business Systemization Framework

## Purpose
To provide a practical, step-by-step methodology to systemize any small business, enabling the day-to-day work to get done without constant founder involvement.

## Core Philosophy

> "Systemization is a continuous improvement engine that your team uses every day to make your business more documented and efficient."

## The Five Lies of Systemization

### Lie 1: "Be Like Me"
- Sample size of one is insufficient
- What worked for one founder may not work for you
- Expertise comes from thousands of reps, not personal anecdotes
- Don't copy someone else's system; build your own

### Lie 2: "Record Loom Videos"
- Video documentation creates a "documentation graveyard"
- Videos are hard to update, impossible to search, and quickly outdated
- Written documentation is easier to maintain, search, and version control
- Write, don't record

### Lie 3: "Hire a Genius" (Integrator/COO)
- You already have enough knowledge to systemize your business
- Experts accelerate but don't replace your involvement
- No one cares about your business as much as you do
- The founder must drive steps 1-2; the team owns steps 3-5

## Systems Thinking Philosophy

### What Makes a Good Business?
- **Repeatability** — deliver on your promise consistently
- **Key Person Independence** — works without the owner
- **Definition:** A business is something that can generate profit without the business owner
- If you only get paid when you show up, you have a job, not a business

### The Technician Trap
- Founders start as technicians (doing the work)
- Behaviours that got you to $600k (micromanaging, being the hero) become barriers to scaling
- You must transition from being *in* the business to being *on* the business
- **Rule:** What got you here won't get you there

### Systems as Compounding Assets
- Each system is an asset that compounds over time
- Do the work once → identify what works → profit from it repeatedly
- Einstein called compounding the 8th wonder of the world
- Systems have a compounding effect: small efficiencies stack → massive profit

### Optionality
- Systems give you choices (optionality)
- Options include: exit, scale, franchise, acquire, roll-up strategy
- Without systems, you have no choice — you're trapped in the business

*See `CEO/Systems_Thinking_Business_Architecture.md` for the complete systems thinking framework.*

## Phase 0: Product-Market Fit (Before Systemizing)

**Critical Insight:** Before you systemize anything, confirm that you have something people actually want to buy.

**Methodology:**
1. Create a loose list of deliverables (based on experience, customer feedback, and what you'd enjoy)
2. Get on 50+ sales calls (no screening, no expectations, just conversations)
3. Ask: "What's going wrong for you? What would help in a perfect world?"
4. Listen for alignment between what they want and what you're offering
5. Quote prices and observe reactions

**Goal:** Validate demand and pricing before investing in systems
**Timeline:** 2-3 months
**Rule:** Don't systemize until you have product-market fit

## The Five-Step Systemization Framework

```
Step 1: WHAT
│
├── List every area of responsibility in your business
├── Break down to granular actions (not high-level categories)
├── Include everything: sales, marketing, production, finance, HR
└── This is the founder's job — you know what needs to happen

Step 2: WHO
│
├── Assign each area to a ROLE (not a person)
├── Roles enable scalability; names create dependencies
├── Document: Title, Reports To, Key Responsibilities, KPIs
└── This is also the founder's job — you decide who owns what

Step 3: WHERE
│
├── Store everything in ONE work management tool
├── One tab, one search bar — no scattered documents
├── Recommended: ClickUp, Asana, Monday.com
├── If it's not in the tool, it doesn't exist
└── The team owns this — they decide how to organize

Step 4: WHEN
│
├── Define cadence: daily, weekly, monthly, quarterly
├── Set deadlines and recurring rhythms
├── Create calendars and check-in points
├── Make the invisible visible (when things are due)
└── The team owns this — they define the rhythm

Step 5: HOW
│
├── Create detailed SOPs, checklists, and templates
├── BUT only for the 20% that truly need step-by-step instructions
├── The other 80% is handled by clear What/Who/Where/When
├── Use the 80/20 rule: document what matters most
└── The team owns this — they write the SOPs
```

### Process Maps in the 5-Step Framework

Process mapping fits into the "How" phase but serves all phases:

```
PHASE               PROCESS MAP CONTRIBUTION
─────               ────────────────────────
What                 Visual list of all actions in a process
Who                  Swim lanes show who is responsible for each step
Where                Linked to the work management tool
When                 Timelines and decision points shown visually
How                  Detailed instructions linked from the map
```

## The 80/20 Rule of Documentation

```
NOT EVERY TASK NEEDS A FULL SOP

THE 20% THAT NEED DETAILED SOPs:
├── High-frequency tasks (done daily/weekly)
├── High-impact tasks (mistakes are costly)
├── Complex tasks (multiple decision points)
├── Compliance tasks (legal/regulatory requirements)
└── Cross-functional tasks (multiple people involved)

THE 80% THAT DON'T:
├── Low-frequency tasks (done monthly/quarterly)
├── Low-impact tasks (mistakes are easily fixed)
├── Simple tasks (few steps, no decision points)
├── Individual tasks (only one person does it)
└── Intuitive tasks (common sense)

THE RULE:
└── "Document with purpose, not perfection."
```

## Team-Led vs. Founder-Led

```
FOUNDER-LED (Steps 1-2):
│
├── You set the structure
├── You define What needs to happen
├── You assign Who owns each area
├── This requires your knowledge and authority
└── No one else can do this for you

TEAM-LED (Steps 3-5):
│
├── The team owns execution and improvement
├── They decide Where to store information
├── They define When things are due
├── They write How to do it (SOPs)
└── Adoption increases when they co-create

THE SHIFT:
├── Founder starts the system
├── Team adopts and improves it
├── Founder reviews and guides
└── System becomes self-sustaining
```

## Feedback Loops

```
WHEN SOMETHING GOES WRONG:

Step 1: PREVENTATIVE ACTION
├── Fix the immediate problem
├── Identify the root cause
├── Add a step to prevent recurrence
└── Update the SOP

Step 2: REVISE TEMPLATE
├── After each project, review the template
├── Add any missing tasks
├── Remove any unnecessary steps
└── Update for new learnings

Step 3: ADD TO FAQ
├── If a question is asked more than once, it's an FAQ
├── Add it to the relevant documentation
├── Prevent the same question from being asked again
└── Build institutional knowledge

THE RULE:
└── "Make improvement part of the process, not an afterthought."
```

## Adoption Through Co-Creation

```
WHY CO-CREATION WORKS:
├── People support what they help create
├── Team members know their work better than you do
├── Writing the SOP forces clarity
└── Adoption is higher when they own it

HOW TO CO-CREATE:
├── Founder defines What and Who (Steps 1-2)
├── Team defines Where, When, and How (Steps 3-5)
├── Have team members write their own SOPs
├── Review together, then implement
└── Give feedback, but let them own the document

THE RESULT:
├── Higher adoption rates
├── Better documentation (they know the details)
├── Continuous improvement (they'll update their own SOPs)
└── Stronger accountability (they wrote it, they own it)
```

## Documentation Maintenance

```
THE 90-DAY REVIEW:
├── Every 90 days, review all documentation
├── Check: Is this still accurate?
├── Check: Has anything changed?
├── Check: Are there new FAQs?
└── Update as needed

NEW HIRES AS QUALITY AUDITORS:
├── New hires don't know "how we've always done it"
├── They see gaps that experienced team members miss
├── Use their fresh perspective to improve documentation
└── Assign documentation review as part of onboarding

THE RULE:
└── "If it's not maintained, it's not a system."
```

## Automated Lead Qualification (Added by UI)

### The Problem

- Founders or front desk staff manually qualify leads
- This is inefficient, emotional, and unscalable
- Human intervention = bottleneck

### The Solution

1. **Define qualification criteria** (based on customer avatar + unit economics)
2. **Automate the qualification process** using tools like ManyChat/Privia
3. **The system qualifies, not the person**

### Example Workflow

```
1. User comments "mentor" on social media post
2. Automation sends a standardised DM
3. User responds, triggering a script
4. System checks criteria (budget, need, timing, etc.)
5. If criteria met → moves to CRM as qualified lead
6. If not → automated decline
```

### Qualification Criteria Must Be Impersonal

```
□ Budget: Can they afford it? (Yes/No)
□ Need: Do they have the problem you solve? (Yes/No)
□ Timing: Are they ready to buy now? (Yes/No)
□ Authority: Can they make the purchase decision? (Yes/No)

Need 4/4 to qualify. No subjective criteria allowed.
Rule: "You are selecting a customer, not a life partner."
```

### Tools

- ManyChat / Privia for DM automation
- CRM for lead tracking
- No human intervention required for initial qualification

## The CEO Operating System (End State)

The ultimate goal of systemization is to build 8 systems that allow the CEO to remove themselves from operations:

```
THE 8 SYSTEMS
├── 1. Decision Architecture — Categorise decisions (Type A/B/C) and create decision trees
├── 2. Communication Firewall — 4 levels: Emergencies, Same-day, Weekly, Monthly
├── 3. Delegation Framework — Brain dump → Quality filter → Feedback loop
├── 4. Approval Matrix — Tiered financial authority ($0-2k, $2-30k, $30k+)
├── 5. Information Dashboard — AC/DC framework (Attract, Convert, Deliver, Collect)
├── 6. Escalation Protocol — 3 levels + 10-10-10 rule
├── 7. Automation Stack — 3 tiers (Admin, Marketing, Operations)
└── 8. Replacement Test — Can you walk away for 4 weeks?
```

These systems work together to create a business that runs without you. See `Operations/CEO_Operating_Systems.md` for the complete framework.

## Rules

- **Always start with What** — you can't assign what you haven't defined
- **Always assign to roles, not names** — roles scale; names create dependencies
- **Always use one tool** — scattered information is lost information
- **Always apply the 80/20 rule** — document what matters, skip what doesn't
- **Always co-create with your team** — adoption comes from ownership
- **Never record videos for documentation** — write instead
- **Never skip the 90-day review** — stale systems fail
- **Never assume people know** — if it's not written down, it doesn't exist

## Heuristics

- If you're the only one who knows how things work, you don't have a system
- If your team can't find the information, it doesn't exist
- If documentation is outdated, it's worse than no documentation
- If you're doing it differently every time, you need an SOP
- If adoption is low, the system was imposed, not co-created
