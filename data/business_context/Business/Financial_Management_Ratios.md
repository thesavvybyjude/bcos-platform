# Financial Management & Ratios

## Purpose
To provide a framework for tracking financial performance, optimizing efficiency, and ensuring long-term profitability through data-driven decision making.

## Core Philosophy

> "What you cannot measure, you cannot manage."

## Principles

### 1. Numbers Tell the Story
- All business activities and results can be expressed in numerical form
- Financial data reveals what's really happening (not what you think is happening)
- Regular review prevents small problems from becoming big ones

### 2. Ratios Reveal Efficiency
- Raw numbers are less meaningful than ratios
- Ratios compare performance to benchmarks
- Trends over time matter more than single snapshots

### 3. Gross Profit is King
- Production efficiency drives everything else
- If gross margin is declining, nothing else matters
- Protect the core economics before optimizing anything else

### 4. Cost Control is Profit Creation
- Every dollar saved drops straight to the bottom line
- Marketing and admin ratios reveal waste
- Benchmark against industry standards

### 5. Financial Modeling Enables Decision-Making
- Model scenarios before committing resources
- Understand the P&L impact of every major decision
- Cash flow is survival; profit is growth

## Decision Frameworks

### Key Financial Ratios

```
PRODUCTION EFFICIENCY:
└── Gross Profit Margin = (Sales - Cost of Sales) / Sales
    ├── Target: Industry-dependent (typically 40-60%)
    ├── Declining → investigate raw materials, waste, production process
    └── Improving → scale production, negotiate better supplier terms

DEMAND GENERATION EFFICIENCY:
└── Marketing-to-Sales Ratio = Marketing Spend / Sales
    ├── Target: Industry-dependent (typically 5-15%)
    ├── Rising → evaluate campaign ROI, adjust targeting
    └── Declining → either efficiency improving or under-investing in growth

OVERHEAD EFFICIENCY:
└── Admin-to-Sales Ratio = Admin Expense / Sales
    ├── Target: Industry-dependent (typically 10-20%)
    ├── Rising → overhead bloat, automation needed
    └── Declining → operational efficiency improving

ULTIMATE PROFITABILITY:
└── Net Profit Margin = Net Profit / Sales
    ├── Target: Industry-dependent (typically 10-20%)
    ├── The number that matters most
    └── If net margin is negative, fix gross margin first
```

### Ratio Improvement Framework

```
IF GROSS MARGIN IS DECLINING:
│
├── Check raw material costs (supplier renegotiation?)
├── Check production waste (process improvement?)
├── Check pricing (are you charging enough?)
└── Check product mix (are low-margin products dominating?)

IF MARKETING-TO-SALES IS RISING:
│
├── Check campaign performance (which channels underperform?)
├── Check targeting (are you reaching the right audience?)
├── Check conversion (is the offer compelling?)
└── Check attribution (are you counting all marketing costs?)

IF ADMIN-TO-SALES IS RISING:
│
├── Check headcount (are you overstaffed?)
├── Check overhead (office, tools, subscriptions)
├── Check process (manual tasks that could be automated?)
└── Check outsourcing (should anything be contracted out?)
```

## Rules

- **Always review financials monthly** — you can't manage what you don't measure
- **Always calculate ratios** — raw numbers alone are misleading
- **Always compare to benchmarks** — your numbers mean nothing in isolation
- **Always track trends** — one month is an anomaly; three months is a trend
- **Never ignore declining gross margin** — it's the foundation of everything
- **Never spend on marketing before fixing gross margin** — you'll just lose money faster

## Heuristics

- If gross margin is below industry average, fix production before anything else
- If marketing spend isn't generating proportional sales, fix targeting or offer
- If admin costs are growing faster than sales, overhead is bloating
- If net margin is negative, you have a fundamental business model problem
- Cash flow problems kill businesses; profit problems just slow them down

## Unit Economics: CAC vs LTV (Added by UI)

### Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC)

- Total cost to acquire one customer
- Includes marketing, sales, and any free products/services given away
- **Formula:** CAC = Total Acquisition Spend / Number of New Customers
- Goal: Keep CAC fixed as you scale

### Lifetime Value (LTV)

- Total revenue a customer generates over their entire relationship with the business
- **Formula:** LTV = Average Purchase Value × Average Purchase Frequency × Average Customer Lifespan
- Goal: LTV > CAC (ideally 3x+)

### Fixed vs Variable Costs

- **Fixed costs** make scaling profitable (cost doesn't increase with customers)
- **Variable CAC** makes scaling difficult and unpredictable
- A business is scalable when CAC is fixed and LTV > CAC

### Break-Even Analysis

```
Break-Even Customers = Fixed Costs / (Price - Variable Cost per Customer)

Example: Fricki's Tutoring Business
├── Fixed cost: R7,000 (notes creation)
├── Price: R200/module
├── LTV: R200 × 12 modules = R2,400
├── Break-even: R7,000 / R200 = 35 customers
└── After 35 customers: every customer is pure profit
```

## Approval Matrix (Financial Delegation)

```
TIER 1: INDIVIDUAL CONTRIBUTORS
├── Authority: $0 - $2,000
├── Examples: Software subscriptions, small discounts
└── Rule: Don't ask permission for small things

TIER 2: TEAM LEADS
├── Authority: $2,000 - $30,000
├── Examples: New hires, team retreats
└── Rule: Flag it, don't stop it

TIER 3: EXECUTIVES
├── Authority: $30,000+
├── Examples: Strategic decisions, fundraising, major products
└── Rule: Full discussion required

SET THRESHOLDS BASED ON:
└── What you can afford to lose, not what makes you comfortable
```
