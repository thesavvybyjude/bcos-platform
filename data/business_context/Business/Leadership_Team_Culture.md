# Leadership & Team Culture

## Purpose
To provide a framework for building high-performing teams, fostering accountability, and developing leaders who can sustain and scale the business.

## Core Philosophy

> "Team = Together Each Achieves More."

## Principles

### Leadership Attributes

#### 1. Vision
- See the destination and articulate it clearly
- If you don't know where you're going, you can't lead others
- Vision defines the destination; positioning defines how you appear on that journey

#### 2. Integrity
- Trustworthiness and honesty in all dealings
- Trust is the foundation of every relationship
- Integrity is non-negotiable; it's the price of leadership

#### 3. Capacity
- Ability to work hard and think strategically
- Capacity is built through experience and learning
- Leaders must continuously develop their capabilities

#### 4. Learning
- Continuous curiosity and improvement
- The moment you stop learning, you start dying
- Leaders are readers; readers are leaders

#### 5. Sacrifice
- Willingness to give time, money, and comfort for the team
- Leadership is service, not privilege
- The team watches what you do, not what you say

### Team Principles

#### 1. Common Vision
- Everyone knows where they are going
- Alignment beats agreement — you don't need everyone to agree, just to move in the same direction
- Vision must be communicated repeatedly

#### 2. Clear Roles
- Each person knows their expectations
- Role ambiguity kills accountability
- Document roles; don't assume people know

#### 3. Training
- Equip people to succeed
- Training is an investment, not an expense
- A trained person can replace themselves; an untrained person creates dependencies

#### 4. Practice
- Rehearse roles to build competence
- Practice builds confidence and consistency
- The team that practices together performs together

#### 5. Support
- No upmanship; we win and lose together
- Teamwork dies when individuals compete internally
- Celebrate team wins; share team losses

#### 6. Reward & Punish
- Recognize performance; address underperformance consistently
- Inconsistency destroys morale
- Both reward and punishment must be timely and proportionate

### 7. Systemization Is a Team Sport
- The founder sets the structure (What/Who), but the team owns the execution and improvement (Where/When/How)
- Adoption increases when team members co-create the systems (write their own SOPs, define their own templates)
- People support what they help create

### 8. Process Maps as Collaboration Tools
- The primary benefit of process maps is **collaboration**
- Share them immediately, even if messy — iteration happens through conversation
- Involve the team in building maps — they know the day-to-day reality better than the founder
- Rule: A process map that isn't shared isn't a process map — it's just a drawing

## Decision Frameworks

### Delegation Framework

```
Step 1: START SMALL
│
├── Delegate low-risk tasks first
├── Build trust through small wins
└── Observe capability and reliability

Step 2: TRAIN
│
├── Provide resources and coaching
├── Let them shadow you first
└── Give clear instructions and expected outcomes

Step 3: MONITOR
│
├── Measure output, not just activity
├── Set check-in points (not micromanagement)
└── Track quality and timeliness

Step 4: TRUST BUT VERIFY
│
├── Give autonomy with checkpoints
├── Review results, not process
└── Intervene only when quality drops

Step 5: SCALE
│
├── Gradually increase responsibility
├── Delegate decision-making, not just tasks
└── Develop the next leader, not just the next doer
```

### The 3-Part Delegation Framework (Matt)

```
PART 1: BRAIN DUMP
├── Audit your calendar and to-do list for the last month
├── Document every single task you've done
└── This is your raw material

PART 2: QUALITY FILTER
├── Mark every task you shouldn't be doing
├── Assign to someone on your team who can do it as good or better
├── Create a Google Doc system and Loom video for each
└── Hand off the system

PART 3: FEEDBACK LOOP
├── Hand off the system and video
├── Check in after 1 week: "What's your confidence level on a scale of 1-10?"
├── Support until confidence reaches 8+
└── Then step back completely
```

**Conflict Note:** *Leila advises against Loom videos (hard to maintain); Matt recommends them for initial delegation. Resolution: Use Loom for handoff, then convert to written SOPs over time.*

### Accountability Through Clarity Framework

```
ACCOUNTABILITY STARTS WITH CLARITY:

When everyone knows:
├── WHAT they are responsible for
├── WHO else is involved
├── WHERE to find the information
├── WHEN things are due
└── HOW to do the task (if documented)

…then accountability becomes natural.

IF PEOPLE ARE STILL NOT ACCOUNTABLE:
├── Check: Is the system clear? (What/Who/Where/When/How)
├── Check: Was the system co-created or imposed?
├── Check: Is it a cultural issue? (values misalignment)
├── Check: Is it a personnel issue? (wrong person in role)
└── Address the root cause, not the symptom
```

### The Systems Champion Role

```
THE PROBLEM:
├── Business owners are too busy to systemize (important but never urgent)
├── Founders are often not naturally systems people — that's normal
└── The behaviours that helped them grow stop them from systemizing

THE SOLUTION: A SYSTEMS CHAMPION
├── Definition: A dedicated person (NOT the business owner) who drives systemization
├── Who: A junior, hungry person with capacity
├── Why junior: Senior people are too busy; junior people have space
├── Outcome: They become the most knowledgeable person in the organisation
└── Timeline: 6-24 months before significant returns

WHO SHOULD IT BE?
├── Junior, not senior — they have capacity and hunger
├── Trained in technical work but don't know how to run the business — this is a gift
├── Naturally organised, uses checklists, systems thinking comes easily
└── It's their top priority because they need to learn to do well in their role

THE ROLE:
├── 1. Identify mission-critical systems across all departments
├── 2. Record key team members doing their core tasks
├── 3. Turn recordings into documented processes
├── 4. Centrally store all documentation
└── 5. Continuously improve and update

INVESTMENT TIMELINE:
├── 6-12 months: significant returns begin
├── 24 months: "I can't imagine doing business any other way"
└── Each new systems champion inherits the previous work — compounding effect
```

### Maker/Manager Communication Protocols

```
THE "MAKER'S NO":
├── When a Maker declines a meeting, do not take offense
├── See it as them keeping a larger commitment to you, the company, and the work
└── This is not a "real world no" — it's a "Maker's no"

IDEAL DAY CONVERSATIONS:
├── Ask your team: "What does a maximally productive day/week look like for you?"
├── Follow their answer to the greatest degree possible
└── Example: Editing team requested all meetings bunched on certain days → implemented → productivity increased

COMMUNICATE YOUR HAT:
├── "I'm putting my Maker hat on today"
├── "I'm putting my Manager hat on today"
└── Sets expectations with one phrase

MEETING PROTOCOL:
├── If someone is unresponsive during Maker blocks → they're working
├── Only interrupt if the meeting is truly worth destroying their block
└── If it can wait, wait
```

## Management Curriculum (Academic Context)

Management is about:
1. **Planning** — Setting goals and strategies
2. **Organising** — Structuring departments and roles
3. **Leadership** — Leading and motivating people
4. **Control** — Monitoring performance and ensuring goals are met

**Subfields:**
- Personnel Management (HR)
- Inventory Management
- Supply Chain Management
- Operations Management

## Rules

- **Always lead with vision** — people follow direction, not tasks
- **Always train before delegating** — untrained delegation is abdication
- **Always align personal and business goals** — alignment creates loyalty
- **Always reward and address performance consistently** — inconsistency destroys morale
- **Never play favorites** — real or perceived, it kills team culture
- **Never skip the difficult conversation** — avoidance compounds problems

## Heuristics

- If you can't take a week off without things falling apart, you have a people problem
- If one person's departure would cripple a function, you have a dependency problem
- If gossip is the primary communication channel, you have a culture problem
- If everyone agrees all the time, you have a thinking problem
- If turnover is high, the problem is leadership, not the team
