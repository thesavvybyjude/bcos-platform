# Practical Systemization Methodology

## Purpose
To provide a hands-on, real-world methodology for systemizing a business based on 8 years of experience working with thousands of small businesses.

## Core Philosophy

> "Before you systemize anything, confirm that you have something people actually want to buy."

## Principles

### 1. Product-Market Fit First (Step 0)
- Before you systemize anything, confirm you have something people actually want to buy
- Get on 50+ sales calls (no screening, no expectations, just conversations)
- Ask: "What's going wrong for you? What would help in a perfect world?"
- Listen for alignment between what they want and what you're offering
- Quote prices and observe reactions
- **Rule:** Don't systemize until you have product-market fit

### 2. Don't Over-Systemize Upfront
- Only build what the hypothesis requires
- You can always add more, but you can't get wasted time back
- Systemization is possible in 2-3 months, not 3-5 years

### 3. Small Tweaks First, Big Changes Later
- Start with small, quick fixes (moving a task, changing an email)
- Save big structural changes (client portals, major systems) until you have 5+ reps and confidence
- This prevents over-investing in systems that may need to change

### 4. Co-Creative Training
- Don't pre-write giant handbooks
- Build the playbook *with* trainees
- Ask: "What do you need?"
- Only build what the hypothesis requires

## Phased Implementation (Maker/Manager Context)

Understanding the Maker/Manager productivity system directly informs how to approach systemization:

**V1 (Small Business):**
- Maker time happens outside business hours (nights/weekends/early mornings)
- This is where you build the systems that will run your business
- You work the job you have AND work the job you want

**V2 (Intermediate):**
- First half of day = Maker (build systems, strategic thinking)
- Second half = Manager (coordination, meetings, delegation)
- Plan calendar back-to-front to protect mornings

**V3 (Advanced):**
- Entire Maker days (2-4 per week) = System building, strategy, innovation
- Entire Manager days = Meetings, coordination, people management

**Rule:** The Systems Champion handles the Manager work of documentation; the founder handles the Maker work of system design.

## Decision Frameworks

### The Pilot Project Approach

```
PHASE 0: VALIDATE PRODUCT-MARKET FIT
├── Create a loose list of deliverables
├── Get on 50+ sales calls
├── Ask: "What's going wrong? What would help?"
├── Quote prices and observe reactions
├── Validate demand and pricing
└── Build a CRM list from Day 1

PHASE 1: PILOT PROJECT
├── Step 1: Outline a Hypothesis
│   ├── Write down your guess of how the service will be delivered
│   ├── Include tasks and dates (e.g., 10 actions with deadlines)
│   └── Treat it as a hypothesis, not a fixed plan
│
├── Step 2: Build a Simple CRM
│   ├── Use your work management tool from Day 1
│   ├── Automate follow-ups: 3 touches before giving up
│   └── Track every lead and conversation
│
├── Step 3: Fulfill with "Keep Notes" System
│   ├── During each project, log every little thing that went wrong or could be better
│   ├── This is a running log of improvements
│   └── Don't fix everything at once — just log it
│
├── Step 4: Retrospective
│   ├── After each client, review your notes
│   ├── Implement changes for the next client
│   └── Build a positive feedback loop
│
└── Step 5: Iterate
    ├── Small tweaks first (moving a task, changing an email)
    ├── Big changes after 5+ reps and confidence
    └── Beta rollout: 2 clients → feedback → iterate → roll out to all
```

### The "Keep Notes" System

```
DURING EACH PROJECT, LOG:
├── What went wrong
├── What could be better
├── Client observations
├── Your own insights and ideas
└── Anything that felt inefficient or confusing

WHEN TO REVIEW:
├── At the end of each project (retrospective)
└── Implement changes before the next project

RULE:
└── Small tweaks first. Big changes after 5+ reps and confidence.
```

### Beta Rollout Process

```
STEP 1: IDENTIFY MAJOR CHANGE
├── Something that needs more than a small tweak
└── Requires structural change

STEP 2: TEST WITH 2 CLIENTS
├── Roll out to 2 clients first
├── Log feedback
└── Iterate based on what you learn

STEP 3: ITERATE
├── Fix issues found in beta
├── Refine the change
└── Confirm it works

STEP 4: ROLL OUT TO ALL
├── Only after beta is validated
├── Train team on the new system
└── Monitor for issues
```

### What to Validate (The Evaluation Frameworks)

When doing your 50+ sales calls (Phase 0), validate against these frameworks:

#### The 4 U's
- Is the problem **unworkable** (serious consequences)?
- Is it **unavoidable** (must be dealt with)?
- Is it **urgent** (top priority)?
- Is it **underserved** (not enough solutions)?

#### The Gain/Pain Ratio
- **Gain:** Money saved, time saved, productivity, quality of life.
- **Pain:** Finding you, trying you, buying you, adopting, training, risk, inertia.
- **Threshold:** Need an order of magnitude (10x) gain for people to change.

**Rule:** If the gain/pain ratio isn't at least 10:1, people won't adopt your product.

### Co-Creative Training Process

```
WHEN ADDING TEAM MEMBERS:
├── Don't pre-write giant handbooks
├── Ask the trainee: "What do you need to know?"
├── Build the playbook together, one piece at a time
├── Only build what the hypothesis requires
└── Each iteration improves the playbook

RULE:
└── You can always spend more time to systemize more, but if you over-systemize or create things that don't actually help, you will never get your time back.
```

## Rules

- **Always validate product-market fit first** — don't systemize until you have it
- **Always use the "Keep Notes" system** — log improvements as you go
- **Always start with small tweaks** — save big changes for after 5+ reps
- **Always co-create playbooks with trainees** — don't pre-write handbooks
- **Always attach metrics to every phase** — measure what matters
- **Never over-systemize upfront** — only build what the hypothesis requires
- **Never skip the retrospective** — review notes after each client
- **Never roll out major changes without beta testing** — test with 2 clients first

## Heuristics

- If you don't have product-market fit, you're systemizing a business that may not exist
- If you're writing handbooks before hiring, you're building for a team that doesn't exist yet
- If you're making big changes before 5+ reps, you're guessing, not iterating
- If you're not keeping notes, you're forgetting what you learned
- If your systems aren't improving with each client, you're not iterating
- If you can't explain your process in 10 steps, it's too complex
- If systemization is taking years, you're over-complicating it
