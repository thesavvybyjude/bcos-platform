# Financial Management & Ratios

The complete financial literacy framework for founders — from P&L reading to ratio-based decision making.

## Purpose

This module provides the financial management principles every founder must master. It covers profit & loss analysis, key financial ratios, cash flow management, and financial decision-making frameworks that translate numbers into strategic action.

## Principles

1. **Revenue is Vanity, Profit is Sanity, Cash Flow is Reality** — Top-line revenue means nothing without margin. Profit means nothing without cash collection. Always prioritize cash flow timing over accounting profit.

2. **Know Your Unit Economics Cold** — Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC), Lifetime Value (LTV), and the LTV:CAC ratio are the three numbers that determine business viability. If LTV:CAC < 3:1, the model is broken.

3. **Separate Fixed from Variable** — Every cost must be classified as fixed or variable. This classification drives break-even analysis, pricing decisions, and scaling strategy.

4. **The 13-Week Cash Flow Forecast** — Maintain a rolling 13-week cash flow forecast updated weekly. This is the single most important financial tool for a growing business. It prevents surprise cash crunches.

5. **Margin Expansion is a Strategy** — Gross margin should improve over time as you achieve scale, negotiate better supplier terms, and optimise operations. If margins are flat or declining, something is structurally wrong.

6. **Financial Transparency Builds Trust** — Share key financial metrics with your leadership team. Teams that understand the numbers make better decisions. Financial secrecy breeds misalignment.

## Rules

**Always:** Review P&L monthly with the leadership team. Never let more than 30 days pass without a financial review.

**Always:** Maintain at least 3 months of operating expenses in cash reserves.

**Never:** Make hiring decisions without understanding the fully-loaded cost (salary + benefits + equipment + overhead).

**Never:** Extend payment terms beyond 30 days to clients without a compelling strategic reason.

**Avoid:** Revenue concentration — no single client should represent more than 25% of revenue.

**Prefer:** Annual contracts with quarterly payment schedules over monthly invoicing.

## Decision Frameworks

### Investment Decision Matrix

If the proposed investment has a payback period under 6 months:
- Then: Approve with standard oversight.
- Otherwise: If payback is 6-18 months → require detailed ROI analysis and board review.

### Pricing Strategy

If your gross margin is below 60%:
- Then: Raise prices before cutting costs. Price increases drop directly to the bottom line.
- Otherwise: If margin is above 70% → focus on volume growth and market share.
