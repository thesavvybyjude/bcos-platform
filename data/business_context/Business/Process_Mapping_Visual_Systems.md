# Process Mapping & Visual Systems

## Purpose
To provide a framework for creating visual process maps that serve as blueprints for your business, enabling collaboration, clarity, and continuous improvement.

## Core Philosophy

> "Trying to build a business without process maps is like building a skyscraper without blueprints."

## Principles

### 1. Visual > Written for Knowledge Transfer
- A picture is worth a thousand words
- Visual maps are easier to understand, collaborate on, and update than text-only documents
- They serve as a "blueprint" before building detailed SOPs

### 2. Start with What's Bothering You Most
- Don't try to map everything at once
- Begin with the process that causes the most pain or confusion
- Momentum builds from solving real problems

### 3. Share Immediately, Even If Messy
- Collaboration is the primary benefit of process maps
- A process map that isn't shared isn't a process map — it's just a drawing
- Perfection is the enemy of progress

### 4. Process Maps Are Fluid
- Treat them as living documents, not final artifacts
- Update them as the business evolves
- Stale maps are worse than no maps

## Decision Frameworks

### Two Types of Process Maps

```
1. PROTOTYPE MAP (Discovery)
│
├── Purpose: Discover and iterate on the process
├── Characteristics: Messy, collaborative, includes current state + improvements + challenges
├── Tools: Whiteboard, posted notes, pen and paper, Lucid Chart/Miro
├── Goal: Get everything out of heads and onto paper
└── When: Before writing any SOP

2. DOCUMENTATION MAP (Final Blueprint)
│
├── Purpose: Show the final, agreed-upon process
├── Characteristics: Clean, concise, no fluff or what-ifs
├── Tools: Lucid Chart, Miro (digital)
├── Goal: A reusable blueprint for training and execution
└── When: After the prototype map is validated
```

### The Company Brain

A complete visual system with three components:

```
COMPANY BRAIN
├── 1. ORG CHART
│   ├── All employees and departments
│   ├── Reporting lines
│   └── Role definitions
│
├── 2. DATA INFRASTRUCTURE
│   ├── All entities (clients, brands, offers, vendors, contractors)
│   ├── Relationships between entities
│   └── How data flows through the business
│
└── 3. PROCESS MAPS
    ├── One tab per subprocess
    ├── Onboarding, sales, fulfillment, etc.
    └── The full workflow visualised
```

### Process Map vs SOP vs Checklist

```
LEVEL 1: PROCESS MAP (Visual Blueprint)
├── Shows: What, Who, When
├── Format: Flowchart with swim lanes
├── Purpose: Big picture understanding
└── Example: "How a lead becomes a customer"

LEVEL 2: SOP (Detailed Instructions)
├── Shows: How (step-by-step)
├── Format: Written document
├── Purpose: Detailed execution guide
└── Example: "How to send the welcome email sequence"

LEVEL 3: CHECKLIST (Quick Verification)
├── Shows: Critical steps only
├── Format: Checkbox list
├── Purpose: Quality assurance
└── Example: "Onboarding completion checklist"
```

### Best Tools for Process Mapping

```
LUCID CHART
├── Enterprise-grade
├── Good for larger teams
├── Strong sharing and permissions
└── Integrates with other tools

MIRO
├── Collaborative whiteboard
├── Good for prototyping
├── Real-time collaboration
└── Visual and intuitive

KEY REQUIREMENTS:
├── Shareability — team must be able to access and edit
├── Security/permissions — control who can view vs edit
└── Version history — track changes over time
```

## Rules

- **Always start with a prototype map** before writing any SOP
- **Always use swim lanes** to show who does what
- **Always share immediately** — don't wait for perfection
- **Always include decision points** (diamonds) to show branching logic
- **Never try to map everything at once** — start with what's bothering you most
- **Never keep maps to yourself** — collaboration is the benefit
- **Never treat maps as final** — they are living documents
- **Never skip the visual step** — going straight to SOPs creates confusion

## Heuristics

- If you can't draw it on a whiteboard, you don't understand the process well enough
- If the team isn't using the maps, they weren't co-created
- If the maps are never updated, they're outdated
- If you're writing an SOP without a map first, you're skipping the blueprint
- If new hires can't understand the process from the map, it's too complex
- If the map has more than 20 steps, break it into sub-processes

## Templates

### Process Map Quick-Start Template

```
PROCESS NAME: _______________
START: What triggers this process?
END: What is the output?

STEPS (draw as boxes):
1. _______________
2. _______________
3. _______________
4. _______________
5. _______________

DECISIONS (draw as diamonds):
- If X, then _______________
- If Y, then _______________

WHO DOES WHAT (swim lanes):
├── Person/Role 1: Steps ___, ___
├── Person/Role 2: Steps ___, ___
└── Person/Role 3: Steps ___, ___

IMPROVEMENT OPPORTUNITIES:
□ _______________
□ _______________
□ _______________
```

### Process Documentation Checklist

```
□ Process name and purpose defined
□ Start and end points clear
□ All steps documented in sequence
□ Decision points identified
□ Responsible person assigned (swim lanes)
□ Quality checkpoints defined
□ Exceptions documented
□ Shared with team for feedback
□ Updated based on feedback
□ Version controlled
```
