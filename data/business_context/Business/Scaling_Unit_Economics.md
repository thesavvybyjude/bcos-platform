# Scaling & Unit Economics

## Purpose
To provide a framework for understanding what makes a business scalable, how to calculate unit economics, and how to design systems that enable growth without the founder's constant involvement.

## Core Philosophy

> "Scaling is a system, not a feeling."

## Principles

### 1. Scaling = System, Not Feeling
- Entrepreneurs hit plateaus because they don't change how they operate
- What worked at 1 person doesn't work at 5, 15, or 50
- The solution: build systems, not just work harder
- Feelings don't scale; systems do

### 2. Customer Avatar Based on Unit Economics
- Define your ideal customer based on unit economics, not feelings or assumptions
- Must be impersonal and criteria-based
- You are selecting a customer, not a life partner
- Budget is a key component: what can your customer afford?

### 3. Fixed Costs Are Your Friend
- Fixed costs make scaling profitable
- When CAC is fixed, each additional customer increases margin
- Variable CAC makes scaling difficult and unpredictable

### 4. Mass Customisation Over Mass Production
- Mass Production: One product, many buyers (notes for everyone)
- Mass Customisation: One product, scalable support (workshops for many simultaneously)
- One-to-many beats one-to-one for scalability

### 5. Stop Features Silently
- If no one complains when you remove a feature, it wasn't valuable
- Test value by subtraction, not addition
- The best features are the ones people miss when they're gone

## Decision Frameworks

### Unit Economics: CAC vs LTV

```
CUSTOMER ACQUISITION COST (CAC):
│
├── Total cost to acquire one customer
├── Includes: marketing, sales, free products/services given away
├── Formula: CAC = Total Acquisition Spend / Number of New Customers
└── Goal: Keep CAC fixed as you scale

LIFETIME VALUE (LTV):
│
├── Total revenue a customer generates over their entire relationship
├── Formula: LTV = Average Purchase Value × Frequency × Lifespan
├── Goal: LTV > CAC (ideally 3x+)
└── Higher LTV = more you can spend on acquisition

SCALABILITY RULE:
│
├── A business is scalable when CAC is FIXED and LTV > CAC
├── If CAC is variable, scaling becomes difficult
├── Break-even = Fixed CAC / (LTV - Variable Cost per Customer)
└── After break-even, every customer is pure profit
```

### Break-Even Analysis

```
EXAMPLE: Fricki's Tutoring Business

FIXED COSTS:
├── Cost to create notes: R7,000 (one-time, sunk)
└── Total fixed cost: R7,000

VARIABLE REVENUE:
├── Workshop price: R200/module
├── Modules per customer: 6/semester × 2 semesters = 12
├── LTV = R200 × 12 = R2,400
└── Variable cost per customer: ~R0 (digital delivery)

BREAK-EVEN:
├── Break-even customers = Fixed Costs / Price per Unit
├── Break-even = R7,000 / R200 = 35 customers
└── After 35 customers: every customer is pure profit

THE MATH:
├── 35 customers × R200 = R7,000 (break-even)
├── 50 customers × R200 = R10,000 (R3,000 profit)
├── 100 customers × R200 = R20,000 (R13,000 profit)
└── 500 customers × R200 = R100,000 (R93,000 profit)

THIS IS THE DIFFERENCE BETWEEN A BUSINESS AND A HOBBY.
```

### Customer Avatar Framework

```
STEP 1: DEFINE THE ECONOMICS
│
├── What is your CAC? (How much to acquire one customer?)
├── What is your LTV? (How much will they spend over time?)
├── What is your break-even point?
└── What budget does your customer have?

STEP 2: DEFINE THE PROFILE
│
├── Demographics (age, location, income)
├── Psychographics (values, interests, pain points)
├── Behaviour (where they hang out, what they buy)
└── Budget (what can they afford?)

STEP 3: MAKE IT IMPERSONAL
│
├── Criteria must be objective and economic
├── "Does this customer make my business viable?"
├── Not: "Do I like this customer?"
└── Not: "Is this customer like me?"

THE RULE:
└── "You are selecting a customer, not a life partner."
```

### Lead Qualification Automation

```
THE PROBLEM:
│
├── Founders manually qualify leads
├── This is inefficient, emotional, and unscalable
└── Human intervention = bottleneck

THE SOLUTION:
│
├── Define qualification criteria (based on avatar + unit economics)
├── Automate the process (ManyChat, Privia, CRM)
├── The system qualifies, not the person
└── No human intervention for initial qualification

WORKFLOW:
│
├── 1. User comments "mentor" on social post
├── 2. Automation sends standardised DM
├── 3. User responds, triggering a script
├── 4. System checks criteria (budget, need, timing)
├── 5. If criteria met → moves to CRM as qualified lead
└── 6. If not → automated decline

CRITERIA MUST BE IMPERSONAL:
├── Budget: Can they afford it? (Yes/No)
├── Need: Do they have the problem you solve? (Yes/No)
├── Timing: Are they ready to buy now? (Yes/No)
├── Authority: Can they make the purchase decision? (Yes/No)
└── No subjective criteria allowed
```

### TAM (Total Addressable Market) Analysis

```
STEP 1: DEFINE YOUR MARKET
│
├── Who is your customer? (use avatar)
├── What problem do you solve?
└── What is the total number of potential customers?

STEP 2: CALCULATE TAM
│
├── TAM = Total potential customers × Average LTV
├── Example: 100,000 students × R2,400 LTV = R240,000,000 TAM
└── This is the maximum revenue opportunity

STEP 3: ESTIMATE MARKET CAPTURE
│
├── Year 1: 1% of TAM = R2,400,000
├── Year 3: 5% of TAM = R12,000,000
├── Year 5: 10% of TAM = R24,000,000
└── Be realistic; most businesses capture <5%

STEP 4: VALIDATE FEASIBILITY
│
├── Can you reach 1% of TAM in Year 1?
├── What channels will you use?
├── What is the cost to reach them?
└── Does the unit economics work at scale?
```

### Defensibility (The Moat)

When scaling, ensure your business is defensible:

#### Types of Defensibility
1. **IP** — Patents, proprietary technology.
2. **Switching Costs** — Hard for customers to leave.
3. **Network Effects** — Value increases with more users.
4. **Data** — More data = better product = more data (flywheel).
5. **Contracts** — Long-term agreements lock in customers.
6. **Brand** — Strong brand recognition.

**Question:** What makes it hard for competitors to copy you?

#### The 3 D's (Beyond Faster/Better/Cheaper)
- **Disruptive** — Changes how an industry works.
- **Discontinuous** — Enables what couldn't be done before.
- **Defensible** — Creates a lasting moat.

**Rule:** "Faster, better, cheaper" is dangerous — incumbents have more resources. Find your 3 D's.

## Rules

- **Always calculate CAC and LTV first** — before anything else
- **Always keep CAC fixed** — variable CAC kills scalability
- **Always make qualification impersonal** — economic criteria, not feelings
- **Always test features by subtraction** — stop silently, watch for complaints
- **Always calculate break-even** — know how many customers you need
- **Never scale before unit economics work** — you'll just lose money faster
- **Never qualify leads manually if you can automate it** — human intervention is the enemy of scale
- **Never assume your customer is like you** — they're not

## Heuristics

- If CAC is variable, you don't have a scalable business
- If LTV < CAC, you're losing money on every customer
- If you can't calculate break-even, you don't understand your business
- If qualification is emotional, you're picking friends, not customers
- If features don't get complaints when removed, they weren't valuable

### Lead Qualification Script

```
STEP 1: INITIAL CONTACT
"Hi [Name], thanks for reaching out. I'd love to learn more about your situation."

STEP 2: QUALIFY BUDGET
"What's your budget for solving this problem?"
├── Meets criteria? → Continue
└── Doesn't meet? → "I appreciate your interest, but this might not be the right fit."

STEP 3: QUALIFY NEED
"Tell me about the specific problem you're trying to solve."
├── Matches your solution? → Continue
└── Doesn't match? → "That's actually outside our expertise."

STEP 4: QUALIFY TIMING
"Are you looking to start now, or is this more of a future consideration?"
├── Ready now? → Continue
└── Not ready? → "Let's connect when the timing is better."

STEP 5: QUALIFY AUTHORITY
"Will you be the one making the purchase decision, or is there someone else involved?"
├── Decision maker? → Move to close
└── Not decision maker? → "Would it make sense to include them in our next conversation?"

RESULT: Qualified (4/4) → Proceed | Not Qualified → Automated decline
```
