# Self Awareness

## Purpose
To provide a framework for founder self-awareness.

## Core Philosophy
**The greatest leadership asset is knowing yourself.**

- Your strengths are where you should focus
- Your weaknesses are where you should hire
- Self-awareness is not a personality trait; it is a practice
- The founder is the bottleneck

## Principles

### 1. Know Your Strengths
- What are you uniquely good at?
- What do you do that no one else on the team can do?
- Double down on your strengths
- Delegate the rest

### 2. Know Your Weaknesses
- What do you suck at?
- What do you avoid?
- What drains your energy?
- Hire for your weaknesses

### 3. Stay in Your Lane
- The most dangerous founders are the ones who think they are good at everything
- Focus on what you are best at
- Let others do the rest
- Great founders are great delegators

### 4. Continuous Self-Reflection
- Self-awareness is not a one-time thing
- It requires constant reflection
- Ask yourself: What am I doing well? What am I avoiding?
- The more self-aware you are, the better your decisions

## Process

### Self-Awareness Practice:
```
1. Write down your strengths
2. Write down your weaknesses
3. Ask your team for honest feedback
4. Double down on strengths
5. Hire for weaknesses
6. Revisit quarterly
7. Stay in your lane
```

## Decision Framework

```
Am I Playing to My Strengths?
  |
  +-- Am I spending time on what I am good at?
  |   +-- YES -> Good
  |   +-- NO -> Need to shift
  |
  +-- Have I hired for my weaknesses?
  |   +-- YES -> Good
  |   +-- NO -> Need to hire
  |
  +-- Do I know what my team thinks of me?
  |   +-- YES -> Good
  |   +-- NO -> Ask them
  |
  +-- Am I the bottleneck?
      +-- NO -> Good
      +-- YES -> Need to delegate
```

## Rules

### Always:
- Know your strengths
- Know your weaknesses
- Hire for your weaknesses
- Stay in your lane

### Never:
- Think you are good at everything
- Ignore your weaknesses
- Spend time on things you are bad at
- Be the bottleneck
