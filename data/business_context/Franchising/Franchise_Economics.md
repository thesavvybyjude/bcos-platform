# Franchise_Economics.md

## Purpose
To provide frameworks for franchise unit economics.

## Core Philosophy
**A healthy franchise generates 15-18% EBITDA.**

- Franchise must be profitable
- Payback period should be 12-18 months
- Multiple unit operators are more profitable
- Economics vary by market

## Principles

**1. Revenue Model**
```
Franchise Revenue
  |
  |- Franchise Fee (one-time, $20-50k+)
  |- Royalties (5-12% of revenue monthly)
  |- Marketing Fund (1-3% of revenue monthly)
```

**2. Healthy EBITDA Target: 15-18%**
- Rent should be max 15% of revenue
- Labor optimized (simple operations)
- COGS controlled through verticalization
- Economics vary by market but bottom line must work

**3. Payback Period**
- US market: 12-18 months typical
- Some markets: 2-6 months possible
- Location-dependent
- Should guide franchisee expectations

## Benchmark Table

| Metric | Target | Notes |
|--------|--------|-------|
| Rent % Revenue | ≤15% | Maximum healthy level |
| EBITDA Margin | 15-18% | Healthy franchise operation |
| Payback Period | 12-18 months | US market standard |
| Startup Cost | $200-300K | Simple operations advantage |

## Process

**Franchise Economics Evaluation:**
```
1. Calculate total revenue potential
2. Estimate fixed costs (rent, labor, COGS)
3. Determine EBITDA (15-18% target)
4. Calculate payback period
5. Validate with market data
6. Adjust for location
7. Set expectations with franchisee
8. Monitor performance
```

## Decision Framework

```
Is This Franchise Viable?
  |
  |- Is EBITDA >15%?
  |   |- YES → Viable
  |   |- NO → Need optimization
  |
  |- Is payback <18 months?
  |   |- YES → Strong franchise
  |   |- NO → Need adjustment
  |
  |- Is rent ≤15% of revenue?
  |   |- YES → Good location economics
  |   |- NO → Too expensive
  |
  |- Can the franchisee achieve target?
      |- YES → Proceed
      |- NO → Need different model
```

## Rules

**Always:**
- Target 15-18% EBITDA
- Keep rent under 15% revenue
- Provide realistic payback expectations
- Monitor franchisee P&L

**Never:**
- Overpromise returns
- Underestimate operational costs
- Ignore market differences
- Sell unprofitable franchises
