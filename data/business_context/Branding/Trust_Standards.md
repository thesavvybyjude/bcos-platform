# Trust_Standards.md

## Purpose
To provide a framework for building trust in low-trust categories.

## Core Philosophy
**In a low-trust category, high trust is a competitive advantage.**

- The supplement industry is low-trust
- High trust brands win
- Trust is built through standards
- Trust is protected through decisions

## Principles

### 1. High Trust in Low Trust Categories
```
Low Trust Category
  │
  ├─ Unregulated
  ├─ Many bad actors
  └─ Consumers are skeptical

High Trust Brand
  │
  ├─ NSF Certified for Sport
  ├─ Transparent sourcing
  ├─ Clinical research
  └─ Partners with trusted experts
```

### 2. The Momentous Standard
- Science is the first pillar
- Sourcing is the second pillar
- Certification is the third pillar
- Prove what you're sourcing is right

### 3. The Science First Approach
- Start with the science
- What does the data say?
- Is this the right product to have?
- If the science doesn't evolve, cut it

### 4. The Fidosia Decision
- Launched with early clinical data (mice studies)
- Science didn't evolve over 2 years
- Product became a "goose"
- Cut it to protect the brand
- Anchored the team, customers, and investors

## Process

### Trust Building Framework:
```
1. Start with science (clinical data)
2. Source the highest quality ingredients
3. Certify everything (NSF Certified for Sport)
4. If a product doesn't meet standards → Cut it
5. Communicate decisions transparently
6. Reinforce standards with every decision
7. Trust compounds over time
```

## Decision Framework

```
Does This Product Meet Our Trust Standards?
  │
  ├─ Is the science solid?
  │   ├─ YES → Continue
  │   └─ NO → Don't launch (or cut)
  │
  ├─ Is the sourcing high quality?
  │   ├─ YES → Continue
  │   └─ NO → Fix or cut
  │
  ├─ Is it certified (NSF)?
  │   ├─ YES → Continue
  │   └─ NO → Get certified or cut
  │
  └─ Is it "duck, duck, duck" or "goose"?
      ├─ Duck → Keep
      └─ Goose → Cut
```

## Rules

**Always:**
- Start with science
- Source the highest quality
- Certify everything
- Cut what doesn't fit

**Never:**
- Compromise on science
- Source low quality
- Skip certification
- Keep a "goose" in your portfolio
