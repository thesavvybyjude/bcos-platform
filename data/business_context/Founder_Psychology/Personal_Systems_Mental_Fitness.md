# Personal Systems & Mental Fitness

## Purpose
To provide frameworks for building intentional personal and family systems, developing mental fitness, and creating habits that support both personal and professional success.

## Core Philosophy

> "It takes eight years to build a brand."

> "Consistency, then intensity, then technique. But people jump to technique because they feel like it's easier."

## Principles

### 1. The 12 Pursuits Framework
- 12 months, 12 focus areas (6 physical, 6 mental)
- Each month: bring in an expert, give challenges, offer rewards
- The idea: teach people to create good, healthy habits
- Community-based setting creates accountability

### 2. Consistency, then Intensity, then Technique
- First: Build the habit (consistency)
- Then: Add intensity
- Finally: Refine technique
- People jump to technique because it feels easier, but habit comes first

### 3. Simplicity is the Answer
- Complexity is the enemy
- 80% of health and wellness is based on very simple, consecutive habits
- We're wired to look for quick fixes, but that's a trap

### 4. The Benjamin Franklin Principle
- 13 virtues, 4 weeks each (13 goes into 52 evenly)
- Focus on one thing at a time for meaningful improvement
- Self-improvement is overwhelming if you try to do everything at once

### 5. The Key Habit: Sleep
- Going to bed early is the key habit that unlocks everything
- Wake up early doesn't mean anything if you don't know when you went to bed
- 8+ hours of sleep is the foundation

### 6. The First Things First Principle (Steven Covey)
- Big jar: tennis balls (big priorities), ping pong balls, marbles, sand
- Start with the tennis balls, everything else fits
- Start with marbles, nothing else fits

### 7. Atomic Habits Framework
- The answer is not goal setting; it's daily practices
- Example: 10 minutes of organizing per day instead of "never have a messy desk"
- Consistency, intensity, technique applies here too

### 8. Nutrition: Simple Caloric Discipline (Added by Manny Lubin)
- Weight loss is simple: "eat less, move more"
- The challenge is in the execution, not the knowledge
- Example: Manny ate two meals a day for two years, lost 65 lbs
- Simple, but not easy — requires consistency and discipline
- "It really is that simple, you don't have to do anything crazy"

### 9. The Diet Execution Problem (Added by Manny Lubin)
- "Everyone can diet and lose weight — and then you have to continue to do that"
- The issue isn't knowing what to do, it's doing it consistently
- Example: Keto works, but you have to keep doing it
- "It's like you're going to be on this diet for the rest of your life"
- Solution: Find a sustainable approach you can maintain

### 10. The Caloric Discipline Framework (Added by Manny Lubin)
- CALORIC CEILING: Must stay below it (e.g., 2,200 calories when cutting)
- PROTEIN FLOOR: Must stay above it (e.g., 150-200g per day Monday-Thursday)
- THE CHALLENGE: If you want 150-200g protein but don't want 3,000-5,000 calories, you have to go with drinks
- WHY DRINKS WIN: Bars = 20g protein, 200-240 cal; Drinks = 20g protein, 100 cal
- More protein per calorie = more efficient

### 11. Personal Habits Framework (Added by Manny Lubin)
- Same thing, same time, every day
- Rotate brands, not the routine
- Simplicity is the answer
- NUTRITION STAPLES: Slate (protein drinks), Zia (soda at night), Oli Pop/Poppi (sodas), Yo protein bars, Bear Bells/Quest/Atlas bars, Zero sugar yogurts, Cottage cheese (macros)
- Find what works for you (some prefer cottage cheese, some prefer Greek yogurt)

### 8. Annual Family Goal Setting
- Treat your family like a business: long-range planning, budgeting, forecasting
- 3-4 day annual couples trip with structured agenda
- Set family goals (e.g., learn Spanish, no sugar for a year)
- Reward for achieving the goal

### 9. The Power of Breath Work
- Most overlooked practice
- Double inhale (Huberman), box breathing
- Always available, free, 5 minutes or less
- Can help with sleep, fitness, relationships, mental health

### 10. The Jar Analogy
- Tennis balls (first things), ping pong balls, marbles, sand
- Put first things first, then everything else fits
- Chase the small things, and you have no room for what matters

## Decision Frameworks

### The 12 Pursuits Program Framework

```
12 MONTHS, 12 FOCUS AREAS:

JANUARY: Progress starts here (Perspective and Vision)
FEBRUARY: Sleep
MARCH: Hydration
APRIL: Breath Work
MAY: Nutrition
JUNE: Move
JULY: Go Outdoors
AUGUST: Learn
SEPTEMBER: Social
OCTOBER: Self-Care
NOVEMBER: Gratitude
DECEMBER: Serve

HOW IT WORKS:
├── Bring in an expert to speak
├── Give a challenge (e.g., "fill out the big calendar")
├── Offer rewards for completion
├── Community Slack channel for accountability
└── Stack habits over time
```

### Consistency to Intensity to Technique Framework

```
CONSISTENCY (Start Here):
└── "Move your body for 30 minutes a day, 5-6 days a week"

INTENSITY (Add After Consistency):
└── "Now push harder, go faster, lift heavier"

TECHNIQUE (Finally):
└── "Now refine form, optimize, get the 1% improvements"

WHY THIS ORDER:
├── People jump to technique because it feels like an easy solution
├── Technique is worthless without the habit
└── "Build the habit, then you can lean into it"
```

### The Key Habit Framework

```
IDENTIFY THE KEY HABIT:
└── The one habit that unlocks all the others

EXAMPLE:
├── Key habit: Going to bed early
│   └── Wake up early
│   └── Morning routine
│   └── Workout
│   └── Productive day
│   └── Sleep early again

WHY IT WORKS:
└── Wake up early is the wrong focus
└── Going to bed early drives waking up early
└── Address the root, not the symptom
```

### Annual Family Goal Setting Framework

```
ANNUAL COUPLES TRIP (3-4 Days):
├── Whiteboard or flipchart
├── Long-range planning
├── Budgeting and forecasting
└── Same structured agenda every year

FAMILY GOALS:
├── "Learn Spanish this year"
├── "No sugar for a year" (with cheat days)
└── Reward at end (trip to Peru)

FAMILY CULTURE:
├── Family cheer (nightly)
├── Core values
└── "Intention with your life brings richness"
```

### Breath Work Framework

```
THE MOST OVERLOOKED PRACTICE:

WHY IT MATTERS:
├── 10-15% gains on the same lifts with proper breathing
├── Improves sleep quality
├── Reduces anxiety (heart rate down before meetings/podcasts)
└── Always available, zero cost

TECHNIQUES:
├── Double inhale (Huberman method)
├── Box breathing (in 4, hold 4, out 4, hold 4)
└── Mouth breathing is harmful

IMPLEMENTATION:
└── 5 minutes or less
└── Not meditation or 20-minute sessions
└── Be mindful of breath walking into a meeting, driving, etc.
```

## Rules

- **Always build the habit before adding intensity or technique** — Consistency first
- **Always identify the key habit that unlocks everything** — For Nate, it's sleep
- **Always put first things first** — Tennis balls in the jar analogy
- **Always plan for the year with intention** — Couples trip, 12 pursuits
- **Never skip breath work** — It's free, always available, and overlooked
- **Never chase the quick fix** — 80% of health is simple, consecutive habits

## Heuristics

- If you're struggling to wake up early, go to bed earlier
- Self-improvement is overwhelming if you try to do everything at once
- Complexity is the enemy; simplicity is the answer
- The person carrying the product matters more than the product itself
- People want to help you if you just ask


### Simon Squibb — Embrace Failure

- Learn to lose those things and not care
- Let your short-term ego go
- Don't worry about driving the right car to give an image to people
- Learn to enjoy looking like a loser
- Let people underestimate you—it's powerful
- "A students end up working for D students because A students are scared to lose"

### Simon Squibb — Learn to Be a D Student

- Love to fail
- Take your time
- Don't let someone else decide whether you're successful or not
- Power up your internal ego that tells you you're not a loser

### Simon Squibb — Hack Luck

1. **Be persistent**—outlast other people (purpose and passion help with this)
2. **Know your destination**—what is success for you?
3. **Learn to take risk**—lean into fear, learn to love fear

### Simon Squibb — The Secret to It All

- Learn to accept that you could lose everything and it doesn't matter
- You've got to be willing to risk it all
- "Do not rush. Do not see this as anything but a marathon. It's not a sprint."

### Simon Squibb — The 3% Rule

- Your dream is often just a 3% difference from your existing life
- 3% difference between chimpanzees and humans
- You might be just 3% away from living your purpose

### Simon Squibb — Delayed Gratification

- Don't rush to charge customers
- First customer: do the work for free
- Build relationships, over-deliver, create advocates
- Facebook and Instagram built massive user bases before monetizing
- "Do not think about making money straight up. Build a brand not a business."

### Systems Thinking — The Three Principles of Personal Systems

```
PRINCIPLE 1: THINK HOLISTICALLY
    │
    ├── What are all the factors that could influence success?
    ├── What have you tried before and why didn't it work?
    └── Expect the plan to fail and plan for it

PRINCIPLE 2: BUILD FOR REPEATABILITY
    │
    ├── Does it rely on willpower or motivation?
    ├── Will it work on the worst day?
    └── Is there as little friction as possible?

PRINCIPLE 3: PEEL THE BAND-AIDS
    │
    ├── What temporary fixes are you using? (Naps, timers, shortcuts)
    ├── What's the underlying issue? (Sleep, attention span)
    └── How can you solve the root cause?
```

### Systems Thinking — The Discomfort Scale

```
UNCOMFORTABLE (Making Change)
    │
    └── Changing nighttime routine
    └── Going to bed earlier
    └── Staying at work to study
    │
    ▼
MORE UNCOMFORTABLE (Not Changing)
    │
    └── Constant stress and pressure
    └── Disappointment from failing
    └── Never making meaningful progress

The discomfort of change is usually less than the discomfort of staying the same.
```
