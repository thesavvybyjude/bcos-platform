# Business_Models.md

## Purpose
To provide a framework for evaluating business models.

## Core Philosophy
**The best businesses are consumable with strong LTV, small SKU count, massive TAM, and 75%+ margins.**

- Fashion is the worst category
- Health/wellness with one SKU is the best
- Retail: Target, Walmart, Costco matter
- Everything else is noise

## Principles

**1. The Perfect Business Model**
```
Checklist:
  1. Consumable (or strong LTV)
  2. Strong LTV
  3. Small SKU count
  4. Massive TAM
  5. 75%+ gross margins
  6. Mass market retail potential
  7. Vanity/health element
```

**2. The Grunes Example**
- One flavor (gummies)
- Anybody can eat them
- Subscription-first (predictable supply chain)
- Domestic supply chain (no tariffs)
- Target, Walmart, Costco

**3. The Fashion Trap**
- Worst category to be in
- People will do it for free (rich parents)
- Wholesale accounts are struggling
- Nordstrom's: Taken private, restructuring
- Saks: Not paying vendors

**4. The Peter Teal Lesson**
- If you want to work really hard in a knife fight and make no money, open a restaurant
- Monopolies are good
- Competition is for losers
- Pick the right game

## Decision Framework

```
Is This a Good Business Model?
  │
  ├─ Is it consumable?
  │   ├─ YES → Good
  │   └─ NO → Harder
  │
  ├─ Is LTV strong?
  │   ├─ YES → Good
  │   └─ NO → Harder
  │
  ├─ Is there a large TAM?
  │   ├─ YES → Good
  │   └─ NO → Limited upside
  │
  ├─ Are margins 75%+?
  │   ├─ YES → Good
  │   └─ NO → Harder
  │
  ├─ Can it sell in Target/Walmart/Costco?
  │   ├─ YES → Good
  │   └─ NO → Limited scale
  │
  └─ Is there a health/vanity element?
      ├─ YES → Good
      └─ NO → Harder
```

## Rules

**Always:**
- Pick the right game
- Target mass market retail
- Keep SKU count small
- Aim for 75%+ margins

**Never:**
- Enter fashion
- Build a business without LTV
- Have bloated SKU counts
- Ignore mass market retail
