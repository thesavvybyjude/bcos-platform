# Podcast as Business Development Tool

## Purpose
To provide a framework for using podcasting as a strategic business development tool for fundraising, hiring, relationship building, and brand building.

## Core Philosophy

> "If I was famous, they would not have [__] with me with that term sheet."

> "Build a podcast. If you can't provide value for a very specific group of people, you'll never be able to grow."

## Principles

### 1. Fame = Protection in Business
- When you're a well-known founder in your industry, there's reputational risk to screwing you
- Investors buy into the myth as much as fans do
- Visibility creates a level of respect that anonymity doesn't

### 2. The Origin Story: Why D Started a Podcast
- 2016: Took last business for exit, got term sheet from Carlyle, they backed out last minute
- Devastated, realized if he was "famous" they wouldn't have done that
- "I have to become famous. What do I got to do to become famous?"

### 3. Podcast as Hiring Tool
- Hired so many listeners at Ghost and other companies
- Job candidates listen to all episodes to get insights for interviews
- "People will meet me, then start listening to the podcast—that's retention for the business too"

### 4. Podcast as Sales Tool
- The energy level, body language, and general excitement convey across to the other person
- What you learn doing a podcast—game face on, high energy—applies to sales and fundraising
- "If I talk at a normal tone in my office it's boring. When I do a podcast I'm like all right game face on."

### 5. Podcast as Thought Leadership
- "I'm a thought leader in my industry now"
- When he started Ghost, listeners were like "something's happening at ghost"
- You can drip concepts through the podcast before you announce

### 6. The Tagline Framework
- "Make your cool friend smarter and your smart friends cooler"
- A clear tagline gives people a reason to listen
- It defines the value proposition

### 7. The 1% Rule
- 1% of people make it past 21 episodes (D has done 97+)
- Consistency is the key differentiator
- "You have to make a commitment out the gate because it's very frustrating to start"

### 8. Niche Down to Go Deep
- If starting a podcast today: hyper-niche and really focused
- Everyone is going shallow—the whole world wants to give 5-second opinions
- Going deep is the competitive advantage

## Decision Frameworks

### The "Become Famous" Framework

```
THE PROBLEM:
+-- Anonymous founders get screwed in deals
+-- No reputational risk to back out

THE SOLUTION:
+-- Build visibility in your industry
+-- Become a thought leader
+-- Create reputational risk

THE TOOL:
+-- Podcasting (or any content platform)

THE RESULT:
+-- Investors treat you differently
+-- Partners think twice before backing out
+-- You become a "mythical creature" in your space
```

### Podcast Niche Framework

```
IF STARTING A PODCAST TODAY:
+-- Pick a hyper-niche
+-- Go deep, not shallow
+-- Provide value for a very specific group of people

WHY:
+-- Everyone is going shallow (5-second opinions)
+-- Going deep is the competitive advantage
+-- First customer → second → 10th → 100th

THE 100 TRUE FANS CONCEPT:
+-- If you have 100 people listening, you won
+-- 100 engaged people is incredibly valuable
+-- A thousand true fans can build any business
```

### Podcast Preparation Framework

```
PREPARATION IS SIMPLE:
+-- Throughout the week, save articles you read
+-- Send them to everyone in the morning
+-- "This is what we're going to talk about"

WHAT RESONATES:
+-- Know what your audience hates (e.g., politics)
+-- Blend business/finance + pop culture
+-- People want to stay connected to the culture

FOR BUSINESS PODCASTS:
+-- Deep and narrow, not wide and shallow
+-- Strictly operators, not influencers/entertainers
```

### Podcast Value Framework

```
VALUE CHANNELS:
+-- Hiring: Listeners become employees
+-- Fundraising: Listeners become investors
+-- Sales: Partners become listeners (retention)
+-- Thought Leadership: "Something's happening at ghost"
+-- Personal Development: Communication, energy, sales skills

THE ATTRIBUTION PROBLEM:
+-- No multi-touch attribution tool truly tells you where it comes from
+-- But the impact is undeniable
+-- People listen to get insights for interviews
```

## Rules

- **Always have a clear tagline** — "Make your cool friend smarter and your smart friends cooler"
- **Always commit to a year minimum** — It's frustrating to start; you need time
- **Always go hyper-niche** — Everyone is shallow; deep is the advantage
- **Always prepare by saving articles** — No over-preparation needed
- **Always give instant feedback** — What resonates and what doesn't
- **Never talk about politics** — It's polarizing and drives listeners away
- **Never let the podcast disrupt work** — Do it at night or weekends

## Heuristics

- If you're a founder, your podcast is a business development tool, not just content
- 100 engaged listeners is more valuable than 1M passive views
- People listen to your podcast to learn about you before they work with you
- The energy you bring on a podcast is the energy you bring to sales meetings

## Triggers

### When Considering a Podcast:

```
Thinking about starting a podcast

-> Ask: "Can I provide value for a very specific group of people?"
-> Ask: "Can I commit to at least one year?"
-> Ask: "What's my tagline?"

If yes to all: Start.
If no: Don't.
```

### When Preparing for a Podcast:

```
You have a podcast episode coming up

-> Throughout the week: Save articles you read
-> Morning of: Send to everyone
-> "This is what we're going to talk about"
-> If they want to read it, great. If not, I'll give my opinion.
```

## Failure Modes

1. **Not having a clear tagline** — People don't know what they're getting
2. **Not committing to a year** — It's frustrating to start; 1% make it past 21 episodes
3. **Going too broad** — Everyone is shallow; deep is the advantage
4. **Letting the podcast disrupt work** — Do it at night or weekends
5. **Talking about politics** — Polarizing and drives listeners away
