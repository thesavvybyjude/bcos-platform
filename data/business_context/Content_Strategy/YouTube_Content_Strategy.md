# YouTube & Content Strategy

## Purpose
To provide comprehensive frameworks for growing on YouTube, creating compelling packaging, building audience trust, and monetizing through sponsorships and education.

## Core Philosophy

> "YouTube is not a video platform. It's a click and watch platform."

> "If they don't click, they don't watch."

## The Three Rules of YouTube

### Rule 1: If They Don't Click, They Don't Watch
- The packaging (title + thumbnail) is everything
- You must instill enough curiosity that they have "no choice but to click"
- 75-80% of your time should be spent on packaging

### Rule 2: Respect Their Time
- Confirm the click in the first 7 seconds
- Make it personal in the next 20 seconds
- Open a new loop in the final 10 seconds of the intro

### Rule 3: Give Them More
- Returning viewers are the most important metric
- Make content that builds community, not just views
- Memorable views > Empty views

## Principles

### 1. The Curiosity Gap
- Distance between information presented and information they want to know
- Title + thumbnail should open the gap, not close it

### 2. Click-Through Rate (CTR) is Everything
- Under 5% on a new video = re-evaluate
- 5-10% is the sweet spot
- CTR + Retention + Returning Viewers = YouTube satisfaction

### 3. Views ≠ Brand
- 44M views on a short ≠ building a brand
- Three categories of views:
  - **Forgettable:** Watched and immediately forgotten (bathroom scrolling)
  - **Regrettable:** Watched and feel gross after (doom-scrolling)
  - **Memorable:** Watched and changes perspective (podcasts, documentaries)

### 4. 80/20 Rule of Production
- 80% of time on pre-production/packaging
- 20% on production/post-production

### 5. The 30-Second Intro Formula
- First 7 seconds: Confirm the click (deliver on promise)
- Next 20 seconds: Make it personal (show your unique perspective)
- Final 10 seconds: Open a new loop (create a question to keep them watching)

### 6. Screenshot + Build Taste
- Screenshot great titles and thumbnails
- Build a "Pinterest board" of what you like
- Taste comes from pulling everything together

### 7. The List Structure
- Every great video is a title/thumbnail on a list
- Slowly reveal items on the list
- Example: "The 5 Types of Wealth" — you don't know #5 until the end

### 8. Relative Zero
- 80,000 views can feel like zero if you're used to more
- Don't let relative numbers dictate your relationship with your work
- Value the work, not just the views

## Decision Frameworks

### YouTube Packaging Process

```
IDEA FORMAT
    │
    ▼
IDENTITIES (Who is this for?)
└── Aspiring creators? Professional creators? Industry?
    │
    ▼
EMOTIONS (What do I want them to feel?)
└── Educated? Empowered? Less alone?
    │
    ▼
ACTIONS (What do I want them to do?)
└── Press publish? Build a business? Subscribe?
    │
    ▼
TITLE IDEATION
├── Write 10 ideas/day (brainstorming is a practice)
├── Use frameworks (versus, lists, "I tried", "X years of Y in Z minutes")
└── Test 3 conceptual variations × 3-4 text overlays = ~12 thumbnails
    │
    ▼
THUMBNAIL DESIGN
├── Must win in isolation (not just with title)
├── Complementary to title (not redundant)
└── Creates curiosity, doesn't close it
    │
    ▼
INTRO (First 30 Seconds)
├── 0-7s: Confirm the click
├── 7-27s: Make it personal (unique perspective)
└── 27-30s: Open a new loop (unanswered question)
    │
    ▼
CONTENT (The Story)
├── Great story = series of questions + answers
├── Always have an open question (if none, story is over)
└── Omission is powerful: "audience is dying to do math"
    │
    ▼
PUBLISH
```

### Title + Thumbnail Testing Framework

```
Upload video with 3 different thumbnails + 1 title
    │
    ▼
After initial data, pick winning thumbnail
    │
    ▼
Test 3-4 different text overlays on winning thumbnail
    │
    ▼
Optimize for:
├── CTR (click-through rate) — 5-10% is target
└── Audience quality (not just quantity)
```

### Three Categories of Views Framework

```
FORGETTABLE VIEWS
├── Watched and completely forgot
├── Example: Bathroom scrolling, line waiting
└── Low value, low brand impact

REGRETTABLE VIEWS
├── Watched and feel gross after
├── Example: Doom-scrolling, brain-rot content
└── Negative brand impact

MEMORABLE VIEWS
├── Watched and it stuck with you
├── Example: Podcasts that changed perspective, documentaries
└── High value, brand-building

Goal: Optimize for memorable minutes, not total views.
```

### Niche Research Process

```
1. Type your niche into YouTube
2. Search variations: "[Niche] 101", "How to [do niche thing]"
3. Find videos with 100k+, 500k+, 1M+ views
4. Create spreadsheet: Title, Thumbnail, Views, Channel, Notes
5. Look for patterns: What's familiar? What's different?
6. Goal: Familiar enough to be recognized, different enough to be noticed
```

## Rules

- **Always spend 75-80% of time on packaging** — If they don't click, they don't watch
- **Always have a curiosity gap** — Title + thumbnail should open, not close, the gap
- **Always confirm the click in first 7 seconds** — Deliver on your promise immediately
- **Always open a new loop in the intro** — Give them a reason to stay
- **Always test multiple thumbnails** — 12 variations is normal
- **Always optimize for the right audience** — Not all views are equal
- **Never make title + thumbnail redundant** — They should complement, not repeat
- **Never let relative zero define your work** — 80,000 views is still 80,000 people
- **Never confuse brand and viewership** — Memorable views > empty views

## Heuristics

- If CTR is under 5% on a new video, re-evaluate your packaging
- If 70% retention after 30 seconds = good (for a new video)
- If you don't watch YouTube, don't make YouTube videos
- The audience is a collaborator, not just a consumer
- If there's no open question, the story is over

## Triggers

### When Brainstorming Ideas:

```
Feeling stuck on ideas

→ Write 10 titles every single day
→ Most will be ___, but after 5 days you have 50 ideas
→ Use frameworks: versus, lists, "I tried", "X years of Y"

If you're serious about YouTube, you have to watch YouTube.
Screenshot great titles and thumbnails.
Build taste by pulling everything together.
```

### When Packaging a Video:

```
Ready to package your video

→ Ask: "Is there a curiosity gap?"
→ Ask: "Does the thumbnail win in isolation?"
→ Ask: "Do they tell the same story or complement each other?"

If title and thumbnail are redundant = wasted real estate
Open the gap, don't close it.
```

### When You Feel Views Are "Low":

```
Video gets 80,000 views, feels like failure

→ Remember: relative zero is a trap
→ 80,000 is still a lot of people
→ Ask: Are these memorable views or empty views?

If they're memorable minutes, the value is there.
If you're building brand, the views compound over time.
```
