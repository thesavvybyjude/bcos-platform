# Poker_Chip_Strategy.md

## Purpose
To provide a framework for managing risk and bets.

## Core Philosophy
**Your poker chip size is 10% of your bankroll.**

- Divide your total revenue base by 10
- That's your poker chip
- High-conviction bets = 1-2 chips
- Too small = No impact; too big = Bankruptcy risk

## Principles

### 1. Poker Chip = Revenue/10
```
If you're at $10M revenue:
  Poker chip = $1M

If you're at $1M revenue:
  Poker chip = $100K

If you're at $100M revenue:
  Poker chip = $10M
```

### 2. Bet Sizing Guidelines
- 1 poker chip = Comfortable bet
- 2 poker chips = High conviction
- 3+ poker chips = Risky
- 0.1 poker chips = Wasted time

### 3. Rebalance Quarterly
- Business grows → Poker chip grows
- Re-evaluate every few months
- Bet sizes should scale
- Small bets at big scale = Waste

## Process

### Poker Chip Framework:
```
1. Calculate your revenue base
2. Divide by 10 = Your poker chip
3. For any new bet:
   - Is it 1 chip or more?
   - If yes: worth pursuing
   - If no: likely not worth the overhead
4. For high conviction: 2 chips
5. Re-evaluate quarterly
```

## Decision Framework

```
What's the Right Bet Size?
  │
  ├─ What's my revenue base?
  │   ├─ $10M → $1M chips
  │   ├─ $100M → $10M chips
  │   └─ Calculate yours
  │
  ├─ Is this 1 chip or more?
  │   ├─ YES → Worth pursuing
  │   └─ NO → Probably waste of time
  │
  ├─ Do I have high conviction?
  │   ├─ YES → Consider 2 chips
  │   └─ NO → 1 chip max
  │
  └─ If it goes to 0, can I survive?
      ├─ YES → Proceed
      └─ NO → Too risky
```

## Rules

**Always:**
- Bet 1 chip minimum
- Rebalance quarterly
- Scale bets with business
- Think in poker chips

**Never:**
- Waste time on sub-chip bets
- Bet too large (3+ chips)
- Ignore the math
- Let bet sizing stay static
