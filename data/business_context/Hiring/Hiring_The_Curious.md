# Hiring The Curious

## Purpose
To provide a framework for hiring for curiosity.

## Core Philosophy
**Curiosity is the most important trait in a hire.**

- Skills can be taught; curiosity cannot
- Curious people learn faster
- Curious people solve problems better
- Curious people are never satisfied with average

## Principles

### 1. Curiosity Is Non-Negotiable
- Skills fade, curiosity compounds
- The most valuable employees are the ones who ask questions
- Hire for curiosity, train for skills
- A curious person will figure it out

### 2. The Curious Hire
- They ask good questions
- They are not satisfied with surface-level answers
- They seek to understand the why
- They are always learning

### 3. How to Assess Curiosity
- Do they ask questions in the interview?
- Do they show genuine interest in the business?
- Do they want to understand the why behind things?
- Did they research the company before they came?

### 4. The Cost of Hiring Without Curiosity
- They will do only what they are told
- They will not improve processes
- They will not find better ways
- They will hold the company back

## Decision Framework

```
Is This Candidate Curious?
  |
  +-- Do they ask good questions?
  |   +-- YES -> Good
  |   +-- NO -> Potential red flag
  |
  +-- Did they research the company?
  |   +-- YES -> Good
  |   +-- NO -> Lack of curiosity
  |
  +-- Do they seek to understand the why?
  |   +-- YES -> Good
  |   +-- NO -> Surface-level thinker
  |
  +-- Are they always learning?
      +-- YES -> Good
      +-- NO -> Stagnant
```

## Rules

### Always:
- Hire for curiosity
- Look for good questions
- Value learning over credentials
- Curiosity compounds

### Never:
- Hire someone who does not ask questions
- Value skills over curiosity
- Think curiosity is not important
- Hire average curiosity
